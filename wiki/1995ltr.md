# 1995 Annual Letter Summary

*Report on Berkshire Hathaway's performance and philosophy as of February 23, 1996 (covering 1995 results)*

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Intrinsic Value Growth Constraint Reinforced
- Structural definition: "There's no reason to do handsprings over 1995's gains. This was a year in which any fool could make a bundle in the stock market. And we did. To paraphrase President Kennedy, a rising tide lifts all yachts"
- Quantitative insight: "Our gain in net worth during 1995 was $5.3 billion, or 45.0%. Per-share book value grew by a little less, 43.1%"
- **Source**: [[1995ltr.md#p0]]

### Look-Through Earnings Framework Operationalized
- Structural insight: "The following table illustrates our progress on both fronts. In the tabulation, we show the marketable securities owned per share of Berkshire at ten-year intervals. A second column lists our per-share operating earnings (before taxes and purchase-price adjustments but after interest and corporate overhead) from all other activities"
- Behavioral insight: "We proceed with two advantages: First, our operating managers are outstanding and, in most cases, have an unusually strong attachment to Berkshire. Second, Charlie and I have had considerable experience in allocating capital and try to go at that job rationally and objectively"
- **Source**: [[1995ltr.md#p0]]

### Super-Cat Insurance Competitive Advantage Codified
- Structural analysis: "Ajit Jain is the guiding genius of our super-cat business and writes important non-cat business as well. In insurance, the term 'catastrophe' is applied to an event, such as a hurricane or earthquake, that causes a great many insured losses"
- Economic insight: "Berkshire is sought out for many kinds of insurance, both super-cat and large single-risk, because: (1) our financial strength is unmatched... (2) we can supply a quote faster than anyone in the business; and (3) we will issue policies with limits larger than anyone else is prepared to write"
- **Source**: [[1995ltr.md#p1]]

### Acquisition Behavior Taxonomy Refined
- Structural insight: "At Berkshire, we have all the difficulties in perceiving the future that other acquisition-minded companies do. Like they also, we face the inherent problem that the seller of a business practically always knows far more about it than the buyer and also picks the time of sale - a time when the business is likely to be walking 'just fine.'"
- Behavioral contrast: "In making acquisitions, we have a further advantage: As payment, we can offer sellers a stock backed by an extraordinary collection of outstanding businesses. An individual or a family wishing to dispose of a single fine business, but also wishing to defer personal taxes indefinitely, is apt to find Berkshire stock a particularly comfortable holding"
- **Source**: [[1995ltr.md#p0]]

## 📊 Investment Decisions

### GEICO Acquisition Strategy
- Formalized principle: "Right after yearend, we completed the purchase of 100% of GEICO, the seventh largest auto insurer in the United States, with about 3.7 million cars insured"
- Structural insight: "I've had a 45-year association with GEICO... Our acquisition of 100% of GEICO caused Davy to incur a large tax. Characteristically, he still warmly supported the transaction"
- **Source**: [[1995ltr.md#p0]]

### Capital Allocation Framework
- Formalized principle: "Over the years, we've been Woody-like in our thinking, attempting to increase our marketable investments in wonderful businesses, while simultaneously trying to buy similar businesses in their entirety"
- Behavioral insight: "We proceed with two advantages: First, our operating managers are outstanding and, in most cases, have an unusually strong attachment to Berkshire. Second, Charlie and I have had considerable experience in allocating capital and try to go at that job rationally and objectively"
- **Source**: [[1995ltr.md#p0]]
