## 📜 Source Summary

### Key Highlights

- **$42.5B GAAP net income**, composed of $21.9B operating earnings + $4.9B realized gains + $26.7B unrealized gain − $11B PCC write-down → highlights accounting distortion and capital allocation realism
- **Two Strings framework**: Explicitly rejects conglomerate stigma by embracing both controlled businesses *and* non-controlled equity stakes as equally valid capital deployment strategies
- **Family Jewels**: Identifies four core value drivers — (1) Property/Casualty insurance ($138B float), (2) BNSF railroad, (3) Apple (5.4% stake), (4) BHE utility — with repurchases amplifying ownership in all four
- **Tale of Two Cities**: Celebrates American entrepreneurship via See’s Candy (LA), GEICO (DC), National Indemnity (Omaha), NFM (Omaha), Clayton Homes & Pilot (Knoxville)
- **Partnership philosophy**: Traces lineage from Buffett Partnership Ltd. (1956) → Berkshire acquisition (1965) → dissolution (1969) → Blue Chip Stamps merger (1983) → “Although our form is corporate, our attitude is partnership”

### Core Insights

#### GAAP Earnings Distortion & The $11B PCC Write-Down
> "The final component in our GAAP figure – that ugly $11 billion write-down – is almost entirely the quantification of a mistake I made in 2016. That year, Berkshire purchased Precision Castparts (“PCC”), and I paid too much for the company." [[p.3]]

- **Capital allocation realism**: Acknowledges overpayment for PCC due to excessive optimism about normalized aerospace profits
- **Accounting transparency**: Distinguishes between operating performance ($21.9B) and accounting artifacts ($26.7B unrealized gain, $11B write-down)
- **Ownership accountability**: “No one misled me... I was simply too optimistic” → personal ownership of error without deflection

#### Two Strings to Our Bow: Controlled vs. Non-Controlled Ownership
> "Charlie and I want our conglomerate to own all or part of a diverse group of businesses with good economic characteristics and good managers. Whether Berkshire controls these businesses, however, is unimportant to us." [[p.4]]

- **Strategic flexibility**: Rejects rigid acquisition dogma; embraces full control *or* minority stakes based on opportunity, not ideology
- **Economic logic over control fetish**: “Owning a non-controlling portion of a wonderful business is more profitable, more enjoyable and far less work than struggling with 100% of a marginal enterprise" [[p.4]]
- **Anti-conglomerate stance**: Contrasts Berkshire with historical conglomerates that overpaid for mediocre businesses using inflated stock currency

#### Family Jewels Framework: BNSF, Apple, BHE, Insurance
> "Most of Berkshire’s value, however, resides in four businesses, three controlled and one in which we have only a 5.4% interest. All four are jewels." [[p.5]]

| Jewel | Type | Key Metric |
|-------|------|------------|
| Insurance | Controlled | $138B float, equity-heavy investment strategy enabled by financial strength |
| BNSF | Controlled | Largest U.S. railroad by freight volume |
| Apple | Non-controlled | 5.4% stake, cost $36B, now owns 10% more of Apple than in 2018 due to repurchases |
| BHE | Controlled | 91% owned, earnings grew from $122M to $3.4B in 21 years |

- **Repurchase synergy**: Berkshire’s buybacks + Apple’s buybacks compound ownership growth — “you now indirectly own a full 10% more of Apple’s assets and future earnings than you did in July 2018" [[p.6]]
- **Float as strategic weapon**: $138B float enables equity-heavy investing while peers are bond-constrained due to regulation/credit ratings

#### Tale of Two Cities: See's Candy, GEICO, Omaha & Knoxville
> "Success stories abound throughout America... individuals with an idea, ambition and often just a pittance of capital have succeeded beyond their dreams." [[p.7]]

- **Geographic diversity**: LA (See’s), DC (GEICO), Omaha (National Indemnity, NFM), Knoxville (Clayton, Pilot)
- **Founder narratives**: Rose Blumkin (NFM, started with $2,500), Leo Goodwin (GEICO, $100K), Jack Ringwalt (National Indemnity, $125K), Jim Clayton (Clayton Homes, shoestring), Jim Haslam (Pilot, $6,000)
- **Intergenerational continuity**: Louie Blumkin (NFM), Kevin Clayton & Jim Haslam Jr. (Pilot), third/fourth gen Blumkins (NFM)

#### Berkshire Partnership Philosophy & Owner Longevity
> "Although our form is corporate, our attitude is partnership." [[p.10]]

- **Historical lineage**: Traces from 1956 partnerships → 1965 Berkshire acquisition → 1969 dissolution → 1983 Blue Chip merger → formalized partnership doctrine
- **Owner demographics**: “Million-plus individual investors who simply trust us to represent their interests” + “Stan Truhlsen turned 100” + “Charlie and I are 97 and 90” → longevity as cultural signature
- **Anti-Wall Street stance**: “We already have the investors we want and don’t think that they, on balance, would be upgraded by replacements" [[p.11]]

### 📌 Notable Quotes

> "Too much of a good thing can be . . . wonderful." [[p.6]]

> "Never bet against America." [[p.9]]

# 2020 Annual Letter Summary

*Report on Berkshire Hathaway's performance and philosophy as of February 27, 2021 (covering 2020 results)*

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Two Strings to Our Bow
- Explicit framing: "Berkshire is a conglomerate – but only in part"
- Dual strategy: *controlled businesses* (100% ownership) + *non-controlled businesses* (partial ownership)
- Emphasis on owning *wonderful businesses* regardless of control status
- **Source**: [[2020ltr.md#p4]]

### Family Jewels Framework
- Formalized “Big Four”: Insurance, Apple, BNSF, BHE
- Each jewel contributes distinct strategic advantages (float, consumer loyalty, rail infrastructure, energy transition)
- **Source**: [[2020ltr.md#p5]]

### Repurchase Power Dynamics
- Berkshire repurchases increase *indirect ownership* in investees like Apple via share count reduction
- Apple’s own repurchases increased Berkshire’s stake from 5.2% → 5.4% without cost
- **Source**: [[2020ltr.md#p6]]

### Tale of Two Cities
- Omaha (National Indemnity, NFM) and Knoxville (Clayton Homes, Pilot) as exemplars of American entrepreneurship
- Core insight: “Never bet against America” rooted in local talent, ambition, and capital-light startups
- **Source**: [[2020ltr.md#p7-9]]

## 💬 Notable Quotes

> "Owning a non-controlling portion of a wonderful business is more profitable, more enjoyable and far less work than struggling with 100% of a marginal enterprise." — [[2020ltr.md#p4]]

> "The math of repurchases grinds away slowly, but can be powerful over time." — [[2020ltr.md#p6]]

> "There has been, however, a change in the company’s size. In 1937, its first full year of operation, GEICO did $238,288 of business. Last year the figure was $35 billion." — [[2020ltr.md#p7]]


## 📊 Investment Decisions

### Precision Castparts Write-Down
- $11B write-down due to overpayment in 2016 acquisition
- Acknowledged as “big one” mistake; CEO Mark Donegan retained and praised
- **Source**: [[2020ltr.md#p3]]

### Occidental Petroleum Investment
- $10B investment in preferred stock and warrants
- Strategic rationale: U.S. oil & gas leadership, carbon-capture potential
- **Source**: [[2020ltr.md#p7]]
