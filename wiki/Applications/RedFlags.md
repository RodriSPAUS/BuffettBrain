## Purpose

A concise, evidence-based list of structural and behavioral red flags that signal a business is *not* suitable for long-term Berkshire-style ownership. These are non-negotiable disqualifiers—no exceptions.

> "The fidelity disappointments can hurt beyond their financial impact, a pain that can approach that of a failed marriage." [[Sources/2024ltr]]

## Core Red Flags

| # | Red Flag | Why It Disqualifies | Source Anchor |
|---|----------|----------------------|---------------|
| 1 | **Compensation Tied to Stock Price** | Encourages short-term manipulation over long-term value creation; violates fidelity principle | [[Sources/2024ltr]] |
| 2 | **Frequent Acquisitions Without Integration Success** | Signals empire-building, not capital discipline; destroys intrinsic value through overpayment | [[Sources/2023ltr]] |
| 3 | **Float or Capital Source Eroding** | Float shrinking or cost rising indicates underwriting failure or loss of competitive advantage | [[Sources/2024ltr]] |
| 4 | **Tax Strategy Focused on Avoidance vs. Deferral** | Aggressive tax avoidance erodes trust and increases regulatory risk; deferral is the disciplined path | [[Sources/2024ltr]] |
| 5 | **Management Changes Without Clear Succession Plan** | Breaks continuity of capital allocation and moat defense; fidelity requires stability | [[Sources/2024ltr]] |

## Usage Instructions

- 🚩 Immediate Rejection: Any single red flag triggers automatic rejection.
- 🔍 Verification Required: Each red flag must be verified with dated source evidence—not anecdote or rumor.
- 📉 Escalation Path: If red flag appears, escalate to full Moat + Management + Capital Allocation review before final decision.

> "We paid only one cash dividend. On January 3, 1967, we disbursed our sole payment – $101,755 or 10¢ per A share." [[Sources/2024ltr]] → extreme discipline is the antidote to red-flag behavior
