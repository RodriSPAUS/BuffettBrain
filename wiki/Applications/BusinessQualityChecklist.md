## Purpose

A rapid, evidence-based checklist to assess whether a business meets Berkshire’s threshold for high-quality ownership. Each item must be verified with *direct source evidence* from letters or financial statements—not speculation.

> "The key to investing is not assessing how much an industry is going to grow, or how dynamic it will be, but rather selecting the business with the deepest moat." [[Sources/2024ltr#p4]]

## Core Criteria

| # | Criterion | Evidence Required | Source Anchor |
|---|-----------|-------------------|---------------|
| 1 | **Durable Moat** | Documented pricing power, cost advantage, network effects, or intangible assets (brand, regulatory license) sustained for ≥10 years | [[Sources/2024ltr#p4]] |
| 2 | **Capital Allocation Discipline** | Consistent reinvestment in high-ROIC projects, selective acquisitions, and rational share repurchases—not dividends or empire-building | [[Sources/2024ltr#p5]] |
| 3 | **Management Fidelity** | Compensation tied to performance, no stock options, long tenure, and public alignment with owner interests | [[Sources/2024ltr#p2–3]] |
| 4 | **Float or Equivalent Capital Advantage** | Low-cost, scalable capital source (e.g., insurance float, subscription revenue, deferred taxes) that compounds over time | [[Sources/2024ltr#p9]] |
| 5 | **Tax Efficiency** | Significant retained earnings, low effective tax rate, and deferral strategies that amplify compound growth | [[Sources/2024ltr#p5]] |

## Usage Instructions

- ✅ Pass: All five criteria met with clear, dated evidence.
- ⚠️ Conditional: Four criteria met; one requires deeper due diligence (e.g., moat erosion risk).
- ❌ Fail: Three or fewer criteria met—reject immediately.

> "When you find a truly wonderful business, stick with it. Patience pays, and one wonderful business can offset the many mediocre decisions that are inevitable." [[Sources/2023ltr#p8]]
