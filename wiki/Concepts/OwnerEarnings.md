# Owner Earnings: The True Cash Generation of a Business

## Definition

Reported earnings are an accounting output; owner earnings are what an owner can actually take out of a business without impairing it. The gap between the two is the reason Buffett distrusts a single year's return-on-equity figure.

Two distinct corrections make up the idea in the early letters.

## Correction 1 — Earnings the Owner Never Sees Reported

A shareholder owning under 20% of a company records only the dividends received, not the share of earnings retained on their behalf. Buffett argues the retained portion is no less real: 

> "The value to Berkshire Hathaway of retained earnings is not determined by whether we own 100%, 50%, 20% or 1% of the businesses in which they reside. Rather, the value of those retained earnings is determined by the use to which they are put and the subsequent level of earnings produced by that usage." [[Sources/1980ltr]]

By 1981 this unrecorded amount had passed reported operating earnings — GEICO, General Foods, R. J. Reynolds and The Washington Post alone were expected to contribute over $35 million of it in 1982. Buffett's conclusion about the metric is explicit: the accounting rules that ignore these earnings "diminish the utility of our annual return on equity calculation, or any other single year measure of economic performance." [[Sources/1981ltr]]

The translation is real but not punctual: "While market values track business values quite well over long periods, in any given year the relationship can gyrate capriciously." [[Sources/1981ltr]]

## Correction 2 — Capital Expenditure the Income Statement Omits

The complementary error runs the other way: reported earnings can overstate what an owner may withdraw, because some capital spending is mandatory just to stand still. The 1981 letter states the mechanism before the term existed — 

> "inflation acts as a gigantic corporate tapeworm. That tapeworm preemptively consumes its requisite daily diet of investment dollars regardless of the health of the host organism. Whatever the level of reported profits (even if nil), more dollars for receivables, inventory and fixed assets are continuously required by the business in order to merely match the unit volume of the previous year." [[Sources/1981ltr]]

Under those conditions a business earning 8% or 10% on equity "often has no leftovers for expansion, debt reduction or "real" dividends" [[Sources/1981ltr]].

The Scott Fetzer purchase in [[Sources/1985ltr]] and its accounting treatment in [[Sources/1986ltr]] are where this becomes a worked calculation.

## Long-Term Focus on Sustainable Earnings

Owner earnings emphasize the importance of sustainable cash generation over accounting metrics:

> "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." [[Sources/1989ltr]]
