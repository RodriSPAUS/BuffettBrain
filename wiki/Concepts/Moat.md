## Definition

A *moat* is a durable competitive advantage that allows a business to earn above-average returns on capital over long periods. It protects the company from competitors and enables pricing power, cost advantages, network effects, or intangible assets like brands.

> "The key to investing is not assessing how much an industry is going to grow, or how dynamic it will be, but rather selecting the business with the deepest moat." [[Sources/2024ltr]]

## Examples from Letters

- **GEICO**: "GEICO was a long-held gem that needed major repolishing... Todd has worked tirelessly in getting the job done." [[Sources/2024ltr]] → brand loyalty and low-cost structure as enduring moat
- **Coca-Cola**: "When you find a truly wonderful business, stick with it. Patience pays, and one wonderful business can offset the many mediocre decisions that are inevitable." [[Sources/2023ltr]] → global brand and distribution as unassailable moat
- **Berkshire’s Insurance Float**: "The float is likely to grow a bit over time and, with intelligent underwriting (and some luck), has a reasonable prospect of being costless." [[Sources/2024ltr]] → float as structural moat amplifier

## Contrasts & Nuances

- **Climate-resilient moats**: "P/C insurance pricing strengthened during 2024, reflecting a major increase in damage from convective storms. Climate change may have been announcing its arrival." [[Sources/2024ltr]] → moats must now withstand physical shocks, not just competition
- **Moat vs. Scale**: "Doubling our huge base is simply not possible within, say, a five-year period... We have no possibility of eye-popping performance." [[Sources/2023ltr]] → scale alone does not create moat; durability matters more than size
- **Moat Erosion Warning**: "Wall Street... would like its customers to make money, but what truly causes its denizens’ juices to flow is feverish activity." [[Sources/2023ltr]] → market incentives can undermine moat discipline
