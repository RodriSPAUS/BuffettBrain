## 📜 Source Summary

### Key Highlights

- **Operating earnings discipline**: $30.8B record operating earnings (GAAP exclusive of equity gains/losses) → “Charlie and I focus on this operational figure and urge you to do so as well" [[p.5]]
- **GAAP distortion**: Quarterly GAAP earnings wildly fluctuate (e.g., Q2: $9.3B operating vs. $(43.8B) GAAP) → “The GAAP earnings are 100% misleading when viewed quarterly or even annually" [[p.5]]
- **Business-picking philosophy reinforced**: “Charlie and I are not stock-pickers; we are business-pickers" [[p.3]], with emphasis on “long-lasting favorable economic characteristics and trustworthy managers" [[p.3]]
- **Insurance float growth**: Increased from $147B to $164B in 2022, aided by Alleghany acquisition → “Since purchasing our first property-casualty insurer in 1967, Berkshire’s float has increased 8,000-fold" [[p.5]]
- **Alleghany acquisition**: Property-casualty insurer led by Joe Brandon → “Alleghany delivers special value to us because Berkshire’s unmatched financial strength allows its insurance subsidiaries to follow valuable and enduring investment strategies" [[p.5]]
- **Repurchase power dynamics**: Berkshire repurchased 1.2% of shares; Apple and Amex repurchases also increased Berkshire’s ownership → “When the share count goes down, your interest in our many businesses goes up" [[p.6]]

### Core Insights

#### Operating Earnings Discipline & GAAP Distortion
> "The GAAP earnings are 100% misleading when viewed quarterly or even annually." [[p.5]]

- **Operational focus**: “Operating earnings” defined as GAAP income excluding capital gains/losses from equity holdings → pure measure of underlying business performance
- **Quarterly volatility**: Q2 2022 shows $9.3B operating earnings but $(43.8B) GAAP earnings → highlights how unrealized equity gains/losses dominate GAAP reporting
- **Long-term framing**: “Capital gains... have been hugely important to Berkshire over past decades" but “quarter-by-quarter gyrations... totally misinform investors" [[p.5]] → GAAP is noise; operating earnings are signal

#### Business-Picking vs. Stock-Picking Philosophy Reinforced
> "Charlie and I are not stock-pickers; we are business-pickers." [[p.3]]

- **Ownership mindset**: “We own publicly-traded stocks based on our expectations about their long-term business performance, not because we view them as vehicles for adroit purchases and sales." [[p.3]]
- **Managerial trust**: “Our goal... is to make meaningful investments in businesses with both long-lasting favorable economic characteristics and trustworthy managers." [[p.3]]
- **Creative destruction acceptance**: “Capitalism has two sides: The system creates an ever-growing pile of losers while concurrently delivering a gusher of improved goods and services." [[p.3]] → acknowledges failure as inherent to capitalism

#### Insurance Float Growth to $164B & Strategic Capital
> "Aided by Alleghany, our insurance float increased during 2022 from $147 billion to $164 billion." [[p.5]]

- **Float as strategic advantage**: “Though not recognized in our financial statements, this float has been an extraordinary asset for Berkshire." [[p.5]]
- **Scale & durability**: $164B float represents ~$100K per A-share → massive, sticky capital for long-term equity allocation
- **Underwriting discipline**: “With disciplined underwriting, these funds have a decent chance of being cost-free over time." [[p.5]] → float is not just size but quality

#### Alleghany Acquisition & Insurance Strategy Deepening
> "Alleghany delivers special value to us because Berkshire’s unmatched financial strength allows its insurance subsidiaries to follow valuable and enduring investment strategies." [[p.5]]

- **Strategic fit**: Alleghany’s property-casualty expertise complements Berkshire’s existing insurance operations → expands capacity and diversification
- **Leadership continuity**: Joe Brandon, Alleghany’s CEO, “understands both Berkshire and insurance" → ensures cultural alignment and execution fidelity
- **Financial strength leverage**: Berkshire’s balance sheet enables insurance subsidiaries to pursue “valuable and enduring investment strategies unavailable to virtually all competitors" [[p.5]] → competitive moat extension

#### Repurchase Power Dynamics & Value Accretion
> "At Berkshire, we directly increased your interest in our unique collection of businesses by repurchasing 1.2% of the company’s outstanding shares." [[p.6]]

- **Direct accretion**: Repurchases increase each shareholder’s proportional ownership in all Berkshire businesses (controlled and non-controlled)
- **Third-party accretion**: Apple and Amex repurchases increased Berkshire’s stake without any cost → “repurchases increased Berkshire’s ownership a bit without any cost to us" [[p.6]]
- **Value-accretive discipline**: “Every small bit helps if repurchases are made at value-accretive prices." [[p.6]] → price sensitivity remains paramount

### 📌 Notable Quotes

> "The weeds wither away in significance as the flowers bloom." [[p.4]]

> "Even the operating earnings figure that we favor can easily be manipulated by managers who wish to do so." [[p.6]]

> "There is no such thing as a 100% sure thing when investing. Thus, the use of leverage is dangerous." [[p.9]]

# 2022 Annual Letter Summary

*Report on Berkshire Hathaway's performance and philosophy as of February 25, 2023 (covering 2022 results)*

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Business-Picking vs. Stock-Picking
- Explicit framing: "Charlie and I are not stock-pickers; we are business-pickers"
- Emphasis on *long-term business performance*, not short-term trading
- **Source**: [[2022ltr.md#p3]]

### The Secret Sauce: Compounding Winners
- Coca-Cola and American Express case studies: $1.3B investments → $25B/$22B valuations
- Core insight: "The weeds wither away in significance as the flowers bloom"
- **Source**: [[2022ltr.md#p4-5]]

### Operating Earnings Discipline
- Rejection of GAAP earnings due to capricious capital gains/losses
- Preference for *operating earnings* ($30.8B in 2022) as stable metric
- **Source**: [[2022ltr.md#p5]]

### Float as Strategic Capital
- Insurance float increased from $147B to $164B in 2022
- Float treated as *permanent, low-cost capital* for investment, not idle cash
- **Source**: [[2022ltr.md#p5]]

## 💬 Notable Quotes

> "In August 1994 – yes, 1994 – Berkshire completed its seven-year purchase of the 400 million shares of Coca-Cola we now own." — [[2022ltr.md#p4]]

> "The lesson for investors: The weeds wither away in significance as the flowers bloom." — [[2022ltr.md#p4]]

> "Warren and I don’t focus on the froth of the market. We seek out good long-term investments and stubbornly hold them for a long time." — [[2022ltr.md#p9]]


## 📊 Investment Decisions

### Alleghany Corporation Acquisition
- Property-casualty insurer acquisition led by Joe Brandon
- Strategic rationale: leverages Berkshire’s financial strength for superior investment strategies
- **Source**: [[2022ltr.md#p5]]

### Share Repurchases
- Berkshire repurchased 1.2% of outstanding shares at value-accretive prices
- Apple and Amex repurchases increased Berkshire’s ownership without cost
- **Source**: [[2022ltr.md#p6]]
