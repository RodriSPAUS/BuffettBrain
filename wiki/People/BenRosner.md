# Ben Rosner

Ran Associated Cotton Shops (later renamed Associated Retail Stores) under
Diversified Retailing Company, the sibling holding to [[Cases/BerkshireHathaway]]
— sold the business in 1967 and, like [[People/JackRingwalt]] at National
Indemnity, kept running it.

## The sale, and the man who stayed

> "The principal selling executives, Ben Rosner and Jack Ringwalt, have continued to do a superb job (the only kind they know), and in every respect have far more than lived up to their end of the bargain." [[Sources/1968ltr]]

> "Those with primary responsibility for their operations, Ken Chace at Berkshire Hathaway, Louis Kohn at Hochschild Kohn, Jack Ringwalt at National Indemnity and Ben Rosner at Associated Cotton Shops, continue to meld effort and ability into results." [[Sources/1968ltr]]

## A byword for cost discipline

Rosner's frugality becomes Buffett's own private standard for judging the
partnership's own office expenses, cited by name years after the deal:

> "Since 1963 (Assets $9,405,400) rent has gone from $3,947 to $5,823 (Ben Rosner would never have forgiven me if I had signed a percentage lease) travel from $3,206 to $3,603, and dues and subscriptions from $900 to $994." [[Sources/1969ltr]]

## What the business did under him

Associated Cotton Shops was, with National Indemnity, one of the two
businesses earning about 20% on capital employed in 1968 — a return few
Fortune 500 manufacturers matched that year. [[Concepts/CapitalAllocation]]
