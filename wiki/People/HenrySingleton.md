# Henry Singleton

Chairman of Teledyne, cited by Buffett as one of the rare acquirers whose record
was genuinely earned rather than borrowed from an inflationary tailwind.

## The 1981 citation

Buffett names him in the second of two categories of successful acquirer — "the
managerial superstars - men who can recognize that rare prince who is disguised
as a toad, and who have managerial abilities that enable them to peel away the
disguise" [[Sources/1981ltr]] — alongside Ben Heineman at Northwest Industries,
Erwin Zaban at National Service Industries and [[People/TomMurphy]] at Capital
Cities.

The qualification Buffett attaches matters as much as the compliment: this group
had largely stopped acquiring, "and often have found repurchase of their own
shares to be the most sensible employment of corporate capital"
[[Sources/1981ltr]]. Singleton's Teledyne buybacks are the standard illustration
of that behaviour, though the letters in this collection do not describe them in
detail.
