# Tim Cook

CEO of Apple, and, in the conference-call transcripts, the manager Buffett
singles out for a specific kind of praise: not for what he built, but for what
he did with something someone else built. See [[Cases/AmericanExpress]] and
[[Concepts/CapitalAllocation]] for the buyback mechanics Buffett credits Cook
for using well.

## "Nobody Could Have Developed It Like He Has"

At the 2021 annual meeting, discussing why Berkshire continued to hold Apple
despite trimming the position, Buffett distinguishes Cook's talent from Steve
Jobs's differently:

> "He's one of the best managers in the world, and I've seen a lot of
> managers." [[Sources/Conference_call_2021]]

By the 2025 meeting, with Cook in the audience, Buffett makes the comparison
explicit and personally thanks him from the stage:

> "And nobody but Steve could have created Apple, but nobody could -- but Tim
> could have developed it like it has." [[Sources/Conference_call_2025]]

> "I'm somewhat embarrassed to say that Tim Cook has made Berkshire a lot more
> money than I've ever made Berkshire Hathaway." [[Sources/Conference_call_2025]]

## The Buyback Tax, Illustrated Through Apple

Discussing the 1% federal excise tax on share repurchases, Buffett uses
Apple's roughly $100 billion annual buyback program to make the tax concrete —
about $1 billion a year now paid on Cook's repurchases:

> "And Tim Cook has done a wonderful job. I mean, really wonderful job running
> Apple." [[Sources/Conference_call_2025]] [[Concepts/CapitalAllocation]]
