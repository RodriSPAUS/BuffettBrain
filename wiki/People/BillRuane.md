# Bill Ruane

The man Buffett names, in the letters winding down Buffett Partnership, Ltd.,
as the money manager he trusts with his own family's capital and recommends to
every partner who does not want to manage their own money after the
partnership dissolves. He appears in exactly one letter in this corpus — the
October 9, 1969 letter to partners [[Raw/1969ltr.md]] — but it is the letter
doing the most consequential single act of trust in the whole partnership
archive: Buffett is handing over, in public, the people he is personally
responsible for.

## Why him

They met in Ben Graham's class at Columbia in 1951, and Buffett's endorsement
rests on nearly two decades of watching him since:

> "If Susie and I were to die while our children are minors, he is one of three trustees who have carte blanche on investment matters - the other two are not available for continuous investment management for all partners, large or small." [[Raw/1969ltr.md]]

Buffett is explicit that recommending a person, unlike recommending a security,
is a decision with nothing to gain personally and something to lose — "if
results were excellent it would do me no good and, if something went wrong, I
might well get a portion of the blame" — and makes it anyway because of the
trust partners have placed in him. [[Principles/Fidelity]]

## The record, stated with its worst years included

Ruane had recently formed a NYSE firm, Ruane, Cunniff & Stires, Inc., managing
individual accounts on a fee basis. Buffett gives the record with its low point
left in, in the same spirit as his own "we will have our share of losing
years" candour:

> "From 1956-1961 and from 1964-1968, a composite of his individual accounts averaged over 40% per annum. However, in 1962, undoubtedly somewhat as a product of the euphoric experience of the earlier years, he was down about 50%. As he re-oriented his thinking, 1963 was about breakeven." [[Raw/1969ltr.md]]

To date in 1969 Ruane was down about 15%, which Buffett calls "fairly typical
of most money managers" that year — the same year Buffett's own liquidating
partnership expected only a roughly breakeven result.

## The caveats, given in advance

Buffett names three specific risks rather than a generic endorsement: managing
larger sums (Ruane's firm was then at $20–30 million, "and they will continue
to add accounts"); getting pulled into the operating detail of running a NYSE
firm rather than spending all his time on money management; and, most
structurally, that even excellent management was, in Buffett's view, likely to
produce only a limited edge over passive investment in the decade ahead — the
same argument that drove his own decision to close BPL rather than keep
competing in a market he no longer felt he understood. [[Raw/1969ltr.md]]

> "I think this is the main risk you run with Bill - and average performance is just not that terrible a risk." [[Raw/1969ltr.md]]
