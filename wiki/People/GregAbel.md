# Greg Abel

Buffett's designated successor as CEO of Berkshire, and before that the builder of
its energy and utility business. Like [[People/AjitJain]], Abel appears across two
decades of letters in a steadily rising role — first as a manager's "key
associate," last as the man who will write these letters himself.

## From MidAmerican to all non-insurance operations

He enters the record in 2002 as the deputy at MidAmerican (later
[[Cases/BerkshireHathawayEnergy]]):

> "Dave Sokol, MEHC's CEO, and Greg Abel, his key associate, are huge assets for Berkshire." [[Sources/2002ltr]]

Over the following years Buffett repeatedly credits the results of Berkshire's most
capital-hungry business to Abel's management — the enterprise that would grow into
one of the country's largest regulated utility groups. [[Concepts/CapitalAllocation]]
By 2018 that remit widens to nearly the whole company:

> "Greg Abel was given authority over all other operations." [[Sources/2018ltr]]

## The succession, stated plainly

The 2014 letter first ranks him with Ajit at the top of the management bench:

> "Ajit Jain and Greg Abel are proven performers who would probably be under-described as 'world-class.'" [[Sources/2014ltr]]

Early 2018 formalizes it:

> "Early in 2018, Berkshire's board elected Ajit Jain and Greg Abel as directors of Berkshire and also designated each as Vice Chairman." [[Sources/2017ltr]]

The final letter in the corpus makes the handover explicit:

> "At 94, it won't be long before Greg Abel replaces me as CEO and will be writing the annual letters." [[Sources/2024ltr]]

Buffett adds that "Greg shares the Berkshire creed that a 'report' is what a
Berkshire CEO annually owes to owners," and notes that "Greg, our directors and I
all have a very large investment in Berkshire" — the [[Principles/OwnershipMindset]]
he wants carried past his own tenure. [[Sources/2024ltr]]
