# Jack Ringwalt

Founder of National Indemnity Company, sold it to [[Cases/BerkshireHathaway]]
in 1967, and kept running it — the pattern Buffett prefers whenever the
selling owner is worth keeping.

## The sale, and the man who stayed

> "The principal selling executives, Ben Rosner and Jack Ringwalt, have continued to do a superb job (the only kind they know), and in every respect have far more than lived up to their end of the bargain." [[Sources/1968ltr]] [[Cases/NationalIndemnity]]

> "Those with primary responsibility for their operations, Ken Chace at Berkshire Hathaway, Louis Kohn at Hochschild Kohn, Jack Ringwalt at National Indemnity and Ben Rosner at Associated Cotton Shops, continue to meld effort and ability into results." [[Sources/1968ltr]]

## What the business did under him

By 1968, National Indemnity was earning about 20% on capital employed —
a figure Buffett notes only 37 of the Fortune 500 matched that year:

> "Both of these companies earned about 20% on capital employed in their businesses." [[Sources/1969ltr]] [[Concepts/CapitalAllocation]]
