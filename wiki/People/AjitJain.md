# Ajit Jain

The man Buffett credits with building Berkshire's super-catastrophe and large-risk
reinsurance operation, and the executive he praises in the most unqualified terms
he gives anyone. Ajit is named in nearly every letter from 1988 onward — the
opposite of [[People/HenrySingleton]]'s two-mention thinness — and the arc of
those mentions is a promotion in slow motion, ending with him running all of
Berkshire's insurance.

## What he actually does

Ajit underwrites the risks nobody else will price: single policies large enough to
move Berkshire's whole year, backed by the [[Concepts/Float]] the insurance
operation generates. Buffett's 1998 description is the template for every later
one:

> "This business was developed by Ajit Jain, who over the last decade has personally accounted for a significant portion of Berkshire's operating earnings. While Charlie and I sleep, Ajit keeps thinking of new ways to add value to Berkshire." [[Sources/1998ltr]]

## The praise Buffett reserves for almost no one

In 2009 he puts the valuation in a sentence he has never used about anyone else:

> "If Charlie, I and Ajit are ever in a sinking boat - and you can only save one of us - swim to Ajit." [[Sources/2009ltr]]

By 2014, ranking his eventual successors, Buffett places Ajit at the top of the
management bench:

> "Ajit Jain and Greg Abel are proven performers who would probably be under-described as 'world-class.'" [[Sources/2014ltr]] [[People/GregAbel]]

And looking back from 2015-16 on the decision to give Ajit his start, he totals the
result: "Since then, Ajit has created tens of billions of value for Berkshire
shareholders." [[Sources/2016ltr]]

## Formalized authority

The letters record the promotions directly. The 2017 letter reports the board's action:

> "Early in 2018, Berkshire's board elected Ajit Jain and Greg Abel as directors of Berkshire and also designated each as Vice Chairman." [[Sources/2017ltr]]

In early 2018 Ajit was given charge of all of Berkshire's insurance operations and
Greg Abel of everything else — changes Buffett calls overdue. [[Sources/2018ltr]]
[[Concepts/ManagementQuality]]

> "Berkshire is now far better managed than when I alone was supervising operations." [[Sources/2018ltr]]

## Pandemic Risk, Underpriced by the Whole Industry

At the 2021 annual meeting, with Berkshire's own COVID-19 reserves at about
$1.6 billion against an industry total near $25-30 billion, Ajit is candid
that the risk category itself, not just Berkshire's pricing of it, had been
mishandled across the business:

> "Having said that, I think the big lesson for us, having gone through what
> we've gone through recently, is that while we were aware of the fact that
> pandemic risk is a risk factor, it is totally, totally underpriced by all of
> us in the industry." [[Sources/Conference_call_2021]]

## GEICO's Turnaround, and AI Versus Ajit

By 2025 Ajit credits Todd Combs with closing GEICO's long-standing telematics
gap against Progressive and cutting headcount from roughly 50,000 to 20,000,
producing seven straight quarters with a combined ratio in the 80s:

> "And I never thought I'd live to see the day when anyone could have a
> combined ratio at so low as it is right now." [[Sources/Conference_call_2025]]

> "Ajit Jain The only thing I'd like to add is in addition to the underwriting
> profit, GEICO provides $39 billion of float." [[Sources/Conference_call_2025]]

Asked how AI might reshape insurance underwriting, Ajit describes Berkshire's
posture as deliberately not-first-mover, waiting for the opportunity to
"crystallize" rather than chasing the technology. Buffett's own answer to the
same question values Ajit's judgment above the technology itself:

> "So if you gave me a choice that -- gave me a choice of having $100 billion
> available to participate in an insurance -- property casualty insurance
> business for the next 10 years and a choice of getting the top AI product out
> of whoever is developing it or having Ajit making the decision, I would take
> Ajit any time." [[Sources/Conference_call_2025]]

Separately, Ajit acknowledges Berkshire has effectively exited competition
with large private-equity firms now dominating the life-insurance side of the
float business, judging their leverage and credit risk unattractive:

> "And therefore, we put up the white flag and said, we can't compete in this
> segment right now." [[Sources/Conference_call_2025]] [[Concepts/Float]]
