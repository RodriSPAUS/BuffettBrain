# Tom Murphy

CEO of Capital Cities Communications, which acquired ABC in 1985 with Berkshire
as its financing partner, and — by Buffett's own account decades later — one of
the most consistently admired managers in the letters, spanning from the 1977
letter's earliest praise to the 2007 letter's confession about a deal Buffett
turned down.

## Why Buffett singles him out

In the 1981 letter's dissection of acquisition behaviour, Murphy is the only
manager named in both of the two categories of acquirer whose records Buffett
considers earned — those who bought businesses adapted to inflation, and the
managerial superstars able to make an ordinary business better. Buffett calls him
a twofer, "whose acquisition efforts have been properly focused in Category 1 and
whose operating talents also make him a leader of Category 2" [[Sources/1981ltr]].

The same letter records what such managers actually did with capital in that
period: "these champs have made very few deals in recent years, and often have
found repurchase of their own shares to be the most sensible employment of
corporate capital" [[Sources/1981ltr]].

Four years earlier, in the very first letter of the collection, Buffett had
already declined to second-guess Capital Cities' management — a stance he
repeats across the decade. [[Sources/1977ltr]]

## Capital Cities/ABC

Berkshire agreed in 1984 to buy 3 million shares at $172.50, contingent on
Capital Cities' pending acquisition of ABC — recorded as a subsequent event
before the deal itself had even closed:

> "Our admiration for the management of Capital Cities, led by Tom Murphy and Dan
> Burke, has been expressed several times in previous annual reports. Quite
> simply, they are tops in both ability and integrity." [[Sources/1984ltr]]

Berkshire handed Murphy its votes — an arrangement Buffett says he and
[[People/CharlieMunger]] initiated rather than Murphy: "for an extended period
Tom, as CEO (or Dan, should he be CEO) votes our stock. This arrangement was
initiated by Charlie and me, not by Tom." [[Sources/1985ltr]]

Three years later, Murphy and Burke are named again among the managers of
Berkshire's three "permanent" holdings:

> "Also unchanged is our unqualified admiration of their managements: Tom Murphy
> and Dan Burke at Cap Cities, Bill Snyder and Lou Simpson at GEICO, and Kay
> Graham and Dick Simmons at The Washington Post." [[Sources/1988ltr]]

## The Disney merger, and a personal tribute

The 1995 letter tells the story of how the Cap Cities/Disney merger actually
came together — a chance meeting on Sun Valley's Wildflower Lane where Buffett
ran into Disney CEO Michael Eisner while on his way to golf with Murphy, turning
a years-stalled deal into a signed contract within days. Buffett follows the
story with an extended tribute:

> "It's appropriate that I say a few words here about Murph. To put it simply, he
> is as fine an executive as I have ever seen in my long exposure to business.
> Equally important, he possesses human qualities every bit the equal of his
> managerial qualities. He's an extraordinary friend, parent, husband and
> citizen." [[Sources/1995ltr]]

## The $3-million-hire parable

Years after Cap Cities/ABC itself, Murphy's thinking is still cited as a live
discipline inside Berkshire's insurance operations — a warning against treating
headcount additions lightly:

> Tom Murphy's $3-million-hire parable — that adding one $20,000 employee is
> really a lifetime commitment once raises, benefits and job security are
> counted — is told as the discipline that keeps National Indemnity lean between
> cycles. [[Sources/2004ltr]] [[Concepts/ManagementQuality]]

## The offer Buffett turned down

In a 2007 inventory of his own missed opportunities, Buffett names one specific
Murphy deal he declined:

> He turned down Tom Murphy's 1972 offer of the Dallas-Fort Worth NBC station for
> $35 million — a See's-like, low-capital business that by 2006 alone was earning
> $73 million pre-tax and had a capital value near $800 million. [[Sources/2007ltr]]

> "Why did I say 'no'? The only explanation is that my brain had gone on
> vacation and forgot to notify me." — [[Sources/2007ltr]] [[People/WarrenBuffett]]
