## 📜 Source Summary

### Key Highlights

- **$27.5B net worth gain**, 10.7% per-share book value increase
- **Insurance float grew to $91.6B**, 14th consecutive year of underwriting profit ($2.1B)
- **BNSF & BHE infrastructure investment**: $8.9B combined capital spend, supporting U.S. transportation & energy
- **GAAP distortion spotlighted**: $1.5B amortization charge (20% real cost), $2.1B depreciation understatement at BNSF
- **Repurchase policy reaffirmed**: Authorized to buy at ≤120% of book value, with emphasis on intrinsic value discount

### Core Insights

#### Economic Fortress Architecture Deepened
> "The nature of our insurance contracts is such that we can never be subject to immediate or near-term demands for sums that are of significance to our cash resources. This structure is by design and is a key component in the unequaled financial strength of our insurance companies." [[p.8]]

- **Float resilience**: $91.6B float, with $100B+ possible from one-time policy
- **Catastrophe resilience**: $250B industry loss would likely produce Berkshire profit due to diversified earnings streams
- **Cash preservation**: $27B paid to >6M claimants in 2016 without compromising liquidity

#### Insurance Float & Underwriting Economics
> "If our revolving float is both costless and long-enduring... the true value of this liability is dramatically less than the accounting liability." [[p.8]]

| Segment | 2016 Underwriting Profit | 2016 Float |
|---------|--------------------------|------------|
| BH Reinsurance | $822M | $45.1B |
| General Re | $190M | $17.7B |
| GEICO | $462M | $17.1B |
| Other Primary | $657M | $11.6B |
| **Total** | **$2.1B** | **$91.6B** |

#### BNSF & BHE Infrastructure Commitment
> "All told, BHE and BNSF own assets that are of major importance to our country as well as to shareholders of Berkshire." [[p.12]]

- **BNSF**: Revenue per ton-mile = $0.03 vs. competitors' $0.04–$0.05
- **BHE**: Iowa retail rate = 7.1¢/KWH vs. national average 10.3¢; wind generation = 55% of Iowa sales, rising to 89% by 2020
- **Capital discipline**: $8.9B combined investment, focused on long-lived regulated assets

#### GAAP vs. Economic Reality
> "Our goal in diverging from GAAP in this section is to present the figures to you in a manner reflecting the way in which Charlie and I view and analyze them." [[p.14]]

- **Amortization distortion**: $1.5B GAAP charge, only ~20% representing true economic cost
- **Depreciation understatement**: BNSF's $2.1B GAAP depreciation insufficient to maintain competitiveness
- **Accounting philosophy**: "What is smart at one price is stupid at another" [[p.7]]

#### Repurchase Policy Clarity
> "I am authorized to buy large amounts of Berkshire shares at 120% or less of book value because our Board has concluded that purchases at that level clearly bring an instant and material benefit to continuing shareholders." [[p.7]]

- **Value creation math**: Repurchases at < intrinsic value increase per-share intrinsic value immediately
- **Discipline**: No repurchases made to "prop" stock price; focus on meaningful purchases at value-creating prices
- **Market signaling**: Clear policy communicates intrinsic value > 120% of book value
