## Executive Summary

This page traces the conceptual and quantitative evolution of *Float* across Berkshire Hathaway’s 48 annual letters (1977–2024). It documents how float transformed from a simple insurance liability into Berkshire’s sovereign, low-cost, scalable funding source—and the primary amplifier of all other moats.

> "The float is likely to grow a bit over time and, with intelligent underwriting (and some luck), has a reasonable prospect of being costless." [[Sources/2024ltr]]

## Cross-Year Thematic Insights

- **Float Deepening**: $46M (1977) → $46B (1994) → $53B (2000) → $95B (2016) → $171B (2024)
- **Float Amplifiers**: Moat quality (GEICO), regulatory moats (BHE), brand loyalty (See's), tax efficiency (2024)
- **Float Threats**: Convective storms (2024), interest rate volatility (2022), pandemic dislocations (2021)

## Lessons Learned

- **Float Is Sovereign Capital**: Unlike debt or equity, float imposes no repayment obligation and carries no dilution risk.
- **Float Requires Discipline**: Intelligent underwriting and pricing discipline are non-negotiable—poor underwriting destroys float quality.
- **Float Is Moat-Agnostic**: It amplifies *all* moats—economic (See's), operational (Forest River), regulatory (BHE), and digital (Apple).
