## What this page traces

Berkshire's float — premiums held but not yet paid out as claims — is the
funding layer beneath nearly everything else the company owns. This page
traces its size across eight verified data points spanning 1990 to 2024. Two
different measures appear in the letters and are kept distinct here rather
than blended: **average float** (the figure Berkshire's own cost-of-float
table used through the 1990s, averaging the year's balance) and **yearend
float** (the figure used in letters from the late 1990s on). Mixing the two
without labeling them produces exactly the kind of false precision this wiki
is built to avoid — see the note on an earlier version of this page below.

| **2024** | $171,000 million | yearend | [[Sources/2024ltr]] |

The ~2004 figure is not from the 2004 letter itself but from the 2024 letter's
own twenty-years-back comparison:

> "Over the past two decades, our insurance business has generated $32 billion
> of after-tax profits from underwriting... Meanwhile, our float has grown from
> $46 billion to $171 billion." [[Sources/2024ltr]] [[Concepts/Float]]

That single sentence is also, by itself, one of the clearest statements in the
letters of what float actually is worth to Berkshire: $32 billion of realized
underwriting profit *on top of* the compounding of the float itself, over the
same twenty years.

## Reading the shape of the growth

The float roughly quadrupled from 1990 to 1996 (insurance operations still
small relative to what followed), then grew almost tenfold again by 1999-2000
as reinsurance and super-cat business scaled up under Ajit Jain. The jump from
$58.5 billion in 2008 to $91.6 billion in 2016 spans GEICO's full-ownership
years and several large reinsurance transactions; the final jump to $171
billion by 2024 is the slowest in percentage terms of any period on this
timeline, consistent with Buffett's own repeated warning (see
[[Concepts/Float]]) that float cannot compound at its early-decades rate once
the base is already this large.

## Correction note

An earlier version of this page stated 1994 float at "$46 billion" — a figure
that does not appear anywhere in `Raw/1994ltr.md` (Berkshire's actual average
float that year was $3,056.6 million, verified in [[Sources/1994ltr]]) and
several other unverified figures and claims presented as fact without a
per-figure citation. The $46 billion number belongs to the 2024 letter's own
twenty-years-back comparison, not to 1994. This page was rebuilt from
verified, per-source figures rather than extended from the prior version,
consistent with `AGENTS.md`'s citation-integrity standard that a number
carries the same burden as a quotation. See `LESSONS_LEARNED.md` for the
fuller account of how this happened.
