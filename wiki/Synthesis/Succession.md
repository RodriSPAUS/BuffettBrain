## What this page traces

Berkshire's CEO succession did not arrive as a single announcement — it is
visible, letter by letter and meeting by meeting, as Greg Abel's formal
authority widens over more than two decades, from a utility-company deputy to
the man Buffett recommends to the board as his own replacement. This page
traces that arc end to end, alongside the parallel elevation of
[[People/AjitJain]] to run all of insurance.

## From "Key Associate" to Vice Chairman

The earliest mention of Abel in this corpus is understated — a deputy's
credit line under Dave Sokol at MidAmerican Energy:

> "Dave Sokol, MEHC's CEO, and Greg Abel, his key associate, are huge assets
> for Berkshire." [[Sources/2002ltr]]

By 2014, ranking his own eventual successors, Buffett puts both Abel and
Ajit Jain at the top of the entire management bench, ahead of any outside
hire Berkshire could have made:

> "Ajit Jain and Greg Abel are proven performers who would probably be
> under-described as 'world-class.'" [[Sources/2014ltr]]

## Introduced to Shareholders, 2021

Before any letter names a successor in print, the 2021 annual meeting —
held in Los Angeles, the only Berkshire annual meeting ever held outside
Omaha, so that Charlie Munger could attend in person — is where Abel and
Jain are formally presented to the shareholder audience as Berkshire's
operating leadership, standing beside Buffett and Munger for the first time
in that format:

> "On my right, your left, I have the Vice Chairman in charge of everything
> except insurance and investments, Greg Abel." [[Sources/Conference_call_2021]]
> [[People/GregAbel]]

Board member Ron Olson later reveals, in 2025, that this same 2021 Los
Angeles meeting was also the moment the board's private decision first
became public, by accident:

> "And you all will remember the one and only Board -- I mean, Annual Meeting
> held outside Omaha, in our law office in L.A. And Charlie let it slip."
> [[Sources/Conference_call_2025]]

> "We, the Board had made the decision about Greg being the successor years
> ago." [[Sources/Conference_call_2025]]

That single detail reframes the whole arc: the 2021 introduction was not
foreshadowing a future decision still to be made — the decision had already
been made, and the board had simply not yet said so out loud.

## The Letter, Then the Announcement

The 2024 annual letter states the succession in writing for the first time:

> "At 94, it won't be long before Greg Abel replaces me as CEO and will be
> writing the annual letters." [[Sources/2024ltr]] [[People/WarrenBuffett]]

Five months later, at the very end of his 60th annual meeting, with no
question having prompted it, Buffett converts "it won't be long" into a
specific date and a formal board recommendation:

> "But I think it's -- the time has arrived where Greg, should become the
> Chief Executive Officer of the company at year-end." [[Sources/Conference_call_2025]]

## What the arc establishes

- Succession at Berkshire was never a single event; it was a decades-long,
  visible widening of authority that the public announcement only formalized.
- The board reached its decision years before announcing it — the 2021
  Los Angeles meeting is retrospectively revealed as the moment the decision
  already existed, not the moment it was made.
- Buffett's stated intention to keep every share of his own Berkshire stock
  through the transition is framed as an economic judgment, not sentiment:
  he states plainly that he expects Berkshire's prospects to be better under
  Abel than under himself. [[Sources/Conference_call_2025]]
