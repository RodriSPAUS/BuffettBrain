# 1985 Annual Letter Summary

*Report on Berkshire Hathaway's performance and philosophy as of February 28, 1986 (covering 1985 results)*

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Size as a Performance Constraint
- Formalized "iron law of business": "growth eventually dampens exceptional economics" when capital base exceeds $1 billion
- Quantitative impact: "Our equity capital is more than twenty times what it was only ten years ago" requiring "$5.7 billion" profit over next decade for 15% return
- **Source**: [[1985ltr.md#p0]]

### Market Price vs. Business Value Alignment
- Explicit preference: "my own preference... is for a market price that consistently approximates business value"
- Mechanism: "Given that relationship, all owners prosper precisely as the business prospers during their period of ownership"
- **Source**: [[1985ltr.md#p0]]

### Textile Business Shutdown as Case Study
- Root cause analysis: "commodity business, competing in a world market in which substantial excess capacity exists"
- Capital allocation paradox: "Each proposal to do so looked like an immediate winner" but collectively produced "returns remained anemic"
- **Source**: [[1985ltr.md#p2]]

### Incentive Compensation Philosophy
- Structural critique: "ten-year fixed-price options... gained in value simply because management retained earnings, not because it did well with the capital"
- Berkshire's alternative: "rewards key managers for meeting targets in their own bailiwicks" with bonuses tied to unit-specific performance
- **Source**: [[1985ltr.md#p3]]

## 💬 Notable Quotes

> "When a management with a reputation for brilliance tackles a business with a reputation for poor fundamental economics, it is the reputation of the business that remains intact." — [[1985ltr.md#p2]]

> "If the widget company consistently earned a superior return on capital throughout the period... the praise for him may be well deserved. But if return on capital was lackluster and capital employed increased in pace with earnings, applause should be withheld." — [[1985ltr.md#p3]]

> "My own preference, as perhaps you’d guess, is for a market price that consistently approximates business value." — [[1985ltr.md#p0]]


## 📊 Investment Decisions

### Capital Cities/ABC Acquisition
- First major acquisition: "purchase of a major position in Capital Cities/ABC"
- Strategic rationale: "exceptional underlying economics and outstanding managements" at valuations superior to current market
- **Source**: [[1985ltr.md#p0]]

### Scott & Fetzer Acquisition
- Second major acquisition: "acquisition of Scott & Fetzer"
- Operational synergy: "entry into a large, extended term participation in the insurance business of Fireman’s Fund"
- **Source**: [[1985ltr.md#p0]]
