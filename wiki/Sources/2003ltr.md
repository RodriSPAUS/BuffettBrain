# 2003 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 21.0% gain in per-share book value in 2003, outperforming the S&P 500's 28.7% gain. The letter focused on major investments made during the difficult market conditions of 2001-2002, particularly in energy companies like PetroChina. Buffett explained Berkshire's contrarian investment approach and how market turmoil creates opportunities for patient investors.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Major Energy Investments
Berkshire made significant investments in energy companies during the bear market, including a substantial position in PetroChina Company Limited. The company owned 2,338,961,000 shares representing 1.3% of PetroChina with a market value of $1,340 million.

### Contrarian Investment Approach
The letter emphasized the importance of acting contrary to popular opinion when making investment decisions, buying when others are selling and being fearful when others are greedy.

### Insurance Operations and Float Growth
Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float, which grew to over $44 billion by the end of 2003. The company achieved a $1.7 billion pre-tax underwriting profit, meaning they were actually paid for holding money.

### Market Timing vs. Business Evaluation
The letter differentiated between market timing and business evaluation, emphasizing that Berkshire focuses on the latter rather than trying to predict market movements.

### Long-Term Investment Horizon
Berkshire's approach remains focused on long-term business ownership rather than short-term trading, with the ideal holding period being forever.

### Capital Allocation Philosophy
The letter reinforced Berkshire's capital allocation philosophy of deploying capital where it can earn the highest returns over time.

### Corporate Governance and Shareholder Alignment
Emphasis on the importance of management teams that align with shareholders' interests and operate with integrity.

### Economic Reality vs. Market Fluctuations
Focus on the underlying economics of businesses rather than being distracted by market volatility and short-term price movements.
