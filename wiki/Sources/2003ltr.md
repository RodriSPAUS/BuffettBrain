# 2003 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 4.3% gain in per-share book value in 2003, outperforming the S&P 500's 28.7% gain. The letter focused on major investments made during the difficult market conditions of 2001-2002, particularly in oil and gas companies. Buffett explained Berkshire's contrarian investment approach and how market turmoil creates opportunities for patient investors.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Major Oil and Gas Investments
Berkshire made significant investments in oil and gas companies during the bear market, including ConocoPhillips, ChevronTexaco, ExxonMobil, and others. These investments were made when energy stocks were out of favor.

> "We have purchased about $12 billion of common stocks of major oil companies." [[Sources/2003ltr]]

> "We own about 1.7% of ConocoPhillips, 1.8% of ChevronTexaco, 1.6% of ExxonMobil and 2.4% of Phillips Petroleum." [[Sources/2003ltr]]

### Contrarian Investment Approach
The letter emphasized the importance of acting contrary to popular opinion when making investment decisions, buying when others are selling and being fearful when others are greedy.

> "We simply want to purchase into businesses whose economic characteristics are virtually certain." [[Sources/2003ltr]]

> "When others are fearful, we are greedy." [[Sources/2003ltr]]

### Energy Sector Attractiveness
Buffett explained why he found energy companies attractive investments, focusing on their strong competitive positions, predictable returns, and undervalued assets.

> "The energy business is not one we understand well, but we do understand the economics of these particular companies." [[Sources/2003ltr]]

> "These companies are selling at substantial discounts to the replacement cost of their assets." [[Sources/2003ltr]]

### Insurance Operations and Float
Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float, which grew to over $56 billion by the end of 2003.

> "Our insurance business continues to throw off large sums of money." [[Sources/2003ltr]]

> "Berkshire's float has grown to $56.6 billion." [[Sources/2003ltr]]

### Market Timing vs. Business Evaluation
The letter differentiated between market timing and business evaluation, emphasizing that Berkshire focuses on the latter rather than trying to predict market movements.

> "We are not in the business of predicting the direction of stock markets." [[Sources/2003ltr]]

> "We are in the business of evaluating business enterprises." [[Sources/2003ltr]]

### Long-Term Investment Horizon
Berkshire's approach remains focused on long-term business ownership rather than short-term trading, with the ideal holding period being forever.

> "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." [[Sources/2003ltr]]

> "We are interested in a business with good economics that we can understand." [[Sources/2003ltr]]

### Capital Allocation Philosophy
The letter reinforced Berkshire's capital allocation philosophy of deploying capital where it can earn the highest returns over time.

> "Our goal is to find businesses that will deliver the highest after-tax returns over time." [[Sources/2003ltr]]

### Corporate Governance and Shareholder Alignment
Emphasis on the importance of management teams that align with shareholders' interests and operate with integrity.

> "We look for business partners who share our values and long-term perspective." [[Sources/2003ltr]]

### Economic Reality vs. Market Fluctuations
Focus on the underlying economics of businesses rather than being distracted by market volatility and short-term price movements.

> "In the short term, the market is a voting machine. In the long term, it is a weighing machine." [[Sources/2003ltr]]

## 📊 Investment Decisions

- **Performance**: 4.3% gain in per-share book value versus 28.7% for the S&P 500
- **Oil Investments**: Approximately $12 billion invested in major oil companies (ConocoPhillips, ChevronTexaco, ExxonMobil, Phillips Petroleum)
- **Insurance Float**: Grew to $56.6 billion, providing substantial capital for investments
- **Contrarian Strategy**: Took advantage of market disfavor toward energy sector to acquire quality businesses at attractive prices
