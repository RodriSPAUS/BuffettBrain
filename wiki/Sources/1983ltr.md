# 1983 Annual Letter Summary

*Report on Berkshire Hathaway's performance and philosophy as of February 25, 1984 (covering 1983 results)*

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Manager-Owner Partnership Catechism
- Formalized 10-point partnership doctrine: "Although our form is corporate, our attitude is partnership. Charlie Munger and I think of our shareholders as owner-partners, and of ourselves as managing partners"
- Core principle: "We do not view the company itself as the ultimate owner of our business assets but, instead, view the company as a conduit through which our shareholders own the assets"
- **Source**: [[1983ltr.md#p0]]

### Intrinsic Business Value vs. Book Value
- Foundational distinction: "Book value tells you what has been put in; intrinsic business value estimates what can be taken out"
- Two drivers of intrinsic value exceeding book value: (1) marketable stocks carried at lower of cost/market, (2) economic Goodwill far larger than accounting Goodwill
- **Source**: [[1983ltr.md#p2]]

### Economic Goodwill Revolution
- Explicit reversal of prior bias: "My own thinking has changed drastically from 35 years ago when I was taught to favor tangible assets and to shun businesses whose value depended largely upon economic Goodwill"
- New preference: "businesses that possess large amounts of enduring Goodwill and that utilize a minimum of tangible assets"
- **Source**: [[1983ltr.md#p2]]

### Nebraska Furniture Mart Acquisition
- First major acquisition under new philosophy: "acquisition of a majority interest in Nebraska Furniture Mart and our association with Rose Blumkin and her family"
- Strategic rationale: "ideal business - one built upon exceptional value to the customer that in turn translates into exceptional economics for its owners"
- **Source**: [[1983ltr.md#p1]]

## 📊 Investment Decisions

### GEICO Special Distribution
- Tax-efficient treatment: "$21 million special GEICO distribution" treated as dividend (6.9% tax rate) rather than capital gain (28%)
- Strategic principle: "we will not sell small portions of your company... on a basis inconsistent with the value of the entire enterprise"
- **Source**: [[1983ltr.md#p3]]

### Market Penetration as Franchise Metric
- Buffalo News: "penetration ratio" defined as "percentage of households within the community purchasing the paper each day"
- National ranking: "number one in weekday penetration among the 100 largest papers in the United States"
- **Source**: [[1983ltr.md#p4]]
