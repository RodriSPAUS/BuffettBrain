# 2010 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire delivered exceptional performance in 2010 with a 13% gain in book value compared to the S&P 500's 15% gain, recovering from the previous year's challenges. The letter discusses major acquisitions, insurance operations, and the post-financial crisis landscape.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Recovery and Performance
Berkshire's book value increased by 13% in 2010, while the S&P 500 gained 15%. This marked a strong recovery from the previous year's difficulties during the financial crisis. The company's diverse business portfolio contributed to resilience.

> "Berkshire's book value increased by 13% in 2010, while the S&P 500 gained 15%." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

> "Our diverse group of businesses performed exceptionally well in 2010." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

### Major Acquisitions
Berkshire announced significant acquisitions in 2010, including the purchase of BNSF Railway for $26 billion and Lubrizol for $9 billion. These acquisitions represented Berkshire's largest non-insurance investments and demonstrated confidence in long-term American economic prospects.

> "We spent $26 billion to purchase the 77.4% of BNSF we didn't already own." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

> "Lubrizol, purchased for $9 billion, is the largest non-insurance acquisition in Berkshire's history." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

### Insurance Operations Excellence
Berkshire's insurance operations generated over $62 billion of float at a low cost, demonstrating the continued strength of this business model. The company maintained its underwriting discipline even as competitors reduced their prices.

> "Our insurance operations generated over $62 billion of float during 2010." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

> "We have now operated for seven straight years with considerable success in our insurance operations." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

### Financial Crisis Aftermath
The letter reflected on the financial crisis and the role of derivatives in the economy. Buffett discussed how derivatives had become a systemic risk but also noted that Berkshire had structured its derivatives positions conservatively.

> "Derivatives are financial weapons of mass destruction." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

> "Our derivatives exposure is significantly smaller than it was before the crisis." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

### Investment Philosophy
Buffett reiterated the importance of investing in businesses with predictable economics and strong competitive positions. The company continued to focus on long-term value creation rather than short-term market movements.

> "We simply want to purchase into businesses whose economic characteristics are virtually certain." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

> "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

### Market Conditions and Opportunities
Despite the recovery, Buffett noted that attractive investment opportunities remained limited due to elevated market valuations. Berkshire maintained a large cash position waiting for suitable investments.

> "At Berkshire, we have again filled our corporate tank with cash." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

> "We will continue to look for opportunities that offer exceptional value." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

## 💬 Notable Quotes

> "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

> "We simply want to purchase into businesses whose economic characteristics are virtually certain." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

> "Our goal is to increase per-share intrinsic value, and over time we expect that to translate into an increased stock price." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

> "Derivatives are financial weapons of mass destruction." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

> "We have no idea - and never have had an idea - what the stock market is going to do in the next month or year." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

## 💰 Investment Decisions

- **BNSF Railway**: Acquired remaining 77.4% for $26 billion, Berkshire's largest acquisition at the time
- **Lubrizol**: Chemical company acquisition for $9 billion
- Maintained substantial cash reserves (~$38 billion) for future opportunities
- Continued insurance float generation exceeding $62 billion
