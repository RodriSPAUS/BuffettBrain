# 2010 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 13% gain in per-share book value in 2010, slightly underperforming the S&P 500's 15.1% gain. The letter focused heavily on the acquisition of Burlington Northern Santa Fe (BNSF), which was the highlight of 2010. Buffett discussed how this acquisition increased Berkshire's earning power significantly and positioned the company well for future growth in rail transportation.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### BNSF Acquisition Impact
The acquisition of Burlington Northern Santa Fe proved to be working out even better than expected, increasing Berkshire's "normal" earning power by nearly 40% pre-tax and well over 30% after-tax. This acquisition represented a major strategic bet on the future of freight transportation in America.

### Railroad Competitive Advantages
Railroads have major cost and environmental advantages over trucking, with BNSF moving each ton of freight a record 500 miles on a single gallon of diesel fuel, making it three times more fuel-efficient than trucking. This provides a sustainable competitive advantage in operating costs.

### Capital Investment Strategy
Berkshire demonstrated its enthusiasm for investment by spending $6 billion on property and equipment in 2010, with 90% ($5.4 billion) spent in the United States. Plans for 2011 included setting a new record with $8 billion in capital spending, all in the United States.

### Long-term Perspective
Buffett emphasized that the pace of the earth's movement around the sun is not synchronized with the time required for investment ideas or operating decisions to bear fruit. Some investments, like GEICO's $900 million advertising spend, are made with an eye toward payoffs well down the road.

### Performance Measurement
Berkshire uses book value as an understated proxy for intrinsic value when measuring performance against the S&P 500. The company aims to increase per-share intrinsic value at a rate greater than the S&P 500's increase (including dividends).

### American Economic Optimism
Despite frequent pessimism about America's economic prospects, Buffett argued that America's best days lie ahead, citing how citizens now live an astonishing six times better than when he was born.

### Capital Allocation Constraints
Due to the huge sums of capital Berkshire now manages, the company has eliminated any chance of exceptional performance, though it will strive for better-than-average results.

- Acquisition of remaining 77.4% of Burlington Northern Santa Fe for $22 billion in cash
- $6 billion spent on property and equipment in 2010, 90% in the United States
- Plan to spend $8 billion on capital expenditures in 2011, all in the United States
