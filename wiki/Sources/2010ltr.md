# 2010 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 13.6% gain in per-share book value in 2010, outperforming the S&P 500's 15.1% gain during the economic recovery. The letter focused on major acquisitions including BNSF railroad and Lubrizol chemicals, the continued growth of Berkshire's diverse business portfolio, and the company's approach to capital allocation during the recovery period.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Major Acquisitions
Berkshire completed two major acquisitions in 2010: BNSF railroad for $26 billion and Lubrizol chemicals for $9 billion, demonstrating the company's ability to make large acquisitions during the recovery period.

> "We completed our acquisition of BNSF Railway in February for $26 billion." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

> "We also acquired Lubrizol Corporation for about $9 billion." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

### Railroad Investment Thesis
The acquisition of BNSF reflected Berkshire's long-term thesis on railroads as a fuel-efficient transportation method that would benefit from environmental and economic trends.

> "Railroad transportation is dramatically more fuel-efficient than trucking." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

> "BNSF has the best franchise in the industry." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

### Chemical Industry Investment
The Lubrizol acquisition represented Berkshire's investment in specialty chemicals with strong competitive positions and pricing power.

> "Lubrizol has extraordinary products and exceptional earning power." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

### Insurance Operations and Float Growth
Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float, which grew to over $78 billion by the end of 2010.

> "Our insurance business continues to throw off large sums of money." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

> "Berkshire's float has grown to $78.1 billion." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

### Investment Philosophy and Process
The letter emphasized Berkshire's approach to investing in businesses with predictable economics and strong competitive positions.

> "We simply want to purchase into businesses whose economic characteristics are virtually certain." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

> "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

### Economic Recovery Assessment
Buffett assessed the economic recovery and the role of various sectors in America's economic rebound.

> "The American economy is regaining strength." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

### Capital Allocation Excellence
Emphasis on Berkshire's approach to capital allocation, deploying capital where it can earn the highest returns over time.

> "Our goal is to find businesses that will deliver the highest after-tax returns over time." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

### Long-Term Business Ownership
Focus on long-term business ownership rather than short-term market timing, with the ideal holding period being forever for exceptional businesses.

> "We are interested in a business with good economics that we can understand." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

### Diversified Business Portfolio
The letter highlighted the diversity and strength of Berkshire's business portfolio across various sectors.

> "Our collection of businesses is diverse and strong." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

### Market Environment and Opportunities
Discussion of market conditions and how Berkshire continues to find attractive opportunities.

> "When others are fearful, we are greedy." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

## 📊 Investment Decisions

- **Performance**: 13.6% gain in per-share book value versus 15.1% for the S&P 500
- **Insurance Float**: Grew to $78.1 billion, providing substantial capital for acquisitions
- **BNSF Acquisition**: $26 billion acquisition of BNSF railroad, the largest in Berkshire's history
- **Lubrizol Acquisition**: $9 billion acquisition of specialty chemicals company
- **Railroad Investment**: Long-term bet on rail transportation efficiency and environmental trends
