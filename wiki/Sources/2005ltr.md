# 2005 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 6.4% gain in per-share book value in 2005, underperforming the S&P 500's 4.9% gain. The letter focused on major acquisitions made during the year, the impact of Hurricane Katrina on the insurance business, and the company's approach to capital allocation and business management.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Major Acquisitions in 2005
Berkshire completed several significant acquisitions in 2005 including Medical Protective Company (MedPro), Forest River recreational vehicle manufacturer, Business Wire news distribution service, and Applied Underwriters payroll and insurance services.

### Hurricane Katrina Impact
Hurricane Katrina caused record losses for both Berkshire and the insurance industry, with Berkshire's estimated loss from Katrina at $2.5 billion, and additional $.9 billion from Rita and Wilma.

### Insurance Operations and Float Growth
Despite hurricane losses, Berkshire's insurance operations delivered strong results overall. The company's float grew to $49 billion, and most insurance units achieved underwriting profits.

### GEICO Performance and Growth
GEICO showed exceptional performance with auto policies in force growing by 12.1%, increasing market share from 5.6% to 6.1%. The company invested heavily in advertising ($502 million) and achieved high conversion rates.

### MidAmerican Energy and PacifiCorp Acquisition
Berkshire agreed to acquire PacifiCorp, a major electric utility serving six Western states, significantly expanding MidAmerican's operations.

### Derivatives Experience and Lessons
Detailed discussion of the difficulties in unwinding Gen Re's derivatives operation, with total losses of $404 million since beginning the exit process.

### Business Quality and Competitive Moats
Emphasis on "widening the moat" - continuously improving competitive positions through customer satisfaction, cost reduction, and product improvements.

### Management Philosophy
Importance of passionate managers who run businesses as if they were the only asset their family would own over the next hundred years.
