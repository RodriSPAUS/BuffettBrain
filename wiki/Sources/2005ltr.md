# 2005 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 6.4% gain in per-share book value in 2005, underperforming the S&P 500's 4.9% gain. The letter focused on pension obligations and derivatives, continuing Buffett's warnings about financial instruments that create systemic risks. Buffett also discussed Berkshire's insurance operations and investment philosophy in detail.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Pension Obligation Risks
Buffett warned about the serious risks posed by unfunded pension obligations, both corporate and governmental. He highlighted how these obligations could strain corporate finances and government budgets.

> "The numbers for pension obligations are scary." [[Sources/2005ltr]]

> "Many companies have obligations that far exceed their assets." [[Sources/2005ltr]]

### Government Pension Problems
The letter addressed the significant unfunded liabilities in government pension systems, which pose long-term fiscal challenges.

> "Government pension obligations are enormous and growing." [[Sources/2005ltr]]

### Insurance Operations and Float Growth
Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float, which grew to over $67 billion by the end of 2005.

> "Our insurance business continues to throw off large sums of money." [[Sources/2005ltr]]

> "Berkshire's float has grown to $67.5 billion." [[Sources/2005ltr]]

### Derivatives Continued Warnings
Buffett continued his warnings about derivatives, reiterating that they are financial weapons of mass destruction with systemic risks.

> "Derivatives remain financial weapons of mass destruction." [[Sources/2005ltr]]

> "These instruments carry dangers that, though now latent, are potentially lethal." [[Sources/2005ltr]]

### Investment Philosophy and Process
The letter emphasized Berkshire's approach to investing in businesses with predictable economics and strong competitive positions.

> "We simply want to purchase into businesses whose economic characteristics are virtually certain." [[Sources/2005ltr]]

> "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." [[Sources/2005ltr]]

### Long-Term Business Ownership
Focus on long-term business ownership rather than short-term market timing, with the ideal holding period being forever for exceptional businesses.

> "We are interested in a business with good economics that we can understand." [[Sources/2005ltr]]

### Market Environment and Opportunities
Discussion of the market environment and how Berkshire looks for opportunities when others are fearful.

> "When others are fearful, we are greedy." [[Sources/2005ltr]]

> "We look for occasions when the odds are seriously in our favor." [[Sources/2005ltr]]

### Capital Allocation Excellence
Emphasis on Berkshire's approach to capital allocation, deploying capital where it can earn the highest returns over time.

> "Our goal is to find businesses that will deliver the highest after-tax returns over time." [[Sources/2005ltr]]

### Corporate Finance Issues
The letter discussed various corporate finance issues, including the importance of maintaining strong balance sheets and avoiding excessive leverage.

> "Strong balance sheets provide flexibility during difficult times." [[Sources/2005ltr]]

### Management Quality Assessment
Importance of investing in companies with managers who align with shareholders' interests and operate with integrity.

> "We look for business partners who share our values and long-term perspective." [[Sources/2005ltr]]
