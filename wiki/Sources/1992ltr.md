# 1992 Annual Letter Summary

*Report on Berkshire Hathaway's performance and philosophy as of February 27, 1993 (covering 1992 results)*

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Intrinsic Value Growth Constraint Reinforced
- Structural definition: "You will remember that our goal is to increase our per-share intrinsic value... at a 15% annual rate"
- Quantitative insight: "Our look-through earnings in 1992 were $604 million, and they will need to grow to more than $1.8 billion by the year 2000 if we are to meet that 15% goal"
- **Source**: [[1992ltr.md#p0]]

### Look-Through Earnings Framework Operationalized
- Structural insight: "Look-through earnings consist of: (1) the operating earnings reported... plus; (2) the retained operating earnings of major investees... less; (3) an allowance for the tax that would be paid by Berkshire"
- Behavioral insight: "We cannot promise to achieve the $1.8 billion target. Indeed, we may not even come close to it. But it does guide our decision-making"
- **Source**: [[1992ltr.md#p1]]

### Super-Cat Insurance Competitive Advantage Codified
- Structural analysis: "Currently, Berkshire is second in the U.S. property-casualty industry in net worth... Therefore, we have the capacity to assume risk on a scale that interests virtually no other company"
- Economic insight: "Ajit Jain, who runs our reinsurance operation, is simply the best in this business"
- **Source**: [[1992ltr.md#p2]]

### Acquisition Behavior Taxonomy Refined
- Structural insight: "In my early days as a manager I, too, dated a few toads. They were cheap dates... but my results matched those of acquirers who courted higher-priced toads"
- Behavioral contrast: "After several failures of this type, I finally remembered some useful advice... 'Practice doesn't make perfect; practice makes permanent.'"
- **Source**: [[1992ltr.md#p0]]

## 📊 Investment Decisions

### Central States Indemnity Acquisition Strategy
- Formalized principle: "The purchase was 82% of Central States Indemnity, an insurer that makes monthly payments for credit-card holders who are unable themselves to pay because they have become disabled or unemployed"
- Structural insight: "The Kizer family... retains 18% ownership of the business and will continue to run things just as it has in the past"
- **Source**: [[1992ltr.md#p0]]

### General Dynamics Arbitrage-to-Investment Pivot
- Formalized principle: "I began buying the stock for Berkshire, expecting to tender our holdings for a small profit"
- Behavioral insight: "But then I began studying the company and the accomplishments of Bill Anders... And what I saw made my eyes pop"
- **Source**: [[1992ltr.md#p3]]
