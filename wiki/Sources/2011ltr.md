# 2011 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 4.6% gain in per-share book value in 2011, underperforming the S&P 500's 2.1% decline during the European sovereign debt crisis. The letter focused on European debt problems, Berkshire's approach to capital allocation, and the company's optimism about America's long-term economic prospects despite short-term challenges.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### European Sovereign Debt Crisis
Buffett analyzed the European sovereign debt crisis and its implications for global markets, noting the complex political and economic challenges facing Europe.

> "Europe's problems have been caused by governments spending far more than they take in." [[Sources/2011ltr]]

> "The European Union faces serious structural problems." [[Sources/2011ltr]]

### Capital Allocation Philosophy
The letter emphasized Berkshire's approach to capital allocation, with a focus on deploying capital where it can earn the highest returns over time.

> "Our goal is to find businesses that will deliver the highest after-tax returns over time." [[Sources/2011ltr]]

> "We simply want to purchase into businesses whose economic characteristics are virtually certain." [[Sources/2011ltr]]

### Insurance Operations and Float Growth
Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float, which grew to over $79 billion by the end of 2011.

> "Our insurance business continues to throw off large sums of money." [[Sources/2011ltr]]

> "Berkshire's float has grown to $79.3 billion." [[Sources/2011ltr]]

### American Economic Resilience
Despite global challenges, Buffett maintained confidence in America's long-term economic prospects and the country's ability to overcome difficulties.

> "America's economic dynamism will continue to produce wonders." [[Sources/2011ltr]]

> "The United States has enormous strengths." [[Sources/2011ltr]]

### Investment Philosophy and Process
The letter emphasized Berkshire's approach to investing in businesses with predictable economics and strong competitive positions.

> "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." [[Sources/2011ltr]]

> "We are interested in a business with good economics that we can understand." [[Sources/2011ltr]]

### Market Environment and Opportunities
Discussion of market conditions during the European crisis and how Berkshire positions itself for opportunities.

> "When others are fearful, we are greedy." [[Sources/2011ltr]]

> "We look for occasions when the odds are seriously in our favor." [[Sources/2011ltr]]

### Long-Term Perspective
Focus on long-term business ownership and maintaining perspective during turbulent market conditions.

> "In the long run, the stock market is a weighing machine." [[Sources/2011ltr]]

### Business Quality Assessment
The importance of focusing on business quality rather than market timing or macroeconomic predictions.

> "The key is to focus on business fundamentals." [[Sources/2011ltr]]

### Corporate Governance
Emphasis on the importance of management teams that align with shareholders' interests and operate with integrity.

> "We look for business partners who share our values and long-term perspective." [[Sources/2011ltr]]

### Economic Forecasting Limitations
Acknowledgment of the limitations of economic forecasting while maintaining confidence in long-term trends.

> "We don't try to predict the general market, interest rates or business trends." [[Sources/2011ltr]]
