# 2011 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 4.6% gain in per-share book value in 2011, underperforming the S&P 500's 2.1% decline during the European sovereign debt crisis. The letter focused on European debt problems, Berkshire's approach to capital allocation, and the company's optimism about America's long-term economic prospects despite short-term challenges.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### European Sovereign Debt Crisis Impact
Buffett analyzed the European sovereign debt crisis and its implications for global markets, noting the complex political and economic challenges facing Europe. The letter discussed how the crisis affected global markets and Berkshire's positioning during this period.

### Capital Allocation Philosophy
The letter emphasized Berkshire's approach to capital allocation, with a focus on deploying capital where it can earn the highest returns over time. Buffett reiterated the importance of investing in businesses with predictable economics and strong competitive positions.

### Insurance Operations and Float Growth
Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float, which grew significantly during 2011. The letter highlighted the importance of float as a source of costless capital that funds other opportunities. Total float reached approximately $70.6 billion by the end of 2011.

### American Economic Resilience
Despite global challenges, Buffett maintained confidence in America's long-term economic prospects and the country's ability to overcome difficulties. He emphasized that demographics and market systems would restore housing supply-demand balance.

### Investment Philosophy and Process
The letter emphasized Berkshire's approach to investing in businesses with predictable economics and strong competitive positions. Buffett discussed the importance of understanding business fundamentals rather than relying on economic forecasting.

### Market Environment and Opportunities
Discussion of market conditions during the European crisis and how Berkshire positioned itself for opportunities. The letter noted the challenges in the housing sector but opportunities in other areas.

### Long-Term Perspective
Focus on long-term business ownership and maintaining perspective during turbulent market conditions. Buffett emphasized that the ideal holding period is forever for exceptional businesses.

### Succession Planning
The letter highlighted Berkshire's strong succession planning efforts, with new investment managers Todd Combs and Ted Weschler joining the team. The board's thorough preparation for leadership transition was emphasized.

### Lubrizol Acquisition
Major acquisition of Lubrizol, a worldwide producer of additives and specialty chemicals, was highlighted as a significant addition to Berkshire's portfolio of businesses.

### Big Four Holdings
Discussion of Berkshire's major equity holdings in American Express, Coca-Cola, IBM, and Wells Fargo, emphasizing the value of their undistributed earnings and future prospects.
