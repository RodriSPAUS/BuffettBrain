# 2007 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved an 11.0% gain in per-share book value in 2007, underperforming the S&P 500's 5.5% gain. The letter provided one of Buffett's most prescient warnings about the subprime mortgage crisis that was just beginning to unfold. He warned about the toxic combination of bad loans and complex derivatives that would soon trigger a global financial crisis. The letter also announced the acquisition of Marmon Group.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Subprime Mortgage Crisis Warning
Buffett described how lenders engaged in "weakened lending practices" that led to the housing crisis, with lenders shoveling out money confident that house price appreciation (HPA) would cure all problems.

### Marmon Group Acquisition
Announcement of the largest cash purchase in Berkshire's history - 60% of Marmon Group, with plans to acquire virtually all of the balance within six years. Marmon operates 125 businesses through nine sectors.

### Insurance Operations and Float Growth
Berkshire's insurance operations had an excellent year, with float growing to $58.7 billion by the end of 2007. The company's insurance managers were praised as the best in the business.

### Derivatives Exposure
Discussion of Berkshire's derivative contracts, including 54 contracts requiring payments if certain high-yield bonds default, and put options on four stock indices with 15-20 year terms.

### Investment Philosophy and Process
Emphasis on investing in businesses with durable competitive advantages ("economic moats"), favorable long-term economics, and able management.

### Financial System Fragility
Warning about the fragility of the financial system due to excessive leverage and complex instruments, with the prediction that insurance-industry profit margins would fall significantly in 2008.

### Business Quality Framework
Detailed discussion of the three types of businesses: great (like See's Candy with enduring moats), good (like FlightSafety requiring reinvestment to grow), and gruesome (like airlines requiring constant capital).

## 💬 Notable Quotes

- "It is interesting that the industry has invented new ways to lose money when the old ways seemed to work just fine." - Quote from John Stumpf, CEO of Wells Fargo, about weakened lending practices
- "You only learn who has been swimming naked when the tide goes out" - Reference to seeing the financial folly exposed as house prices fall
- "A deal is a deal" - Emphasizing Berkshire's commitment to honoring agreements

## 💰 Investment Decisions

- Acquisition of 60% of Marmon Group for $4.5 billion, with plans to acquire virtually all of the balance within six years
- Sale of entire PetroChina stake for $4 billion, realizing a $3.5 billion gain
- Continued growth in insurance float to $58.7 billion
- Addition of Richline Group to jewelry operations
