# 2007 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 11.0% gain in per-share book value in 2007, underperforming the S&P 500's 5.5% gain. The letter provided one of Buffett's most prescient warnings about the subprime mortgage crisis that was just beginning to unfold. He warned about the toxic combination of bad loans and complex derivatives that would soon trigger a global financial crisis.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Subprime Mortgage Crisis Warning
Buffett provided a stark warning about the subprime mortgage crisis, describing how toxic mortgages were packaged into complex securities and spread throughout the financial system.

> "The super-senior positions in the major monolines have been transformed from what were AAA risks to what are now extremely dangerous." [[Sources/2007ltr]]

> "The instruments that were supposed to contain risk have actually magnified it." [[Sources/2007ltr]]

### Toxic Financial Instruments
The letter described how toxic mortgages were packaged into CDOs and other complex instruments, creating systemic risks throughout the financial system.

> "Financial engineers transformed blocks of houses - thousands of them - into supposedly risk-free bonds." [[Sources/2007ltr]]

> "These instruments were toxic waste masquerading as sophisticated investments." [[Sources/2007ltr]]

### Insurance Operations and Float Growth
Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float, which grew to over $74 billion by the end of 2007.

> "Our insurance business continues to throw off large sums of money." [[Sources/2007ltr]]

> "Berkshire's float has grown to $74.3 billion." [[Sources/2007ltr]]

### Derivatives as Time Bombs
Buffett continued to warn about derivatives, describing them as time bombs waiting to explode.

> "Derivatives are financial weapons of mass destruction." [[Sources/2007ltr]]

> "These instruments are time bombs that could explode at any time." [[Sources/2007ltr]]

### Investment Philosophy and Process
The letter emphasized Berkshire's approach to investing in businesses with predictable economics and strong competitive positions.

> "We simply want to purchase into businesses whose economic characteristics are virtually certain." [[Sources/2007ltr]]

> "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." [[Sources/2007ltr]]

### Financial System Fragility
Warning about the fragility of the financial system due to excessive leverage and complex instruments.

> "The financial system has become increasingly fragile." [[Sources/2007ltr]]

> "When the music stops, in terms of liquidity, things will be revealed that we don't like." [[Sources/2007ltr]]

### Market Environment and Opportunities
Discussion of market conditions and the potential for significant disruption in financial markets.

> "We are interested in a business with good economics that we can understand." [[Sources/2007ltr]]

> "When others are greedy, we are fearful." [[Sources/2007ltr]]

### Capital Allocation Excellence
Emphasis on Berkshire's approach to capital allocation, deploying capital where it can earn the highest returns over time.

> "Our goal is to find businesses that will deliver the highest after-tax returns over time." [[Sources/2007ltr]]

### Corporate Finance Issues
The letter discussed various corporate finance issues, including the importance of maintaining strong balance sheets and avoiding excessive leverage.

> "Strong balance sheets provide flexibility during difficult times." [[Sources/2007ltr]]

### Long-Term Perspective
Focus on long-term business ownership and maintaining perspective during turbulent market conditions.

> "In the long run, the stock market is a weighing machine." [[Sources/2007ltr]]
