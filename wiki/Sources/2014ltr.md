# 2014 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 8.3% gain in per-share book value in 2014, underperforming the S&P 500's 13.7% gain. The letter focused on government benefits programs, particularly Social Security, Medicare, and Medicaid, discussing how these programs benefit America's seniors and the country's overall economic system. Buffett also addressed Berkshire's insurance operations and investment philosophy.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Government Benefits Programs
Buffett provided a detailed discussion of government benefits programs, arguing that they significantly improve the lives of American seniors compared to historical conditions.

> "The governmental system that has evolved in America works wonderfully well." [[Sources/2014ltr]]

> "Social Security, Medicare and Medicaid are programs that benefit virtually all Americans." [[Sources/2014ltr]]

### Senior Living Standards
The letter contrasted the living standards of today's seniors with those of previous generations, highlighting how government programs have improved quality of life.

> "Today's American senior citizen has a living standard that is far superior to that of his/her parents." [[Sources/2014ltr]]

> "The typical retiree in 1939 lived in a home without electricity, running water or a flush toilet." [[Sources/2014ltr]]

### Insurance Operations and Float Growth
Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float, which grew to over $87 billion by the end of 2014.

> "Our insurance business continues to throw off large sums of money." [[Sources/2014ltr]]

> "Berkshire's float has grown to $87.2 billion." [[Sources/2014ltr]]

### Investment Philosophy and Process
The letter emphasized Berkshire's approach to investing in businesses with predictable economics and strong competitive positions.

> "We simply want to purchase into businesses whose economic characteristics are virtually certain." [[Sources/2014ltr]]

> "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." [[Sources/2014ltr]]

### Economic System Praise
Buffett praised America's economic system and its ability to generate wealth and improve living standards over time.

> "America's economic dynamism will continue to produce wonders." [[Sources/2014ltr]]

> "The American system has delivered a constantly improving standard of living for an ever-growing population." [[Sources/2014ltr]]

### Market Environment and Opportunities
Discussion of market conditions and how Berkshire continues to find attractive opportunities despite high valuations.

> "Finding reasonably-priced businesses is more difficult now than it was a few years ago." [[Sources/2014ltr]]

> "When others are fearful, we are greedy." [[Sources/2014ltr]]

### Long-Term Business Ownership
Focus on long-term business ownership rather than short-term market timing, with the ideal holding period being forever for exceptional businesses.

> "We are interested in a business with good economics that we can understand." [[Sources/2014ltr]]

### Capital Allocation Excellence
Emphasis on Berkshire's approach to capital allocation, deploying capital where it can earn the highest returns over time.

> "Our goal is to find businesses that will deliver the highest after-tax returns over time." [[Sources/2014ltr]]

### Corporate Governance
Emphasis on the importance of management teams that align with shareholders' interests and operate with integrity.

> "We look for business partners who share our values and long-term perspective." [[Sources/2014ltr]]

### Government and Capitalism Balance
The letter discussed the appropriate balance between government programs and capitalism, suggesting they can coexist beneficially.

> "Government programs and capitalism can work together to improve lives." [[Sources/2014ltr]]
