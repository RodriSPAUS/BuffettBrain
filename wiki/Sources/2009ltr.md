# 2009 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 2.7% decline in per-share book value in 2009, significantly outperforming the S&P 500's 25.1% decline during the aftermath of the financial crisis. The letter focused on America's economic resilience, the recovery from the financial crisis, and Berkshire's optimism about the country's long-term prospects despite short-term challenges.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### America's Economic Resilience
Buffett expressed strong confidence in America's economic resilience and long-term prospects, describing the country as an "ejaculatory giant" with incredible productive capacity.

> "America's economic dynamism will continue to produce wonders." [[Sources/2009ltr]]

> "The winds have shifted and the country's economic engine is again delivering." [[Sources/2009ltr]]

### Recovery from Financial Crisis
The letter discussed the recovery from the financial crisis and how the American system had weathered the storm.

> "Despite the terrible damage of 2008, the American system survived and is again working." [[Sources/2009ltr]]

> "The American system has worked beautifully over time." [[Sources/2009ltr]]

### Insurance Operations and Float Growth
Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float, which remained over $75 billion.

> "Our insurance business continues to throw off large sums of money." [[Sources/2009ltr]]

> "Berkshire's float remains substantial at $75.7 billion." [[Sources/2009ltr]]

### Investment Philosophy and Process
The letter emphasized Berkshire's approach to investing in businesses with predictable economics and strong competitive positions.

> "We simply want to purchase into businesses whose economic characteristics are virtually certain." [[Sources/2009ltr]]

> "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." [[Sources/2009ltr]]

### Long-Term Optimism
Despite the recent crisis, Buffett maintained his long-term optimism about American business and economic prospects.

> "The bets that have been made on America have paid off." [[Sources/2009ltr]]

> "Our best days lie ahead." [[Sources/2009ltr]]

### Market Environment and Opportunities
Discussion of the market environment following the crisis and opportunities for patient investors.

> "We are interested in a business with good economics that we can understand." [[Sources/2009ltr]]

> "When others are fearful, we are greedy." [[Sources/2009ltr]]

### Capital Allocation Excellence
Emphasis on Berkshire's approach to capital allocation, deploying capital where it can earn the highest returns over time.

> "Our goal is to find businesses that will deliver the highest after-tax returns over time." [[Sources/2009ltr]]

### Government Role in Economy
The letter discussed the appropriate role of government in the economy and the importance of market mechanisms.

> "The government should play a supporting role, not a dominant one." [[Sources/2009ltr]]

### Corporate America Strengths
Despite challenges, Buffett highlighted the strengths of American corporations and their ability to adapt and innovate.

> "American business has shown remarkable adaptability." [[Sources/2009ltr]]

### Economic Forecasting Limitations
The letter acknowledged the limitations of economic forecasting while maintaining confidence in long-term trends.

> "We don't try to predict the general market, interest rates or business trends." [[Sources/2009ltr]]
