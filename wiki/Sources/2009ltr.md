# 2009 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 19.8% gain in per-share book value in 2009, significantly outperforming the S&P 500's 26.5% gain. The letter discussed the acquisition of Burlington Northern Santa Fe (BNSF), which added approximately 65,000 shareholders. Buffett outlined Berkshire's economic principles and approach to measuring performance, emphasizing focus on intrinsic value rather than market price fluctuations.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### BNSF Acquisition
The acquisition of Burlington Northern Santa Fe added significant scale to Berkshire, bringing approximately 65,000 new shareholders to the existing 500,000. This acquisition marked a major expansion of Berkshire's operations.

### Performance Measurement Approach
Berkshire measures performance against the S&P 500 as a benchmark, with the goal of outperforming this low-cost index. Using per-share book value as a proxy for intrinsic value, despite its limitations, as the ideal measure would be changes in intrinsic value which cannot be calculated with precision.

### Business Selection Criteria
Berkshire avoids businesses whose futures cannot be evaluated regardless of how exciting their products may be. The company sticks with businesses whose profit picture for decades to come seems reasonably predictable.

### Financial Strength and Independence
Berkshire maintains substantial liquidity and avoids dependence on external funding. During the 2008 financial crisis, Berkshire was a supplier of liquidity and capital to the system, not a supplicant, pouring $15.5 billion into the business world when others could only look to the government.

### Management Philosophy
Berkshire operates subsidiaries independently without excessive supervision. The company prefers decentralized decision-making at the operating level rather than centralized bureaucracy, trusting managers to maintain an owner-oriented attitude.

### Capital Allocation Focus
The company limits central management to allocating capital, controlling enterprise risk, choosing managers and setting their compensation, while operational decisions are made at the business level.

### Insurance Business as Growth Engine
Property-casualty insurance operations have been the engine behind Berkshire's growth and will continue to be. The insurance model provides float that can be invested to generate additional returns.

## 💬 Notable Quotes

- "Invert, always invert" as an aid to solving difficult problems
- "We make no attempt to woo Wall Street. Investors who buy and sell based upon media or analyst commentary are not for us."
- "We are certain, for example, that the economy will be in shambles throughout 2009 – and probably well beyond – but that conclusion does not tell us whether the market will rise or fall."
- "Scaling up to giant size doesn't change that truth" - about business partnerships

## 💰 Investment Decisions

- Acquisition of Burlington Northern Santa Fe (BNSF)
- Maintained over $20 billion in cash-equivalent assets for financial strength
- Continued focus on businesses with predictable long-term economics
