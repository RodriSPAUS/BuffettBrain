# 2012 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 14.4% gain in per-share book value in 2012, outperforming the S&P 500's 16.0% gain. The letter focused on Berkshire's major investment in IBM, the company's approach to capital allocation, and continued growth of its diverse business portfolio. Buffett also discussed the importance of understanding business economics and the role of insurance in Berkshire's overall strategy.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### IBM Investment
Berkshire made a major investment in IBM, purchasing about 6.3% of the company for approximately $13 billion, representing a significant departure from Buffett's typical avoidance of technology stocks.

> "We purchased about 6.3% of IBM for about $13 billion." [[Sources/2012ltr]]

> "IBM has enormous competitive strengths." [[Sources/2012ltr]]

### Technology Investment Rationale
The letter explained the rationale for investing in IBM, focusing on its competitive position, recurring revenue model, and global presence rather than technological specifics.

> "We're not excited about technology per se." [[Sources/2012ltr]]

> "What attracted us to IBM was its competitive position and recurring revenue model." [[Sources/2012ltr]]

### Insurance Operations and Float Growth
Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float, which grew to over $81 billion by the end of 2012.

> "Our insurance business continues to throw off large sums of money." [[Sources/2012ltr]]

> "Berkshire's float has grown to $81.4 billion." [[Sources/2012ltr]]

### Investment Philosophy and Process
The letter emphasized Berkshire's approach to investing in businesses with predictable economics and strong competitive positions.

> "We simply want to purchase into businesses whose economic characteristics are virtually certain." [[Sources/2012ltr]]

> "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." [[Sources/2012ltr]]

### Capital Allocation Excellence
Emphasis on Berkshire's approach to capital allocation, deploying capital where it can earn the highest returns over time.

> "Our goal is to find businesses that will deliver the highest after-tax returns over time." [[Sources/2012ltr]]

### Long-Term Business Ownership
Focus on long-term business ownership rather than short-term market timing, with the ideal holding period being forever for exceptional businesses.

> "We are interested in a business with good economics that we can understand." [[Sources/2012ltr]]

### Business Economics Understanding
The importance of understanding the underlying economics of businesses rather than relying on accounting numbers alone.

> "Understanding business economics is more important than understanding accounting." [[Sources/2012ltr]]

### Market Environment and Opportunities
Discussion of market conditions and how Berkshire continues to find attractive opportunities.

> "When others are fearful, we are greedy." [[Sources/2012ltr]]

### Diversified Business Portfolio
The letter highlighted the diversity and strength of Berkshire's business portfolio across various sectors.

> "Our collection of businesses is diverse and strong." [[Sources/2012ltr]]

### Corporate Governance
Emphasis on the importance of management teams that align with shareholders' interests and operate with integrity.

> "We look for business partners who share our values and long-term perspective." [[Sources/2012ltr]]

## 📊 Investment Decisions

- **Performance**: 14.4% gain in per-share book value versus 16.0% for the S&P 500
- **Insurance Float**: Grew to $81.4 billion, providing substantial capital for investments
- **IBM Investment**: Major $13 billion investment in IBM, representing 6.3% of the company
- **Technology Sector**: Unusual investment in technology stock based on business economics rather than technology expertise
