# 2012 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 14.4% gain in per-share book value in 2012, outperforming the S&P 500's 16.0% gain. The letter focused on Berkshire's major investment in IBM, the company's approach to capital allocation, and continued growth of its diverse business portfolio. Buffett also discussed the importance of understanding business economics and the role of insurance in Berkshire's overall strategy.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### IBM Investment
Berkshire made a major investment in IBM, purchasing about 6.3% of the company for approximately $13 billion, representing a significant departure from Buffett's typical avoidance of technology stocks. The letter explained the rationale focusing on IBM's competitive position and recurring revenue model rather than technological specifics.

### Technology Investment Rationale
The letter explained the rationale for investing in IBM, focusing on its competitive position, recurring revenue model, and global presence rather than technological specifics. Buffett acknowledged being late to the IBM investment opportunity.

### Insurance Operations and Float Growth
Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float, which grew to over $73 billion by the end of 2012. The letter highlighted that insurance operations delivered a $1.6 billion underwriting gain, marking the tenth consecutive year of profitable underwriting.

### Investment Philosophy and Process
The letter emphasized Berkshire's approach to investing in businesses with predictable economics and strong competitive positions. Buffett discussed the importance of understanding business fundamentals rather than relying on market timing.

### Capital Allocation Excellence
Emphasis on Berkshire's approach to capital allocation, deploying capital where it can earn the highest returns over time. The letter noted that Berkshire spent a record $9.8 billion on plant and equipment in 2012.

### Powerhouse Five Performance
Detailed discussion of the "powerhouse five" non-insurance companies (BNSF, Iscar, Lubrizol, Marmon Group, and MidAmerican Energy) which delivered record earnings of $10.1 billion pre-tax in 2012.

### Long-Term Business Ownership
Focus on long-term business ownership rather than short-term market timing, with the ideal holding period being forever for exceptional businesses.

### Business Economics Understanding
The importance of understanding the underlying economics of businesses rather than relying on accounting numbers alone. The letter discussed "non-real" amortization expenses that don't reflect actual business costs.

### Market Environment and Opportunities
Discussion of market conditions and how Berkshire continued to find attractive opportunities. Buffett noted that many CEOs were hesitant due to "uncertainty" but Berkshire continued investing.

### Diversified Business Portfolio
The letter highlighted the diversity and strength of Berkshire's business portfolio across various sectors, with many subsidiaries performing well despite tepid U.S. growth.

### Corporate Governance
Emphasis on the importance of management teams that align with shareholders' interests and operate with integrity. Recognition of operating managers who run businesses as if they were the only asset owned by their families.

### Heinz Acquisition
Announcement of agreement to acquire 50% of H.J. Heinz holding company, with Berkshire contributing about $4 billion for common equity and investing $8 billion in preferred shares yielding 9%.

## 📊 Investment Decisions

- **Performance**: 14.4% gain in per-share book value versus 16.0% for the S&P 500
- **Insurance Float**: Grew to $73.1 billion, providing substantial capital for investments
- **IBM Investment**: Major $13 billion investment in IBM, representing 6.0% of the company by end of year
- **Heinz Acquisition**: Agreement to acquire 50% of H.J. Heinz with $12 billion total investment
- **Powerhouse Five**: Aggregate earnings of $10.1 billion pre-tax, up from $9.7 billion in 2011
