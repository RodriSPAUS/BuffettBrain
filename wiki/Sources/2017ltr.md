# 2017 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire's per-share book value grew 23% in 2017 (net worth +$65.3 billion), but Buffett flags upfront that only $36 billion of that gain came from operations — the remaining $29 billion came from the December 2017 U.S. tax reform revaluing Berkshire's deferred tax liabilities. He also warns shareholders about a new GAAP rule that will force unrealized stock gains/losses into reported net income going forward, making that headline number "useless" for analysis. The letter closes the loop on "The Bet," Buffett's ten-year wager against fund-of-hedge-funds, with a final tally and an unexpected coda: the bet's proceeds were switched from bonds into Berkshire stock in 2012. The year also brought Berkshire's first underwriting loss in 15 years (from three major hurricanes) and the elevation of Ajit Jain and Greg Abel to Vice Chairman.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Tax Reform and a New Accounting Rule
Of the $65.3 billion net worth gain, $29 billion came from Congress's rewrite of the U.S. tax code, not operations. Separately, Buffett warns that a new GAAP rule requiring unrealized gains/losses on Berkshire's $170 billion equity portfolio to flow through reported net income will create "truly wild and capricious swings" in the GAAP bottom line, and urges shareholders to focus instead on normalized operating earning power.

### Acquisitions: Pilot Flying J and a Wave of Bolt-Ons
Berkshire's one major stand-alone purchase of 2017 was a 38.6% partnership interest in Pilot Flying J (PFJ), the country's leading travel-center operator (~$20 billion annual volume, 750 locations, run by the Haslam family since "Big Jim" Haslam started with one gas station 60 years ago). Berkshire has a contractual path to 80% ownership by 2023. On why big deals were scarce in 2017's overheated M&A market, Buffett writes:

> "That last requirement proved a barrier to virtually all deals were viewed in 2017, as prices for decent, but far from spectacular, businesses hit an all-time high." [[Sources/2017ltr]] [[Concepts/MarginOfSafety]]

Bolt-on acquisitions included Clayton Homes buying two conventional-home builders (Oakwood Homes, Harris Doyle); Shaw Industries buying U.S. Floors, lifting Shaw's 2017 sales to $5.7 billion; HomeServices' "growth exploded" with the purchase of Long & Foster (the industry's #3 broker), Houlihan Lawrence, and Gloria Nilson, adding 12,300 agents and $127 billion of "sides" volume for the year; and Precision Castparts buying Wilhelm Schulz GmbH, a German maker of corrosion-resistant fittings, about which Buffett candidly admits: "I don't understand manufacturing operations as well as I do the activities of real estate brokers" — or home builders or truck stops, for that matter. [[Sources/2017ltr]]

### Insurance: A Rare Underwriting Loss After Three Hurricanes
Float grew to $114.5 billion at year-end 2017, boosted by a record $10.2 billion premium on a single reinsurance deal — assuming up to $20 billion of AIG's long-tail losses:

| Year | Premium Volume ($M) | Float ($M) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1970 | $39 | $39 |
| 1980 | 185 | 237 |
| 1990 | 582 | 1,632 |
| 2000 | 19,343 | 27,871 |
| 2010 | 30,749 | 65,832 |
| 2017 | 60,597 | 114,500 |

After 14 consecutive years of underwriting profit totaling $28.3 billion pre-tax, Berkshire posted a $3.2 billion pre-tax underwriting loss in 2017, driven by an estimated $3 billion (about $2 billion after tax) of losses from three hurricanes (Harvey, Irma, Maria) against an industry-wide loss Buffett estimates at roughly $100 billion — implying Berkshire absorbed about 3% of the industry's loss, which he calls a reasonable estimate of Berkshire's likely future mega-catastrophe share. He quotes analyst V.J. Dowling on the danger of trusting insurer self-reporting:

> "The loss reserves of an insurer are similar to a self-graded exam." [[Sources/2017ltr]]

Berkshire estimates the annual probability of a $400 billion U.S. mega-catastrophe at about 2%, with Berkshire's likely share of such a loss around $12 billion — an amount smaller than a single year's non-insurance earnings.

### Non-Insurance Operations: A Simplified Summary
Departing from the previous multi-section format, Buffett gives a condensed roll-up: non-insurance pre-tax income was $20 billion in 2017 (+$950 million), with about 44% coming from BNSF and Berkshire Hathaway Energy combined. The next tier — Clayton Homes, IMC, Lubrizol, Marmon and Precision Castparts — earned $5.5 billion combined; a second tier (Forest River, Johns Manville, MiTek, Shaw, TTI) earned $2.1 billion, up from $1.7 billion. At year-end, Berkshire held $116.0 billion in cash and Treasury Bills, up from $86.4 billion, which Buffett calls "far beyond the level Charlie and I wish Berkshire to have."

### Investments: The Top-15 Table and Kraft Heinz
The Kraft Heinz stake (325,442,152 shares, cost $9.8 billion, carried at $17.6 billion GAAP, market value $25.3 billion) remained excluded from the ranked table as an equity-method holding. New positions appeared in Apple ($20,961M cost / $28,213M market, 3.3%), Bank of America ($5,007M/$20,664M, 6.8%), Bank of New York Mellon, BYD, and General Motors, alongside American Express ($1,287M/$15,056M, 17.6%), Coca-Cola ($1,299M/$18,352M, 9.4%) and Wells Fargo ($11,837M/$29,276M, 9.9%), for total common stocks of $74,676M cost / $170,540M market.

### Four Historical Stock-Price Collapses
Buffett tabulates Berkshire's four worst peak-to-trough declines in 53 years — March 1973–January 1975 (–59.1%), October 1987 (–37.1%), 1998–2000 (–48.9%), and 2008–2009 (–50.7%) — as the strongest argument he can offer against ever buying stocks with borrowed money, since "there is simply no telling how far stocks can fall in a short period." [[Sources/2017ltr]] He references Kipling's poem "If" for the mindset needed to exploit such declines rather than be paralyzed by them.

### "The Bet" Concludes: A $2.2 Million Gift and an Unplanned Lesson
Buffett's ten-year wager (begun December 19, 2007) that an unmanaged S&P 500 index fund would beat a basket of fund-of-hedge-funds after fees is now settled. Final results: the five funds-of-funds gained a cumulative 21.7%, 42.3%, 87.7%, 2.8% and 27.0% respectively over the period (average annual gains of 2.0%–6.5%, one exception at 0.3%), while the S&P index fund gained 125.8% cumulative (8.5% annualized). Buffett's blunt summary:

> "Performance comes, performance goes." [[Sources/2017ltr]]

The bet had an unplanned twist: Buffett and counterparty Ted Seides (of Protégé Partners) had each funded their side with zero-coupon Treasury bonds set to mature in 2017, but in November 2012, with those bonds trading at a yield of just 0.88%, the two agreed to sell the bonds and buy Berkshire "B" shares instead — a switch that only needed to beat the paltry bond yield to succeed. The result: designated charity Girls Inc. of Omaha received $2,222,279, more than double the $1 million originally targeted. Buffett draws three lessons: that a long enough time horizon makes diversified equities safer than "risk-free" bonds; that most professional-manager fee structures reward the manager regardless of client outcome; and that Protégé and Buffett together made exactly one investment decision in ten years, while the underlying 200-plus hedge-fund managers made "tens of thousands" of trades to worse effect.

### Ajit Jain and Greg Abel Become Vice Chairmen
Early in 2018, Berkshire's board elected Ajit Jain and Greg Abel as directors and Vice Chairmen — Jain over insurance operations, Abel over all other businesses — while Buffett and Munger focus on investments and capital allocation.

> "Ajit is now responsible for insurance operations, and Greg oversees the rest of our businesses." [[Sources/2017ltr]] [[Concepts/ManagementQuality]]

## 📊 Investment Decisions

- **Performance**: Book value per share +23% (of which $29 billion of the $65.3 billion net-worth gain was tax-reform-driven, not operational)
- **Acquisitions**: 38.6% of Pilot Flying J (path to 80% by 2023); bolt-ons at Clayton Homes, Shaw Industries, HomeServices, and Precision Castparts
- **Insurance**: First underwriting loss in 15 years (-$3.2 billion pre-tax) after three major hurricanes; float grew to $114.5 billion on a record $10.2 billion AIG reinsurance premium
- **New equity positions**: Apple, Bank of America, Bank of New York Mellon, BYD, and General Motors added to the top-holdings table
- **"The Bet" concluded**: S&P 500 index fund returned 125.8% cumulative versus a 2.8%–87.7% range for the five funds-of-funds; Girls Inc. of Omaha received $2,222,279
- **Succession**: Ajit Jain and Greg Abel elected Vice Chairmen, splitting operational oversight
