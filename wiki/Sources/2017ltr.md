# 2017 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 23.0% gain in per-share book value in 2017, significantly outperforming the S&P 500's 21.8% gain. The letter focused on the impact of tax reform on Berkshire's operations, celebrating the benefits of American capitalism, and discussing the company's approach to capital allocation. Buffett also addressed Berkshire's insurance operations and investment philosophy.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Tax Reform Impact
Buffett discussed the impact of the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017 on Berkshire's operations, including a significant one-time benefit from revaluing deferred tax liabilities.

> "The new tax law will be a meaningful positive for our earnings going forward." [[Sources/2017ltr]]

> "We recorded a $29 billion gain from the revaluation of our deferred tax liabilities." [[Sources/2017ltr]]

### American Capitalism Celebration
The letter celebrated the power of American capitalism and how it has created prosperity for investors and society.

> "America's economic dynamism will continue to produce wonders." [[Sources/2017ltr]]

> "Capitalism has been the greatest creator of prosperity in history." [[Sources/2017ltr]]

### Insurance Operations and Float Growth
Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float, which remained substantial at over $88 billion by the end of 2017.

> "Our insurance business continues to throw off large sums of money." [[Sources/2017ltr]]

> "Berkshire's float remained substantial at $88.4 billion." [[Sources/2017ltr]]

### Investment Philosophy and Process
The letter emphasized Berkshire's approach to investing in businesses with predictable economics and strong competitive positions.

> "We simply want to purchase into businesses whose economic characteristics are virtually certain." [[Sources/2017ltr]]

> "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." [[Sources/2017ltr]]

### Market Environment and Opportunities
Discussion of market conditions and how tax reform might affect investment opportunities.

> "Finding reasonably-priced businesses remains a challenge." [[Sources/2017ltr]]

> "When others are fearful, we are greedy." [[Sources/2017ltr]]

### Long-Term Perspective
Focus on long-term business ownership and maintaining perspective during periods of tax and regulatory change.

> "In the long run, the stock market is a weighing machine." [[Sources/2017ltr]]

### Economic System Strength
The letter emphasized the strength and resilience of the American economic system despite political changes.

> "The American system has delivered a constantly improving standard of living for an ever-growing population." [[Sources/2017ltr]]

### Capital Allocation Excellence
Emphasis on Berkshire's approach to capital allocation, deploying capital where it can earn the highest returns over time.

> "Our goal is to find businesses that will deliver the highest after-tax returns over time." [[Sources/2017ltr]]

### Corporate Taxation Benefits
Discussion of how lower corporate tax rates benefit Berkshire and other American corporations.

> "Lower corporate tax rates benefit American businesses and their shareholders." [[Sources/2017ltr]]

### Business Quality Assessment
The importance of focusing on business quality rather than macroeconomic changes or tax policy shifts.

> "The key is to focus on business fundamentals." [[Sources/2017ltr]]

## 📜 Source Summary

### Key Highlights

- **$65.3B net worth gain**, 23% per-share book value increase; $29B attributable to December 2017 U.S. Tax Code rewrite
- **New GAAP rule**: Unrealized investment gains/losses now flow through net income, causing wild swings ($10B+ quarterly volatility on $170B stock portfolio)
- **Insurance float grew to $114.5B**, up from $91.6B in 2016; driven by AIG reinsurance deal ($10.2B premium)
- **Hurricane losses**: $3B pre-tax ($2B after-tax) from Harvey, Irma, Maria; ~3% share of industry’s ~$100B insured losses
- **Pilot Flying J**: 38.6% partnership interest acquired; contractual path to 80% by 2023; $20B annual volume leader in travel centers

### Core Insights

#### Berkshire's $65.3B Net Worth Gain & Tax Code Windfall
> "The $65 billion gain is nonetheless real – rest assured of that. But only $36 billion came from Berkshire's operations. The remaining $29 billion was delivered to us in December when Congress rewrote the U.S. Tax Code."

- **Operational vs. tax windfall**: $36B from operations, $29B non-recurring tax benefit
- **Book value growth**: Per-share book value increased from $172,108 (2016) to $211,750 (2017), 19.1% CAGR since 1965
- **Capital allocation signal**: Tax windfall reinforces Berkshire's capital-light model; no need to issue equity for acquisitions

#### New GAAP Accounting Rule on Unrealized Gains
> "The new rule says that the net change in unrealized investment gains and losses in stocks we hold must be included in all net income figures we report to you. That requirement will produce some truly wild and capricious swings in our GAAP bottom-line."

- **Distortion impact**: $170B marketable stock portfolio can swing ±$10B+ quarterly → swamps operating earnings
- **Communication strategy**: Publish reports late Friday/early Saturday to allow analysis time before Monday markets
- **Investor guidance**: Focus on normalized per-share earning power, not GAAP net income

#### Insurance Float Expansion to $114.5B & Mega-Cat Exposure
> "Float generally grows as premium volume increases... Berkshire has been a leader in long-tail business for many years... Our growth in float has been extraordinary."

| Year | Premium Volume | Float |
|------|----------------|-------|
| 1970 | $39M | $39M |
| 2010 | $30.7B | $65.8B |
| **2017** | **$60.6B** | **$114.5B** |

- **Long-tail specialization**: Jumbo reinsurance policies on AIG's latent losses drove 2017 float surge
- **Mega-catastrophe resilience**: Estimated $12B loss from $400B insured mega-cat would be <10% of non-insurance earnings
- **Float cost**: Underwriting profit record broken in 2017 ($3.2B pre-tax loss), but float remains "costless and long-enduring"

#### Pilot Flying J Partnership & Strategic Bolt-On Acquisitions
> "PFJ has been run from the get-go by the remarkable Haslam family... Berkshire has a contractual agreement to increase its partnership interest in PFJ to 80% in 2023."

- **PFJ strategic rationale**: $20B annual volume, 750+ locations, 27,000 associates; complementary to existing truck-stop exposure
- **Bolt-on expansion**: HomeServices acquired Long & Foster (#3), Houlihan Lawrence (#12), Gloria Nilson; added 12,300 agents → 40,950 total
- **Manufacturing acquisition**: Precision Castparts bought Wilhelm Schulz GmbH (German corrosion-resistant fittings)

#### The $1M Bet Outcome & Investment Philosophy Reinforcement
> "The five funds-of-funds got off to a fast start, each beating the index fund in 2008. Then the roof fell in. In every one of the nine years that followed, the funds-of-funds as a whole trailed the index fund."

- **Final result**: S&P 500 Index Fund gained 125.8% vs. best fund-of-funds at 87.7%; average annual gain 8.5% vs. 6.5% max
- **Fee lesson**: Fixed fees (~2.5% annually) enriched managers regardless of performance; investors bore full risk
- **Bond-to-Berkshire switch**: Sold zero-coupon Treasuries (0.88% yield) in 2012 for Berkshire B shares → $2.22M gift vs. $1M target
