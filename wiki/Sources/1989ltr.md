# 1989 Annual Letter Summary

*Report on Berkshire Hathaway's performance and philosophy as of February 23, 1990 (covering 1989 results)*

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Intrinsic Value vs. Book Value Formalized
- Structural definition: "What counts, however, is intrinsic value - the figure indicating what all of our constituent businesses are rationally worth"
- Quantitative insight: "With perfect foresight, this number can be calculated by taking all future cash flows... and discounting them at prevailing interest rates"
- **Source**: [[1989ltr.md#p0]]

### Look-Through Earnings Framework
- Core principle: "Berkshire's fundamental earning power is best measured by a 'look-through' approach"
- Methodology: "append our share of the operating earnings retained by our investees to our own reported operating earnings, excluding capital gains in both instances"
- **Source**: [[1989ltr.md#p1]]

### Managerial Excellence Quantified
- Structural insight: "Stan's managerial skills deliver at least five extra percentage points in profit margin compared to the earnings that would be achieved by an average manager given the same circumstances"
- Behavioral insight: "The Heldman managerial product is so good that a service call is never needed"
- **Source**: [[1989ltr.md#p2]]

### Insurance Industry Commodity Economics Deepened
- Structural analysis: "The industry has hundreds of participants selling a commodity-like product at independently-established prices"
- Economic insight: "Such a configuration... is certain to cause subnormal profitability in all circumstances but one: a shortage of usable capacity"
- **Source**: [[1989ltr.md#p3]]

## 📊 Investment Decisions

### Borsheim’s First-Year Performance
- Formalized principle: "In its first year with Berkshire, Borsheim's met all expectations"
- Structural insight: "Because of the huge volume it does at one location, the store can maintain an enormous selection across all price ranges"
- **Source**: [[1989ltr.md#p4]]

### Catastrophe Reinsurance Strategy
- Formalized principle: "Our willingness to put such a huge sum on the line for a loss that could occur tomorrow sets us apart from any reinsurer in the world"
- Behavioral contrast: "We will accept more reinsurance risk for our own account than any other company because of two factors: (1) our net worth... (2) we simply don't care what earnings we report quarterly"
- **Source**: [[1989ltr.md#p5]]
