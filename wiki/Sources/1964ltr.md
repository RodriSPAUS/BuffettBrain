# 1964 Annual Letter Summary

`Raw/1964ltr.md` holds two documents: the **January 18, 1964 annual letter**
(full-year 1963 results, with an appendix giving the full case histories of a
work-out, Texas National Petroleum, and the completed [[Cases/DempsterMill]]
story) and the **July 8, 1964 mid-year letter**. The headline is "seven fat
years" — Buffett's own name for a streak he immediately warns cannot
continue, in a letter otherwise notable for finally writing out, case by
case, exactly how a work-out actually gets paid.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Seven fat years, and the warning attached to the number

1963: Dow +20.6%, partnership +38.7% ($3,637,167), limited partners +30.5%.
Compounded 1957–1963: Dow 95.1%, partnership 454.5%, limited partners 311.2%
(annual rates 10.0% / 27.7% / 22.3%). Buffett names the streak, then
immediately discounts it:

> "It appears that we have completed seven fat years." [[Sources/1964ltr]]

> "In a more serious vein, I would like to emphasize that, in my judgment; our 17.7 margin over the Dow shown above is unattainable over any long period of time." [[Sources/1964ltr]]

A ten-point advantage, he says, "would be a very satisfactory accomplishment"
— the same ceiling stated a year earlier, now restated against a much larger
number to beat.

### The Joys of Compounding — the Mona Lisa

Francis I paid 4,000 ecus (about $20,000) for the Mona Lisa in 1540. At 6%
after-tax, Buffett calculates, "the estate now would be worth something over
$1,000,000,000,000,000.00" — a quadrillion dollars, "over 3,000 times the
present national debt, all from 6%." A table of $100,000 compounded at 4%,
8%, 12% and 16% over 10, 20 and 30 years makes the same point without the
theatrics.

### The three categories, written out in full for reference

Generals, work-outs and controls are each given a full paragraph definition
for the first time meant to stand as a lasting reference rather than a
per-letter recap. The work-out definition supplies the line that best
explains why these deals are worth doing despite thin margins:

> "A friend refers to this as getting the last nickel after the other fellow has made the first ninety-five cents." [[Sources/1964ltr]]

On where the next dollar of growth will go:

> "If there is any trend as our assets grow, I would expect it to be toward controls which heretofore have been our smallest category." [[Sources/1964ltr]] [[Cases/DempsterMill]]

### Texas National Petroleum — a work-out told with dates

The appendix walks one 1962 oil-producer sellout to Union Oil of California
through every stage: bonds, common stock and warrants bought across the
announcement-to-closing window; the tax ruling delayed month after month
("still hoping for ruling in July" in June, "early next week" in July, still
nothing by September); closing October 31, 1962. Buffett gives the return
directly — about 20% per annum on the bonds, about 22% per annum on the stock
and warrants — and draws the general lesson: "the deals take longer than
originally projected" and "the payouts tend to average a little better than
estimates." A separate delay in the ruling turned on the University of
Southern California's tax-exempt status as the production-payment holder:

> "This posed a new problem for the Internal Revenue Service, but we understood USC was willing to waive this status which still left them with a satisfactory profit after they borrowed all the money from a bank." [[Sources/1964ltr]]

### Dempster Mill — the case closed, told end to end

The appendix retells the whole arc: bought as a general in 1956 at $18 a
share against $72 of book value; five years of small purchases while
becoming "consistently less impressed with the earnings prospects under
existing management"; majority control (over 70%) reached in August–September
1961 at $30.25; Harry Bottle hired April 23, 1962; nine specific operating
fixes; and, in 1963, a sale of the operating assets after "several earlier
deals had fallen through at reasonably advanced stages" — at close to book
value, plus the securities portfolio built up along the way, for a
realization of about $80 per share. Two morals are drawn explicitly: the
business rewards patience ("it is to our advantage to have securities do
nothing price wise for months, or perhaps years"), and current holdings are
never discussed — "should anyone, including partners, ask us whether we are
interested in any security, we must plead the '5th Amendment.'"
[[Cases/DempsterMill]]

### Mid-year 1964 — three companies, unnamed, that will matter later

The July letter reports a first half of +12.0% (partnership) against the
Dow's +10.9%, and reveals a new phase of buying:

> "Our General category now includes three companies where B.P.L. is the largest single stockholder." [[Sources/1964ltr]]

One had been bought for about eighteen months, the other two for about a
year, with no rush to finish: "It would not surprise me if we continue to do
nothing but patiently buy these securities week after week for at least
another year, and perhaps even two years or more." None is named — this is
the buildup that the 1965 letters will reveal as controlling stakes,
including [[Cases/DempsterMill]]'s eventual successor as the partnership's
largest control position.

### Investment companies — the duck on the pond

A new metaphor for institutional performance tracking the market instead of
beating it:

> "The results of these companies in some ways resemble the activity of a duck sitting on a pond." [[Sources/1964ltr]]

And, on buying being the real skill:

> "This is of particular satisfaction to me since I consider the buying end to be about 90% of this business." [[Sources/1964ltr]]

## 📊 Investment Decisions

- **Dempster Mill Manufacturing — closed.** Full history given end to end: general (1956, $18/share) → board seat → majority control (Aug–Sep 1961, $30.25/share) → Harry Bottle turnaround → operating-asset sale (1963, ~$80/share realization). See [[Cases/DempsterMill]].
- **Texas National Petroleum — a completed work-out.** Bonds, common and warrants bought April–October 1962 around a Union Oil of California sellout; ~20–22% annualized return; closed October 31, 1962.
- **Three unnamed "largest stockholder" positions** — being accumulated patiently since mid-1963/1964, described but not identified.
- **Results (full year 1963):** partnership +38.7% ($3,637,167), limited partners +30.5%, against the Dow's +20.6%. Compounded 1957–63: 454.5% / 311.2% vs 95.1%.
- **Results (H1 1964):** partnership +12.0%, limited partners +10.5%, against the Dow's +10.9%.
- **Structure:** net assets at the start of 1964 were $17,454,900; net unrealized gains entering 1964 of $2,991,090, with $2,826,248.76 realized (96% long-term) by June 30.
