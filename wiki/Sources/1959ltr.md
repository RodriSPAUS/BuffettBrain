# 1959 Partnership Letter Summary

A short letter dominated by one worry: the market looks strong on the surface and
rotten underneath, and Buffett would rather look foolish for caution than lose
capital chasing a "New Era." Behind the caution, the partnerships had their best year
yet, driven by a single holding that had swelled to 35% of assets — the position that
would resolve the following year as [[Cases/SanbornMap]].

## 🔑 Key Themes

### A robust index over a weak market

The Dow rose from 583 to 679 (19.9% with dividends), but Buffett flags the breadth
divergence: more NYSE stocks fell than rose (710 to 628), and both the Railroad and
Utility averages declined. He cites Tri-Continental's president Fred Brown — whose
$400 million fund gained only about 5.7% — on how hard 1959 was for managers "trained
in values," and notes the $1.5 billion Massachusetts Investors Trust managed about 9%.

### The "New Era" is the enemy

The core of the letter is a statement of temperament that never changes across his
whole career [[Concepts/MrMarket]]:

> "I may very well be wrong; however, I would rather sustain the penalties resulting from over-conservatism than face the consequences of error, perhaps with permanent capital loss, resulting from the adoption of a "New Era" philosophy where trees really do grow to the sky." [[Sources/1959ltr]]

He concedes his multi-year caution has "been unnecessary" to date, and holds it
anyway — the priority is avoiding permanent loss, not keeping up.

### One position at 35% of assets

The holding introduced in 1958 has grown from 25% to 35% of assets — "an unusually
large percentage" justified because the company is "partially an investment trust"
owning thirty or forty high-quality securities, carried at a substantial discount to
its asset value. [[Concepts/MarginOfSafety]] Buffett is the largest stockholder by a
wide margin, the two other large holders agree with him, and he expects the situation
resolved "this year." The remaining 65% is the usual mix of undervalued generals and
work-outs, deliberately "insulated from the behavior of the general market."

## 💬 Notable Quotes

> "I may very well be wrong; however, I would rather sustain the penalties resulting from over-conservatism than face the consequences of error, perhaps with permanent capital loss, resulting from the adoption of a "New Era" philosophy where trees really do grow to the sky."

> "To the extent possible, I continue to attempt to invest in situations at least partially insulated from the behavior of the general market."

## 📊 Investment Decisions

- **The 35% concentration** — the future [[Cases/SanbornMap]], a company whose value
  is largely a marketable-securities portfolio held at a discount, with Buffett as
  controlling holder aiming to unlock it within the year.
- **Portfolio posture** — 65% in undervalued situations and work-outs chosen for
  insulation from the market, consistent with the bear-market-outperformance thesis.
- **Results:** the six full-year partnerships gained 22.3% to 30.0%, averaging about
  25.9%, well ahead of the index — achieved, unusually, in a rising market.
