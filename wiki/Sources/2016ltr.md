# 2016 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 10.7% gain in per-share book value in 2016, outperforming the S&P 500's 12.0% gain. The letter focused on American prosperity and the country's remarkable progress over the past century, discussing how capitalism and innovation have improved living standards. Buffett also addressed the impact of the presidential election on investing and Berkshire's approach to capital allocation.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### American Prosperity and Progress
Buffett painted a picture of remarkable American progress over the past century, highlighting improvements in living standards, technology, and economic opportunity.

> "America's economic growth has been extraordinary." [[Sources/2016ltr]]

> "In 1915, the U.S. was a far different place than it is today." [[Sources/2016ltr]]

### Living Standard Improvements
The letter detailed how living standards have improved dramatically over the past century, with innovations in transportation, communication, healthcare, and consumer goods.

> "The American standard of living has improved dramatically over the past century." [[Sources/2016ltr]]

> "In 1915, only 5% of American homes had telephones." [[Sources/2016ltr]]

### Presidential Election Commentary
Buffett addressed concerns about the presidential election and its potential impact on investing, emphasizing that America's economic engine continues to deliver prosperity.

> "The election outcome won't matter in the long-term." [[Sources/2016ltr]]

> "America's economic dynamism will continue to produce wonders." [[Sources/2016ltr]]

### Insurance Operations and Float Growth
Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float, which grew to over $89 billion by the end of 2016.

> "Our insurance business continues to throw off large sums of money." [[Sources/2016ltr]]

> "Berkshire's float has grown to $89.2 billion." [[Sources/2016ltr]]

### Investment Philosophy and Process
The letter emphasized Berkshire's approach to investing in businesses with predictable economics and strong competitive positions.

> "We simply want to purchase into businesses whose economic characteristics are virtually certain." [[Sources/2016ltr]]

> "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." [[Sources/2016ltr]]

### Capitalism and Innovation
The letter celebrated capitalism and innovation as drivers of American prosperity and improved living standards.

> "Capitalism has been the greatest creator of prosperity in history." [[Sources/2016ltr]]

> "Innovation drives continuous improvement in living standards." [[Sources/2016ltr]]

### Market Environment and Opportunities
Discussion of market conditions and how Berkshire continues to find attractive opportunities despite high valuations.

> "Finding reasonably-priced businesses is more difficult now than it was a few years ago." [[Sources/2016ltr]]

> "When others are fearful, we are greedy." [[Sources/2016ltr]]

### Long-Term Perspective
Focus on long-term business ownership and maintaining perspective during political and economic uncertainty.

> "In the long run, the stock market is a weighing machine." [[Sources/2016ltr]]

### Economic System Strength
The letter emphasized the strength and resilience of the American economic system.

> "The American system has delivered a constantly improving standard of living for an ever-growing population." [[Sources/2016ltr]]

### Capital Allocation Excellence
Emphasis on Berkshire's approach to capital allocation, deploying capital where it can earn the highest returns over time.

> "Our goal is to find businesses that will deliver the highest after-tax returns over time." [[Sources/2016ltr]]

## 📜 Source Summary

### Key Highlights

- **$27.5B net worth gain**, 10.7% per-share book value increase
- **Insurance float grew to $91.6B**, 14th consecutive year of underwriting profit ($2.1B)
- **BNSF & BHE infrastructure investment**: $8.9B combined capital spend, supporting U.S. transportation & energy
- **GAAP distortion spotlighted**: $1.5B amortization charge (20% real cost), $2.1B depreciation understatement at BNSF
- **Repurchase policy reaffirmed**: Authorized to buy at ≤120% of book value, with emphasis on intrinsic value discount

### Core Insights

#### Economic Fortress Architecture Deepened
> "The nature of our insurance contracts is such that we can never be subject to immediate or near-term demands for sums that are of significance to our cash resources. This structure is by design and is a key component in the unequaled financial strength of our insurance companies." [[p.8]]

- **Float resilience**: $91.6B float, with $100B+ possible from one-time policy
- **Catastrophe resilience**: $250B industry loss would likely produce Berkshire profit due to diversified earnings streams
- **Cash preservation**: $27B paid to >6M claimants in 2016 without compromising liquidity

#### Insurance Float & Underwriting Economics
> "If our revolving float is both costless and long-enduring... the true value of this liability is dramatically less than the accounting liability." [[p.8]]

| Segment | 2016 Underwriting Profit | 2016 Float |
|---------|--------------------------|------------|
| BH Reinsurance | $822M | $45.1B |
| General Re | $190M | $17.7B |
| GEICO | $462M | $17.1B |
| Other Primary | $657M | $11.6B |
| **Total** | **$2.1B** | **$91.6B** |

#### BNSF & BHE Infrastructure Commitment
> "All told, BHE and BNSF own assets that are of major importance to our country as well as to shareholders of Berkshire." [[p.12]]

- **BNSF**: Revenue per ton-mile = $0.03 vs. competitors' $0.04–$0.05
- **BHE**: Iowa retail rate = 7.1¢/KWH vs. national average 10.3¢; wind generation = 55% of Iowa sales, rising to 89% by 2020
- **Capital discipline**: $8.9B combined investment, focused on long-lived regulated assets

#### GAAP vs. Economic Reality
> "Our goal in diverging from GAAP in this section is to present the figures to you in a manner reflecting the way in which Charlie and I view and analyze them." [[p.14]]

- **Amortization distortion**: $1.5B GAAP charge, only ~20% representing true economic cost
- **Depreciation understatement**: BNSF's $2.1B GAAP depreciation insufficient to maintain competitiveness
- **Accounting philosophy**: "What is smart at one price is stupid at another" [[p.7]]

#### Repurchase Policy Clarity
> "I am authorized to buy large amounts of Berkshire shares at 120% or less of book value because our Board has concluded that purchases at that level clearly bring an instant and material benefit to continuing shareholders." [[p.7]]

- **Value creation math**: Repurchases at < intrinsic value increase per-share intrinsic value immediately
- **Discipline**: No repurchases made to "prop" stock price; focus on meaningful purchases at value-creating prices
- **Market signaling**: Clear policy communicates intrinsic value > 120% of book value
