# 2022 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire's per-share market value grew 4.0% in 2022 against a 19.4% decline for the S&P 500's total return. Operating earnings hit a record $30.8 billion, though Buffett again warns that GAAP net income (which folds in unrealized stock-market swings) is "100% misleading when viewed quarterly or even annually." This is the shortest of the letters in years, built around a few tight set pieces: the Secret Sauce (why Coca-Cola and American Express became two of Berkshire's most valuable holdings simply by being held and not sold), the year's major acquisition (property-casualty insurer Alleghany Corporation), a section on Berkshire's outsized role as a federal taxpayer, and — introduced as a distinct feature for the first time — a curated list of Charlie Munger's aphorisms under the heading Nothing Beats Having a Great Partner.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### The Secret Sauce: Coca-Cola and American Express
Berkshire completed its Coca-Cola purchase (400 million shares, $1.3 billion total cost) in August 1994, and its American Express purchase (also about $1.3 billion) around 1995. Since then, without any further buying, Coke's annual dividend to Berkshire grew from $75 million to $704 million and American Express's from $41 million to $302 million; by year-end 2022 the two stakes were valued at $25 billion and $22 billion respectively, each about 5% of Berkshire's net worth. Buffett contrasts this with a hypothetical flat investment (like a 30-year bond) that had simply held its original $1.3 billion value — it would now be an insignificant 0.3% of net worth. His lesson:

> "The lesson for investors: The weeds wither away in significance as the flowers bloom." [[Sources/2022ltr]]

### The Alleghany Acquisition and Float Growth
Berkshire's major 2022 move was buying Alleghany Corporation, a property-casualty insurer led by Joe Brandon, whom Buffett had worked with previously. Aided by Alleghany, insurance float grew from $147 billion to $164 billion during 2022 — an 8,000-fold increase since Berkshire's first P/C insurer purchase in 1967.

### GAAP Earnings Are "100% Misleading" Quarter to Quarter
Buffett tabulates 2022's wide swings between "operating earnings" and required GAAP earnings by quarter (in billions): Q1 operating $7.0 vs. GAAP $5.5; Q2 operating $9.3 vs. GAAP a $43.8 billion loss; Q3 operating $7.8 vs. GAAP a $2.7 billion loss; Q4 operating $6.7 vs. GAAP $18.2. He urges shareholders to focus solely on operating earnings, which set a full-year record of $30.8 billion, while separately warning that even that preferred metric can be gamed by management, quoting an unnamed CEO's own description of the practice:

> "'Bold imaginative accounting,' as a CEO once described his deception to me, has become one of the shames of capitalism." [[Sources/2022ltr]]

### Share Repurchases: The Auto Dealership Analogy
Berkshire repurchased 1.2% of its own outstanding shares in 2022, while Apple's and American Express's own buybacks separately raised Berkshire's effective ownership in those companies at no cost. Buffett defends repurchases against blanket criticism using a three-partner auto dealership analogy — a passive partner selling their stake back to the company at a price fair to the remaining two harms no one — and is characteristically blunt about critics of the practice:

> "When you are told that all repurchases are harmful to shareholders or to the country, or particularly beneficial to CEOs, you are listening to either an economic illiterate or a silver-tongued demagogue" [[Sources/2022ltr]]

### 58 Years and a Few Figures: Berkshire's Scale Among American Business
Of the roughly 5,000 publicly-traded U.S. companies, only 128 members of the S&P 500 earned $3 billion or more in 2021 (Berkshire's most recent full comparison year), while 23 S&P 500 companies lost money. At year-end 2022, Berkshire was the largest shareholder in eight S&P 500 giants: American Express, Bank of America, Chevron, Coca-Cola, HP Inc., Moody's, Occidental Petroleum, and Paramount Global. Combined with 100%-owned BNSF ($5.9 billion earnings) and 92%-owned BHE ($4.3 billion earnings) — both individually above the $3 billion bar — Buffett counts ten controlled and non-controlled "behemoths" that leave Berkshire more broadly tied to the American economy than any other U.S. company.

### Some Surprising Facts About Federal Taxes
Over the decade ending 2021, the U.S. Treasury collected about $32.3 trillion in taxes while spending $43.9 trillion. Berkshire's corporate income tax contribution over that decade was $32 billion — about one-tenth of one percent of everything the Treasury collected — leading Buffett to a striking hypothetical: had roughly 1,000 taxpayers matched Berkshire's payments, no other business and none of the country's 131 million households would have owed the federal government anything. He illustrates the scale with a physical analogy: a stack of $32 billion in $100 bills would rise more than 21 miles, about three times the cruising altitude of a commercial jet, concluding that Berkshire shareholders can say: "I gave at the office." [[Sources/2022ltr]]

### Nothing Beats Having a Great Partner: Munger's Aphorisms
For the first time presented as a standalone curated feature, Buffett compiles a list of Charlie Munger's recent observations (many drawn from a podcast), including: that the patient investor beats the foolish gambler; that seeing the world undistorted is a prerequisite for good decisions; that indifference to being rational guarantees staying irrational; that leverage is dangerous because "a string of wonderful numbers times zero will always equal zero"; that both Buffett and Munger were slow to warm to railroad stocks but changed their minds as the industry consolidated into four vital carriers; and Munger's own two-line description of how he settles disagreements with Buffett:

> "Warren, think more about it. You're smart and I'm right." [[Sources/2022ltr]] [[People/CharlieMunger]]

## 📊 Investment Decisions

- **Performance**: Per-share market value +4.0% versus the S&P 500's -19.4% total return
- **Operating earnings**: Record $30.8 billion, while GAAP net income swung wildly by quarter
- **Major acquisition**: Alleghany Corporation, a property-casualty insurer led by Joe Brandon
- **Insurance**: Float grew from $147 billion to $164 billion, aided by Alleghany
- **Share repurchases**: 1.2% of Berkshire's outstanding shares bought back
- **Scale**: Berkshire is the largest shareholder in eight S&P 500 companies and owns two more (BNSF, BHE) that would qualify individually
- **Taxes**: Berkshire paid $32 billion in federal corporate income tax over 2012–2021, about 0.1% of all U.S. Treasury collections in that span
