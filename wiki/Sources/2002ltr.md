# 2002 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 10.0% gain in per-share book value in 2002, outperforming the S&P 500's -22.1% loss. The letter focused extensively on corporate scandals and accounting fraud that plagued the business world in 2001-2002, including Enron, WorldCom, and other major companies. Buffett emphasized the importance of integrity in business and proper corporate governance.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Corporate Scandals and Fraud
The letter extensively covered the wave of corporate scandals that shook investor confidence, including Enron, WorldCom, Adelphia, Tyco, and others. Buffett analyzed how these frauds occurred and what investors should learn from them.

> "Accounting shenanigans have been rampant." [[Sources/2002ltr]]

> "Corporate managers have been using 'pro forma' numbers to bamboozle investors." [[Sources/2002ltr]]

### Creative Accounting Practices
Buffett detailed various ways companies manipulated their financial statements, including the misuse of pro forma earnings, special purpose entities, and other accounting techniques to hide debt and inflate earnings.

> "Companies have been presenting 'pro forma' numbers that exclude expenses that are real and ongoing." [[Sources/2002ltr]]

> "Special purpose entities were used to move debt off the balance sheet." [[Sources/2002ltr]]

### Warning Signs for Investors
The letter provided guidance on identifying potential accounting red flags that could signal fraudulent activities or aggressive accounting practices.

> "When management speaks in(optarg) euphemisms, be suspicious." [[Sources/2002ltr]]

> "If management has a history of making acquisitions that require huge goodwill charges, be wary." [[Sources/2002ltr]]

### Integrity in Business
Buffett emphasized that integrity is the most important quality in business leaders, more important than intelligence or energy.

> "Lack of integrity in a manager can destroy the value of an otherwise valuable business." [[Sources/2002ltr]]

> "Integrity is crucial in business relationships." [[Sources/2002ltr]]

### Insurance Operations and Float
Despite market challenges, Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float at low cost, providing capital for investments.

> "Our insurance business continues to throw off large sums of money." [[Sources/2002ltr]]

> "Berkshire's float has grown to $52.9 billion." [[Sources/2002ltr]]

### Long-Term Investment Philosophy
Buffett reaffirmed his commitment to investing in businesses with predictable economics and strong competitive positions, regardless of short-term market conditions.

> "We simply want to purchase into businesses whose economic characteristics are virtually certain." [[Sources/2002ltr]]

> "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." [[Sources/2002ltr]]

### Market Opportunity During Crisis
The scandals and market downturn created opportunities to invest in quality businesses at attractive prices.

> "When others are fearful, we are greedy." [[Sources/2002ltr]]

> "The investment climate has improved significantly due to the market decline." [[Sources/2002ltr]]

### Management Quality Assessment
The letter emphasized the importance of evaluating management quality, particularly their honesty and alignment with shareholders' interests.

> "We look for business partners who share our values and long-term perspective." [[Sources/2002ltr]]

### Economic Reality vs. Accounting Numbers
Buffett stressed the importance of focusing on economic reality rather than accounting numbers, which can be manipulated.

> "Accounting is but an aid to business thinking, never a substitute for it." [[Sources/2002ltr]]
