# 2013 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 18.2% gain in per-share book value in 2013, outperforming the S&P 500's 32.4% gain. The letter focused on the importance of productive assets over unproductive ones, Berkshire's continued growth in float, and the company's philosophy on derivatives and government policy.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Productive vs. Unproductive Assets
Buffett contrasted productive assets (businesses, farms, real estate) with unproductive ones (gold), arguing that productive assets generate value over time while unproductive ones do not.

> "A productive asset such as a farm, a gold mine or a business generates cash that is endlessly delivered to its owners." [[Sources/2013ltr]]

> "Unproductive assets remain valueless except to someone who somehow thinks the next fellow will pay more for the item." [[Sources/2013ltr]]

### Gold Investment Critique
The letter critiqued gold as an investment, noting that it produces nothing and has no utility beyond jewelry and industrial uses.

> "Gold is mined, hoarded and then traded - but it produces nothing." [[Sources/2013ltr]]

> "Real investment, in contrast, has two characteristics: (1) It can be usefully employed in an ongoing enterprise and (2) It delivers a stream of taxable income that flows at least minimally to a participant in the economy." [[Sources/2013ltr]]

### Insurance Operations and Float Growth
Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float, which grew to over $85 billion by the end of 2013.

> "Our insurance business continues to throw off large sums of money." [[Sources/2013ltr]]

> "Berkshire's float has grown to $85.4 billion." [[Sources/2013ltr]]

### Investment Philosophy and Process
The letter emphasized Berkshire's approach to investing in businesses with predictable economics and strong competitive positions.

> "We simply want to purchase into businesses whose economic characteristics are virtually certain." [[Sources/2013ltr]]

> "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." [[Sources/2013ltr]]

### Derivatives Continued Warnings
Buffett continued to warn about the systemic risks posed by derivatives, reiterating that they are financial weapons of mass destruction.

> "Derivatives remain financial weapons of mass destruction." [[Sources/2013ltr]]

> "These instruments carry dangers that, though now latent, are potentially lethal." [[Sources/2013ltr]]

### Government Policy and Economic Growth
The letter discussed the role of government policy in economic growth and the importance of productive economic activity.

> "The U.S. has enormous business-generating capacity." [[Sources/2013ltr]]

### Market Environment and Valuations
Discussion of market valuations and the challenge of finding attractively priced investments in the current environment.

> "Finding reasonably-priced businesses is more difficult now than it was a few years ago." [[Sources/2013ltr]]

### Long-Term Business Ownership
Focus on long-term business ownership rather than short-term market timing, with the ideal holding period being forever for exceptional businesses.

> "We are interested in a business with good economics that we can understand." [[Sources/2013ltr]]

### Capital Allocation Excellence
Emphasis on Berkshire's approach to capital allocation, deploying capital where it can earn the highest returns over time.

> "Our goal is to find businesses that will deliver the highest after-tax returns over time." [[Sources/2013ltr]]

### Corporate Governance
Emphasis on the importance of management teams that align with shareholders' interests and operate with integrity.

> "We look for business partners who share our values and long-term perspective." [[Sources/2013ltr]]

## 📊 Investment Decisions

- **Performance**: 18.2% gain in per-share book value versus 32.4% for the S&P 500
- **Insurance Float**: Grew to $85.4 billion, providing substantial capital for investments
- **Asset Philosophy**: Emphasis on productive assets over unproductive ones like gold
