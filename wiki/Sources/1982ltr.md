# 1982 Annual Letter Summary

*Report on Berkshire Hathaway's performance and philosophy as of March 3, 1983 (covering 1982 results)*

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Non-Reported Ownership Earnings Framework
- Formal renaming: "Non-Reported Ownership Earnings" to distinguish from accounting treatment
- Quantitative confirmation: "our share of undistributed 1982 operating earnings of this group came to well over $40 million" — greater than total reported earnings ($14M dividends)
- **Source**: [[1982ltr.md#p1]]

### Economic vs. Accounting Earnings
- Foundational distinction: "economic earnings that includes all undistributed earnings, regardless of ownership percentage" vs. "accounting earnings"
- Philosophical principle: "value to all owners... is determined by the effectiveness with which those earnings are used - and not by the size of one’s ownership percentage"
- **Source**: [[1982ltr.md#p1]]

### Insurance Industry Structural Analysis
- First structural analysis: "insurance industry operates under the competitive sword of substantial overcapacity" and sells a "relatively undifferentiated commodity-type product"
- Historical shift: "That day is gone" — end of quasi-administered pricing system; new capacity uses price as prime weapon
- **Source**: [[1982ltr.md#p5]]

### Equity Issuance Philosophy
- Foundational rule: "we will not issue shares unless we receive as much intrinsic business value as we give"
- Three solutions to avoid value destruction: (1) business-value-for-business-value merger, (2) stock selling at or above intrinsic value, (3) post-merger share repurchase
- **Source**: [[1982ltr.md#p6]]

## 💬 Notable Quotes

> "We prefer a concept of 'economic' earnings that includes all undistributed earnings, regardless of ownership percentage." — [[1982ltr.md#p1]]

> "The insurance industry operates under the competitive sword of substantial overcapacity." — [[1982ltr.md#p5]]

> "We will not issue shares unless we receive as much intrinsic business value as we give." — [[1982ltr.md#p6]]


## 📊 Investment Decisions

### GEICO Corporation
- Massive market appreciation: "$79 million of our $208 million gain is attributable to an increased market price for GEICO"
- Strategic rationale: "magnificent illustration of the high-profit exception... a company with a wide and sustainable cost advantage"
- **Source**: [[1982ltr.md#p3]]

### Washington Post & Nostalgia Principle
- Investment lesson: "nostalgia should be weighted heavily in stock selection" — first commercial connections at ages 13 (Washington Post) and 20 (GEICO)
- **Source**: [[1982ltr.md#p4]]
