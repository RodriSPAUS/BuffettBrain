# 2004 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 10.5% gain in per-share book value in 2004, slightly underperforming the S&P 500's 10.9% gain. The letter focused on Berkshire's insurance operations, the growth of float, and various business operations. Buffett also discussed the company's investments and macroeconomic views on trade deficits and currency.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Insurance Operations and Float Growth
Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float, which grew to over $46 billion by the end of 2004. The company achieved a $1.55 billion pre-tax underwriting profit, meaning they were actually paid for holding money.

### MidAmerican Energy Holdings
Berkshire owns an 80.5% (fully diluted) interest in MidAmerican Energy Holdings, which operates various utility businesses including Yorkshire Electricity, Northern Electric, and pipeline operations.

### Business Performance and Capital Allocation
The letter emphasized Berkshire's approach to capital allocation and the performance of various business units. Berkshire's long-term performance versus the S&P remains important, as shareholders can buy the S&P through an index fund at very low cost.

### Foreign Currency Positions
Berkshire had approximately $21.4 billion in foreign exchange contracts at year-end, reflecting concerns about U.S. trade deficits and the dollar's long-term value.

### Investment Philosophy and Holdings
Discussion of Berkshire's investment approach, focusing on fractional ownership in businesses rather than stock trading. Major holdings include American Express, Coca-Cola, Gillette, and Wells Fargo.

### Long-Term Investment Approach
Berkshire's approach remains focused on long-term business ownership rather than short-term market timing, with the ideal holding period being forever for exceptional businesses.

### Corporate Governance and Management Quality
Importance of investing in companies with managers who align with shareholders' interests and operate with integrity.

### Economic Reality vs. Market Fluctuations
Focus on the underlying economics of businesses rather than being distracted by market volatility and short-term price movements.
