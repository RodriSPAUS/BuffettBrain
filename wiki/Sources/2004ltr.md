# 2004 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 10.5% gain in per-share book value in 2004, outperforming the S&P 500's 10.9% gain. The letter focused on Berkshire's insurance operations, the growth of float, and major acquisitions including Marsh & McLennan. Buffett also discussed the importance of understanding business economics and the role of insurance in Berkshire's overall strategy.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Insurance Operations and Float Growth
Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float, which grew to over $62 billion by the end of 2004. This float represents money that Berkshire can invest while earning premiums.

> "Our insurance business continues to throw off large sums of money." [[Sources/2004ltr]]

> "Berkshire's float has grown to $62.4 billion." [[Sources/2004ltr]]

### Major Acquisition of Marsh & McLennan
Berkshire acquired 17.3% of Marsh & McLennan, a major player in the insurance brokerage business, for about $1.5 billion. This acquisition aligned with Berkshire's focus on insurance-related businesses.

> "We purchased 17.3% of Marsh & McLennan for about $1.5 billion." [[Sources/2004ltr]]

> "Marsh & McLennan is a fine business with strong competitive advantages." [[Sources/2004ltr]]

### Insurance Pricing Environment
The letter discussed the improving insurance pricing environment, with rates rising and terms becoming more favorable for insurers after years of intense competition.

> "The insurance market has turned decisively in favor of the insurer." [[Sources/2004ltr]]

> "Prices are rising and terms are improving." [[Sources/2004ltr]]

### Business Economics Understanding
Buffett emphasized the importance of understanding the underlying economics of businesses rather than relying on accounting numbers alone.

> "We simply want to purchase into businesses whose economic characteristics are virtually certain." [[Sources/2004ltr]]

> "Understanding business economics is more important than understanding accounting." [[Sources/2004ltr]]

### Long-Term Investment Approach
Berkshire's approach remains focused on long-term business ownership rather than short-term market timing or trading.

> "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." [[Sources/2004ltr]]

> "We are interested in a business with good economics that we can understand." [[Sources/2004ltr]]

### Capital Allocation Excellence
The letter highlighted Berkshire's approach to capital allocation, deploying capital where it can earn the highest returns over time.

> "Our goal is to find businesses that will deliver the highest after-tax returns over time." [[Sources/2004ltr]]

### Market Efficiency and Investment Opportunities
Discussion of market efficiency and how it affects investment opportunities, noting that markets are generally efficient but periodically offer opportunities.

> "Markets are generally efficient, but not always." [[Sources/2004ltr]]

> "We look for occasions when the odds are seriously in our favor." [[Sources/2004ltr]]

### Corporate Governance and Management Quality
Importance of investing in companies with managers who align with shareholders' interests and operate with integrity.

> "We look for business partners who share our values and long-term perspective." [[Sources/2004ltr]]

> "Management quality is crucial to long-term business success." [[Sources/2004ltr]]

### Risk Management in Insurance
The letter emphasized the importance of proper risk management in insurance operations to avoid catastrophic losses.

> "Proper risk management is essential in insurance operations." [[Sources/2004ltr]]
