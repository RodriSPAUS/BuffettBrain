# 1993 Annual Letter Summary

*Report on Berkshire Hathaway's performance and philosophy as of February 26, 1994 (covering 1993 results)*

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Intrinsic Value Growth Constraint Reinforced
- Structural definition: "What we have going for us is a growing collection of good-sized operating businesses that possess economic characteristics ranging from good to terrific, run by managers whose performance ranges from terrific to terrific"
- Quantitative insight: "Our look-through earnings need to increase at about 15% annually if our intrinsic value is to grow at that rate"
- **Source**: [[1993ltr.md#p0]]

### Look-Through Earnings Framework Operationalized
- Structural insight: "Look-through earnings consist of: (1) the operating earnings reported... plus; (2) the retained operating earnings of major investees... less; (3) an allowance for the tax that would be paid by Berkshire"
- Behavioral insight: "We have told you that we expect the undistributed, hypothetically-taxed earnings of our investees to produce at least equivalent gains in Berkshire's intrinsic value"
- **Source**: [[1993ltr.md#p1]]

### Super-Cat Insurance Competitive Advantage Codified
- Structural analysis: "Berkshire is ideally positioned to write super-cat policies. In Ajit Jain, we have by far the best manager in this business"
- Economic insight: "Companies writing these policies need enormous capital, and our net worth is ten to twenty times larger than that of our main competitors"
- **Source**: [[1993ltr.md#p2]]

### Acquisition Behavior Taxonomy Refined
- Structural insight: "At Berkshire, we have no view of the future that dictates what businesses or industries we will enter. Indeed, we think it's usually poison for a corporate giant's shareholders if it embarks upon new ventures pursuant to some grand vision"
- Behavioral contrast: "We prefer instead to focus on the economic characteristics of businesses that we wish to own and the personal characteristics of managers with whom we wish to associate"
- **Source**: [[1993ltr.md#p0]]

## 📊 Investment Decisions

### Dexter Shoe Acquisition Strategy
- Formalized principle: "The acquisition was Dexter Shoe of Dexter, Maine, which manufactures popular-priced men's and women's shoes"
- Structural insight: "Harold and Peter (who were not interested in cash) made a sound decision in exchanging their Dexter stock for shares of Berkshire"
- **Source**: [[1993ltr.md#p0]]

### Tax-Efficient Ownership Transfer Framework
- Formalized principle: "They incurred no tax on this exchange and now own a security that can be easily used for charitable or personal gifts, or that can be converted to cash in amounts, and at times, of their own choosing"
- Behavioral insight: "Though Harold and Peter now have non-controlling shares in Berkshire, rather than controlling shares in Dexter, they know they will be treated as partners and that we will follow owner-oriented practices"
- **Source**: [[1993ltr.md#p0]]
