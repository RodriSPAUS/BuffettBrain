# 1995 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved an exceptional gain of $5.3 billion (45.0%) in net worth during 1995, with per-share book value growing by 43.1%. The letter highlights three major acquisitions: Helzberg's Diamond Shops, R.C. Willey Home Furnishings, and the remaining 50% of GEICO, demonstrating Berkshire's strategy of seeking businesses with excellent economics and outstanding management.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Exceptional Performance and Growth
Berkshire's net worth increased by $5.3 billion (45.0%) in 1995, while per-share book value grew by 43.1%. Over 31 years, per-share book value has grown from $19 to $14,426 at a rate of 23.6% annually. 

> "To paraphrase President Kennedy, a rising tide lifts all yachts." [[Sources/1995ltr]]

### Strategic Acquisitions
Three major acquisitions were completed in 1995, each fitting Berkshire's criteria of excellent economics and outstanding management:

**Helzberg's Diamond Shops**: A family business founded in 1915 that grew from $10 million in sales (1974) to $282 million (1994), with 134 stores in 23 states. Acquired via tax-free exchange of stock.

**R.C. Willey Home Furnishings**: Built from $250,000 in sales (1954) to $257 million (1995), controlling over 50% of the furniture business in Utah.

**GEICO Corporation**: The seventh largest auto insurer in the US with ~3.7 million cars insured, acquired for $2.3 billion to purchase the remaining 50% stake. Buffett's association with GEICO began in 1951.

> "We believe most deals do damage to the shareholders of the acquiring company. Too often, the words from HMS Pinafore apply: 'Things are seldom what they seem, skim milk masquerades as cream.'" [[Sources/1995ltr]]

### Insurance Excellence and Float Generation
Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float at a negative cost. The float grew to $3.6 billion with negative cost, meaning Berkshire earned money on the float. The super-cat business showed large profits due to the lack of major catastrophes.

### Investment Philosophy and Economic Moats
Buffett emphasizes the importance of investing in economic castles protected by unbreachable moats. The GEICO acquisition exemplified this philosophy, with Buffett noting that "GEICO's moat widened in 1995" thanks to Tony Nicely's management team.

> "In business, I look for economic castles protected by unbreachable 'moats.'" [[Sources/1995ltr]]

> "Thanks to Tony and his management team, GEICO's moat widened in 1995." [[Sources/1995ltr]]

### Management Approach and Decentralization
Berkshire maintains a minimal headquarters staff (only 12 people) while granting autonomy to subsidiary managers. This approach allows managers to operate as owners without corporate interference.

### Corporate Governance and Share Structure
The letter discusses a proposed recapitalization to create two classes of stock (Class A and Class B), with Class B shares intended to deter the creation of expense-laden unit trusts while maintaining Berkshire's shareholder-friendly culture.

## 💰 Investment Decisions

- **Helzberg's Diamond Shops**: Acquired for business with excellent economics and outstanding management
- **R.C. Willey Home Furnishings**: Furniture retailer with dominant position in Utah market
- **GEICO Corporation**: Remaining 50% acquired for $2.3 billion, making it a wholly-owned subsidiary
- Maintained major equity positions in Coca-Cola ($7.4B), American Express ($2.0B), Gillette ($2.5B)
