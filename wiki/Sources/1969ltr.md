# 1969 Letters Summary

`Raw/1969ltr.md` holds five documents spanning the partnership's best year
and its end: the **January 22, 1969 annual letter** (full-year 1968 —
the best relative result in the partnership's history), the **May 29, 1969
letter** (the retirement announcement), the **October 9, 1969 letter**
(recommending [[People/BillRuane]], and a stocks-vs-bonds argument that
inverts everything Buffett had argued for a decade), the **December 5, 1969
letter** (detail on Berkshire Hathaway and Diversified Retailing for
departing partners), and the **December 26, 1969 letter** (the distribution
finalized, plus a long partner Q&A). Together they are the record of
Buffett Partnership, Ltd. closing itself down at its own peak, by its own
choice.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### 1968 — "everyone makes mistakes," meaning his own pessimism

Buffett had expected 1968 to be difficult; instead the partnership set its
best-ever mark:

> "Everyone makes mistakes." [[Sources/1969ltr]]

> "We established a new mark at plus 58.8% versus an overall plus 7.7 % for the Dow, including dividends which would have been received through ownership of the Average throughout the year." [[Sources/1969ltr]]

Limited partners: +45.6%. He refuses to let the number stand as a new normal:

> "This result should be treated as a freak like picking up thirteen spades in a bridge game." [[Sources/1969ltr]]

Compounded 1957–1968: Dow 185.7%, partnership 2610.6%, limited partners
1403.5% (annual rates 9.1% / 31.6% / 25.3% — the final complete-year table
this corpus contains; the December letters put 1969 itself at approximately
6% for the year, with no equivalent formal table published).

### Valuing a control position against a moving market price

Berkshire Hathaway's carried value and its quoted price diverge sharply, and
Buffett states outright that this is intentional, not an oversight:

> "We valued B-H at 25 at yearend 1967 when the market was about 20 and 31 at yearend 1968 when the market was about 37." [[Sources/1969ltr]] [[Concepts/IntrinsicValue]]

The line that follows is the one later Berkshire letters make famous:

> "We would have done the same thing if the markets had been 15 and 50 respectively. (“Price is what you pay. value is what you get”)." [[Sources/1969ltr]]

National Indemnity and Associated Cotton Shops both earned about 20% on
capital employed in 1968 — a figure Buffett notes only 37 of the Fortune 500
matched in 1967, "outshin[ing]" IBM, General Electric, General Motors and
DuPont on that specific measure.

### The retirement announcement

The May 29 letter states, without preamble, the decision that ends the
partnership:

> "I intend to give all limited partners the required formal notice of my intention to retire." [[Sources/1969ltr]]

The reasoning is personal, not investment-driven: a wish to stop being "on
stage," publicly competitive, before it consumes every year of his life —

> "The only way to slow down is to stop." [[Sources/1969ltr]]

He owns the 1969 work-out losses as a forecasting error, not bad luck —
misjudging how fast a government trend would move:

> "Our experience in workouts this year has been atrocious - during this period I have felt like the bird that inadvertently flew into the middle of a badminton game." [[Sources/1969ltr]]

> "Documenting one's boners is unpleasant business." [[Sources/1969ltr]] [[Principles/Fidelity]]

### Recommending Bill Ruane, and reversing a decade of advice

Buffett names Bill Ruane, met in Ben Graham's class at Columbia in 1951, as
the manager he entrusts with money for his own family's future and
recommends to every partner without an alternative:

> "This letter - to tell you something of Bill Ruane, the money manager within my knowledge who ranks the highest when combining the factors of integrity, ability and continued availability to all partners." [[Sources/1969ltr]] [[People/BillRuane]]

> "He will be available to any partner, so that no minimum size for accounts will cause any of you a problem." [[Sources/1969ltr]]

He also delivers, for the first time in the corpus, an argument that
tax-free bonds may now rival equities — inverting the entire premise the
partnership was built on:

> "The rather startling conclusion is that under today's historically unusual conditions, passive investment in tax-free bonds is likely to be fully the equivalent of expectations from professionally managed money in stocks, and only modestly inferior to extremely well-managed equity money." [[Sources/1969ltr]]

The reasoning leans on Graham directly:

> "As Ben Graham said: 'In the long run, the market is a weighing machine - in the short run, a voting machine.'" [[Sources/1969ltr]] [[People/BenGraham]]

And on his own refusal to force a result he no longer believes in:

> "I am not attuned to this market environment and I don't want to spoil a decent record by trying to play a game I don't understand just so I can go out a hero." [[Sources/1969ltr]] [[Concepts/CircleOfCompetence]]

### Winding down — cash now, stock for those who want it

The December letters fix the mechanics: partners receive a cash distribution
of at least 64% of January 1, 1969 capital, plus their proportional shares
of Berkshire Hathaway and Diversified Retailing stock for those who elect to
keep them rather than take cash. Both companies are described candidly, still
first-class businesses run by men in their sixties, still paying no dividends
because the capital is needed inside the business. Asked whether partners
should hold onto their B-H or DRC shares, Buffett answers only for himself:

> "All I can say is that I'm going to do so and I plan to buy more." [[Sources/1969ltr]]

DRC's Hochschild, Kohn & Co. subsidiary is sold to Supermarkets General
during this period, leaving DRC holding Associated Retail Stores and cash for
reinvestment.

## 📊 Investment Decisions

- **Partnership — liquidation begun.** Retirement announced May 29, 1969; formal notice given; distributions began January 1970 (at least 64% cash, plus proportional Berkshire Hathaway / Diversified Retailing shares for those electing to keep them).
- **Bill Ruane — recommended successor manager**, available to all partners without a minimum account size. See [[People/BillRuane]].
- **Hochschild, Kohn & Co. — sold** by Diversified Retailing Company to Supermarkets General Corp. in December 1969.
- **Results (full year 1968):** partnership +58.8%, limited partners +45.6%, against the Dow's +7.7% — the widest margin in the partnership's history. Compounded 1957–68: 2610.6% / 1403.5% vs 185.7%.
- **Results (1969, partial):** work-outs atrocious; overall result for the year put at approximately 6% — no formal complete-year table published in this corpus for 1969 itself.
- **Structure:** net assets $104,429,431 entering 1969 (from $105,100 at the May 1956 start; from $7,178,500 at the 1962 consolidation).
