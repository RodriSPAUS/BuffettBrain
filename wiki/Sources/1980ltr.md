# 1980 Annual Letter Summary

*Covering 1980: operating earnings improved to $41.9 million from $36.0 million in 1979, but return on beginning equity capital fell to 17.8% from 18.6%. The letter introduces the concept of non-controlled ownership earnings and provides deep insights into the effects of inflation on corporate returns and the challenges facing the insurance industry.*

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Non-Controlled Ownership Earnings

- Buffett explains the three categories of accounting for ownership in other companies: over 50% (full consolidation), 20-50% (equity method), and under 20% (dividends only).
- Berkshire's concentration in insurance leads to large holdings in the third category (under 20% owned) where only dividends are recorded, not underlying earnings.
- "Our own analysis of earnings reality differs somewhat from generally accepted accounting principles, particularly when those principles must be applied in a world of high and uncertain rates of inflation."
- The value of retained earnings is determined by their use and subsequent earnings produced, not by whether Berkshire owns 100%, 50%, 20% or 1% of the business.

### The Impact of Inflation on Investment Returns

- "High rates of inflation create a tax on capital that makes much corporate investment unwise - at least if measured by the criterion of a positive real investment return to owners."
- Example: in a world of 12% inflation, a business earning 20% on equity and distributing it all to individuals in the 50% tax bracket is actually destroying their real capital.
- "The average tax-paying investor is now running up a down escalator whose pace has accelerated to the point where his upward progress is nil."
- The inflation tax is not limited by reported income, unlike explicit income taxes.

### Corporate Performance vs. Owner Returns

- "Earnings reported in corporate financial statements are no longer the dominant variable that determines whether there are any real earnings for you, the owner."
- Using a hamburger analogy: if you forego ten hamburgers to purchase an investment, receive dividends that buy two hamburgers, and upon sale receive proceeds that buy eight hamburgers, you have had no real income.
- "Only gains in purchasing power represent real earnings on investment."

### GEICO Corporation Analysis

- Berkshire's largest non-controlled holding: 7.2 million shares of GEICO Corp., equal to about a 33% equity interest.
- At the present dividend rate, reported earnings from GEICO amount to a little over $3 million annually, but Buffett estimates their share of earning power is on the order of $20 million annually.
- "Thus, undistributed earnings applicable to this holding alone may amount to 40% of total reported operating earnings of Berkshire."
- Praises GEICO management's skill in capital allocation and their successful stock repurchase program.

## 💬 Notable Quotes

> "The value to Berkshire Hathaway of retained earnings is not determined by whether we own 100%, 50%, 20% or 1% of the businesses in which they reside. Rather, the value of those retained earnings is determined by the use to which they are put and the subsequent level of earnings produced by that usage."

> "If a tree grows in a forest partially owned by us, but we don't record the growth in our financial statements, we still own part of the tree."

> "High rates of inflation create a tax on capital that makes much corporate investment unwise - at least if measured by the criterion of a positive real investment return to owners."

> "You may feel richer, but you won't eat richer."

> "At present inflation rates, we believe individual owners in
medium or high tax brackets (as distinguished from tax-free
entities such as pension funds, eleemosynary institutions, etc.)
should expect no real long-term return from the average American
corporation, even though these individuals reinvest"

## 📊 Investment Decisions

- Significant holdings in non-controlled companies, particularly in the insurance sector and other businesses with strong fundamentals
- Detailed breakdown of Berkshire's holdings in companies like GEICO (7.2M shares, ~33% interest), Affiliated Publications, Alcoa, General Foods, and others
- Financing: In August, Berkshire sold $60 million of 12 3/4% notes due August 1, 2005, with a sinking fund to begin in 1991
- Disposition of Illinois National Bank and Trust of Rockford through an exchange of shares
