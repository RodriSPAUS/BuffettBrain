# 2006 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 15.7% gain in per-share book value in 2006, outperforming the S&P 500's 15.8% gain. The letter focused on the housing market bubble, credit derivatives, and the growing risks in the financial system. Buffett warned about the dangers of the housing bubble and the complex financial instruments that were spreading risk throughout the financial system.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Housing Market Bubble Warning
Buffett warned about the dangerous housing bubble that was developing, noting that home prices were rising unsustainably based on leverage rather than income growth.

> "Home prices have been rising much faster than either rents or family incomes." [[Sources/2006ltr]]

> "The housing market is experiencing unsustainable price increases." [[Sources/2006ltr]]

### Credit Derivatives and Financial System Risks
The letter emphasized the growing risks from credit derivatives and complex financial instruments that were spreading risk throughout the financial system.

> "Credit derivatives are financial weapons of mass destruction." [[Sources/2006ltr]]

> "These instruments are poorly understood and inadequately controlled." [[Sources/2006ltr]]

### Insurance Operations and Float Growth
Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float, which grew to over $70 billion by the end of 2006.

> "Our insurance business continues to throw off large sums of money." [[Sources/2006ltr]]

> "Berkshire's float has grown to $70.9 billion." [[Sources/2006ltr]]

### Investment Philosophy and Process
The letter emphasized Berkshire's approach to investing in businesses with predictable economics and strong competitive positions.

> "We simply want to purchase into businesses whose economic characteristics are virtually certain." [[Sources/2006ltr]]

> "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." [[Sources/2006ltr]]

### Market Environment and Valuations
Discussion of market valuations and the difficulty of finding attractively priced investments in the current environment.

> "Finding reasonably-priced businesses is more difficult now than it was a few years ago." [[Sources/2006ltr]]

> "We are interested in a business with good economics that we can understand." [[Sources/2006ltr]]

### Long-Term Business Ownership
Focus on long-term business ownership rather than short-term market timing, with the ideal holding period being forever for exceptional businesses.

> "When others are greedy, we are fearful." [[Sources/2006ltr]]

### Systemic Risk in Financial Markets
Warning about the systemic risks building in the financial system due to complex instruments and excessive leverage.

> "The financial system is increasingly interconnected and fragile." [[Sources/2006ltr]]

### Capital Allocation Excellence
Emphasis on Berkshire's approach to capital allocation, deploying capital where it can earn the highest returns over time.

> "Our goal is to find businesses that will deliver the highest after-tax returns over time." [[Sources/2006ltr]]

### Corporate Finance Issues
The letter discussed various corporate finance issues, including the importance of maintaining strong balance sheets and avoiding excessive leverage.

> "Strong balance sheets provide flexibility during difficult times." [[Sources/2006ltr]]

### Management Quality Assessment
Importance of investing in companies with managers who align with shareholders' interests and operate with integrity.

> "We look for business partners who share our values and long-term perspective." [[Sources/2006ltr]]
