# 2006 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved an 18.4% gain in per-share book value in 2006, significantly outperforming the S&P 500's 15.8% gain. The letter focused on major acquisitions including ISCAR and Equitas retroactive reinsurance contract, exceptional performance at GEICO, and the company's approach to risk management and capital allocation.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Major Acquisitions in 2006
Berkshire completed several significant acquisitions including ISCAR, an Israeli cutting-tool company, and TTI, a distributor of electronic components. The Equitas retroactive reinsurance contract was a major transaction boosting float by $7 billion.

### Insurance Operations and Float Growth
Berkshire's insurance operations had an exceptional year, with float reaching $50.9 billion. The company's underwriting profits were $3.84 billion, significantly improved from the previous year.

### GEICO Performance and Productivity Gains
GEICO showed extraordinary performance with productivity gains of 47% between 2003-2006. The company's policies increased from 5.7 million to 8.1 million while employees decreased by 3.5%.

### Retroactive Reinsurance and Equitas Deal
Detailed discussion of the $7.12 billion retroactive reinsurance contract with Equitas, providing coverage for future claims up to $13.9 billion. This contract significantly boosted float and exemplifies Berkshire's expertise in complex insurance arrangements.

### ISCAR Acquisition and International Expansion
The acquisition of 80% of ISCAR, an Israeli cutting-tool company, marked Berkshire's expansion into international acquisitions with global operations spanning 61 countries.

### Risk Management and Super-Cat Insurance
Discussion of catastrophic insurance risks and climate change implications for the insurance industry, including lessons from hurricane seasons of 2004-2005.

### Management Philosophy and Succession Planning
Emphasis on finding exceptional managers and succession planning, with three outstanding candidates identified to replace Buffett as CEO.

### Business Quality and Competitive Moats
Importance of continuously widening competitive moats through customer satisfaction, cost reduction, and product improvements.
