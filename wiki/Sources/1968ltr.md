# 1968 Annual Letter Summary

`Raw/1968ltr.md` holds two documents: the **January 24, 1968 annual letter**
(full-year **1967** results — filed here rather than under a "1967" filename
because the archive names each file for the year the letters were *sent*, not
the year reported; see the data note on [[Sources/1966ltr]]) and the **July
11, 1968 mid-year letter**. 1967 is the year the partnership's controlled
companies acquire National Indemnity and Associated Cotton Shops, and the
year work-outs — historically the steadiest category — post the worst result
in the partnership's history, against a market Buffett describes as
"speculative bonbons."

## 🔑 Key Themes

### 1967 by the numbers — good, but no longer exceptional by the market's own standard

Partnership +35.9%, limited partners +28.4%, against the Dow's +19.0%.
Compounded 1957–1967: Dow 165.3%, partnership 1606.9%, limited partners
932.6% (annual rates 9.3% / 29.4% / 23.6%). Realized taxable income of
$27,376,667 from selling long-held positions is explicitly separated from
performance:

> "Which has nothing to do with 1967 performance but should give all of you a feeling of vigorous participation in The Great Society on April 15th." [[Sources/1968ltr]]

The wider environment had shifted: Buffett estimates at least 95% of common-
stock investment companies beat the Dow in 1967, "in many cases by very
substantial amounts" —

> "It was a year when profits achieved were in inverse proportion to age - and I am in the geriatric ward, philosophically." [[Sources/1968ltr]]

Ben Graham is quoted directly on the mood of the market: "Speculation is
neither illegal, immoral nor fattening (financially)." [[People/BenGraham]]

### Work-outs — the worst year in the partnership's history

The category built for predictability delivered almost nothing:

> "The streets were filled with upset applecarts - our applecarts - during 1967." [[Sources/1968ltr]]

On an average investment of $17,246,879, the overall gain was $153,273 —
"0.89 of 1%" — the weakest result Buffett can recall in the category.

### Controls — two new businesses, both praised without reservation

Through the two controlled companies, Diversified Retailing Company and
Berkshire Hathaway, the partnership acquired Associated Cotton Shops and
National Indemnity Company (with National Fire & Marine):

> "These acquisitions couldn't be more gratifying." [[Sources/1968ltr]]

Ben Rosner (Associated Cotton Shops) and Jack Ringwalt (National Indemnity)
are named as the executives who sold and continued running their businesses.
Buffett gives, for the first time, an explicit reason for holding controlled
operating businesses at a *lower* expected return than an aggressive
securities operation could earn:

> "As I have mentioned before, we cannot make the same sort of money out of permanent ownership of controlled businesses that can be made from buying and reselling such businesses, or from skilled investment in marketable securities." [[Sources/1968ltr]]

The "moderated investment objectives" referenced repeatedly this year come
from an October 9, 1967 letter that is not itself present in this corpus'
`Raw/` files — its content is known here only through this letter's and
1969's references to it.

### Generals — one large idea hits its own limit and gets trimmed back

Generals-Relatively Undervalued gained about 72% on its average investment,
substantially the product of one position that had "hit our 40% limit"
(Buffett's own concentration ceiling) after outperforming since 1964; by this
letter, "Our holdings of this security have been very substantially reduced
and we have nothing in this group remotely approaching the size or potential
which formerly existed in this investment." Generals-Private Owner produced
$1,297,215 on $5,141,710 average investment.

### Is this the beginning of the end?

Some withdrawing partners, and some who stayed, asked whether the reduced
objectives meant the partnership was winding down:

> "I was also asked whether this was an initial stage in the phasing out of the partnership." [[Sources/1968ltr]]

> "The answer to this is, 'Definitely, no.'" [[Sources/1968ltr]]

### Mid-year — Diversified Retailing goes semi-public, and a warning about the market's mood

H1 1968: Dow +0.9%; partnership +16% excluding the revaluation of controlled
companies (about a third of assets at the start of the year). The controlled
businesses' managers are named as a group for the first time: Ken Chace
(Berkshire Hathaway), Louis Kohn (Hochschild Kohn), Jack Ringwalt (National
Indemnity), Ben Rosner (Associated Cotton Shops). Diversified Retailing
Company issued its first published annual report after selling debentures to
about 1,000 investors — public from a creditor's standpoint, private in
ownership (three stockholders, BPL at 80%).

"The Present Environment" section is a sharp, named critique of the era's
accounting and promotional practices:

> "To create the proper illusions, it frequently requires accounting distortions (one particularly progressive entrepreneur told me he believed in 'bold, imaginative accounting'), tricks of capitalization and camouflage of the true nature of the operating businesses involved." [[Sources/1968ltr]]

He recommends an article's "'The Emperor Has No Clothes' approach" as at odds
with prevailing opinion, and points readers to a book: "Finally, for a
magnificent account of the current financial scene, you should hurry out and
get a copy of 'The Money Game' by Adam Smith." Buffett is candid that the
partnership itself benefited indirectly from the mania — cheap stocks the
partnership owned were revalued upward by speculative demand — while warning
that this same appetite "tends to substantially diminish the number of
fundamentally attractive investments which remain."

## 📊 Investment Decisions

- **National Indemnity Company (with National Fire & Marine) — acquired**, through Berkshire Hathaway; Jack Ringwalt continues running it.
- **Associated Cotton Shops — acquired**, through Diversified Retailing Company; Ben Rosner continues running it.
- **A large Generals-Relatively Undervalued position — substantially trimmed** after hitting the 40% concentration limit and outperforming since 1964.
- **Results (full year 1967):** partnership +35.9%, limited partners +28.4%, against the Dow's +19.0%. Compounded 1957–67: 1606.9% / 932.6% vs 165.3%.
- **Results (H1 1968):** partnership +16% (excluding controlled-company revaluation) against the Dow's +0.9%.
- **Structure:** net assets $68,108,088 at the start of 1968; about $1,600,000 of partner capital withdrew at yearend, mostly attributed to the reduced objectives announced in October 1967.
