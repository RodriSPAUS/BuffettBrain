# 1970 Letter Summary

`Raw/1970ltr.md` holds a single document: the **February 25, 1970 letter**,
the last one in this corpus. It is not a performance letter at all — the
partnership no longer holds marketable securities to report on — but a
long, careful education in tax-free municipal bonds, written because
partners had just received an unusually large cash distribution and needed
somewhere to put it. It is the method's final demonstration on a subject
that is, for once, not stocks: read only what you understand, and say
plainly where your competence stops.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### A closing service, explicitly bounded in time

Buffett frames the whole letter as a one-time act of help, not a new
advisory relationship:

> "This letter will attempt to provide a very elementary education regarding tax-exempt bonds with emphasis on the types and maturities of bonds which we expect to help partners in purchasing next month." [[Sources/1970ltr]]

> "I have no desire to be in the investment counseling business, directly or indirectly, and will not be available for discussion of financial matters after March 31st." [[Sources/1970ltr]]

> "After March 31st, I don't expect to be around the office for several months." [[Sources/1970ltr]]

### Circle of competence, stated as a rule for bonds too

Buffett is explicit that his help is bounded by what he actually understands,
not by politeness:

> "If you disagree with me as to conclusions regarding types of bonds or maturities (and you would have been right and I would have been wrong if you had disagreed with me on the latter point either one or two years ago), you may well be correct, but we cannot be of assistance to you in the purchase of bonds outside our area." [[Sources/1970ltr]] [[Concepts/CircleOfCompetence]]

He will not touch general-obligation bonds of large cities at all:

> "I don't have the faintest idea how to analyze a New York City, Chicago, Philadelphia, etc." [[Sources/1970ltr]] [[Concepts/CircleOfCompetence]]

And the same discipline that governed stock selection is restated almost
word for word for bonds:

> "If I can't understand something, I tend to forget it." [[Sources/1970ltr]]

### Callable bonds — a one-sided contract, named as such

The letter's sharpest passage is a warning against bonds callable at the
issuer's convenience but not the holder's:

> "It is amazing to me to see people buy bonds which are due in forty years, but where the issuer has the right to call the bonds at a tiny premium in five or ten years." [[Sources/1970ltr]]

> "To buy a bond of the Los Angeles Department of Water and Power at 100 to come due at 100 in 1999 or to come due at 104 in 1974, depending on which is to the advantage of the issuer and to your disadvantage, is the height of foolishness when comparable yields are available on similar credits without such an unfair contract." [[Sources/1970ltr]]

He notes, pointedly, that most Nebraska bonds themselves carry these unfair
call terms without paying any extra yield for the privilege they hand the
issuer.

### The bond market described as illiquid by nature, not by accident

> "Sometimes the tax-free bond market has more similarities to real estate than to stocks." [[Sources/1970ltr]]

Hundreds of thousands of small, non-interchangeable issues, thin trading,
and wide bid-ask spreads (sometimes 15%) are treated as a structural fact of
this market, not a defect to be timed around — matching, on a different
asset class, the same patient tolerance for illiquidity that shaped
[[Cases/CommonwealthTrust]] and [[Cases/DempsterMill]] years earlier.

### Where he will and will not go

The stated areas of purchase are narrow and specific: large revenue-producing
public entities (toll roads, power districts), Industrial Development
Authority bonds backed by top-grade corporate lessees, and Housing Authority
issues (effectively U.S.-government-guaranteed, AAA) where no other credit
judgment is needed. Maturities: ten to twenty-five years, chosen because the
yield curve was "in the high positive range" and, on a coin-flip view of
future rates, longer non-callable bonds offered the better odds.

## 📊 Investment Decisions

- **No securities bought or sold by the partnership itself** — this letter is Buffett personally helping former partners deploy their own distributed cash, at no cost and with an explicit end date (March 31, 1970).
- **Bond selection criteria stated:** large revenue-producing public entities, Industrial Development Authority bonds with strong corporate lessees, and Housing Authority (AAA) issues; ten-to-twenty-five year maturities; non-callable or deep-discount structures preferred; large-city general-obligation credits explicitly declined as outside his competence.
- **Structure:** the last letter in the corpus; no performance table, net asset figure, or partnership results — the wind-down is administrative from this point.
