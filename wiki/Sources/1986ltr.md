# 1986 Annual Letter Summary

*Report on Berkshire Hathaway's performance and philosophy as of February 27, 1987 (covering 1986 results)*

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Managerial Excellence as Primary Value Driver
- Formalized principle: "book value has served for more than a decade as a reasonable if somewhat conservative proxy for business value" because "key managers... improved the earnings of their businesses dramatically while... utilizing little additional capital"
- Core insight: "This accomplishment builds economic value, or 'Goodwill,' that does not show up in the net worth figure on our balance sheet"
- **Source**: [[1986ltr.md#p0]]

### Capital Allocation Framework
- Two core jobs: "attract and keep outstanding managers" and "allocation of capital"
- Structural constraint: "three factors make [capital allocation] considerably more important... we earn more money than average; we retain all that we earn; and... operations require little incremental capital to remain competitive"
- **Source**: [[1986ltr.md#p1]]

### Acquisition Criteria Codification
- Formalized six-point framework: (1) large purchases ($10M+ after-tax), (2) consistent earning power, (3) good ROE with little/no debt, (4) management in place, (5) simple businesses, (6) offering price
- Strategic evolution: "threshold for minimum annual after-tax earnings... moved up to $10 million from the $5 million level"
- **Source**: [[1986ltr.md#p3]]

### Insurance Industry Commodity Dynamics
- Structural analysis: "pricing behavior in the insurance industry continues to be exactly what can be expected in a commodity-type business"
- Cyclical mechanism: "Only under shortage conditions are high profits achieved, and such conditions don’t last long"
- **Source**: [[1986ltr.md#p4]]

## 💬 Notable Quotes

> "If each of us hires people who are smaller than we are, we shall become a company of dwarfs. But, if each of us hires people who are bigger than we are, we shall become a company of giants." — [[1986ltr.md#p1]]

> "The difference between GEICO’s costs and those of its competitors is a kind of moat that protects a valuable and much-sought-after business castle." — [[1986ltr.md#p5]]

> "While the lamb may lie down with the lion, the lamb shouldn’t count on getting a whole lot of sleep." — [[1986ltr.md#p4]]


## 📊 Investment Decisions

### Fechheimer Bros. Co. Acquisition
- First acquisition meeting new criteria: "$55 million valuation for the entire business"
- Structural fit: "economic record is superb; managers are talented, high-grade, and love what they do"
- **Source**: [[1986ltr.md#p3]]

### Scott Fetzer Acquisition Integration
- Operational structure: "World Book and Kirby... presented separately", "Fourteen other businesses... aggregated in Scott Fetzer - Diversified Manufacturing"
- Accounting treatment: "earnings before purchase-price adjustments are taken into account" to reflect true operating performance
- **Source**: [[1986ltr.md#p1]]
