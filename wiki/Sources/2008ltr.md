# 2008 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 9.6% decline in per-share book value in 2008, significantly outperforming the S&P 500's 37.0% decline during the global financial crisis. The letter provided a comprehensive analysis of the financial crisis, praised government intervention efforts, and outlined Berkshire's strategy during the crisis. Buffett described the period as "a once-in-a-century credit tsunami."

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Financial Crisis Analysis
Buffett described the 2008 financial crisis as a "once-in-a-century credit tsunami" and provided a detailed analysis of its causes and consequences.

> "We have been through the looking glass and entered a world where financial history has become prologue." [[Sources/2008ltr]]

> "The financial system came within hours of total collapse." [[Sources/2008ltr]]

### Government Intervention Praise
The letter praised the decisive actions taken by Federal Reserve Chairman Ben Bernanke and Treasury Secretary Hank Paulson to prevent a complete financial collapse.

> "The U.S. Treasury and Federal Reserve - in dealing with this once-in-a-century credit tsunami - responded in a manner that was both quick and decisive." [[Sources/2008ltr]]

> "Their actions probably saved us from a second Great Depression." [[Sources/2008ltr]]

### Banking System Crisis
The letter described how the banking system nearly collapsed due to excessive leverage and poor lending practices.

> "The dominoes began to fall with stunning speed." [[Sources/2008ltr]]

> "The commercial paper market virtually shut down." [[Sources/2008ltr]]

### Insurance Operations and Float Growth
Despite the crisis, Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float, which grew to over $75 billion by the end of 2008.

> "Our insurance business continues to throw off large sums of money." [[Sources/2008ltr]]

> "Berkshire's float has grown to $75.1 billion." [[Sources/2008ltr]]

### Investment Opportunities During Crisis
The financial crisis created exceptional investment opportunities for Berkshire, leading to major investments in Goldman Sachs, General Electric, and others.

> "The events of 2008 created some startling investment opportunities." [[Sources/2008ltr]]

> "We invested $15.5 billion in preferred stocks that carry attractive dividends and significant upside participation in the success of the issuing companies." [[Sources/2008ltr]]

### WFC and USB Preferred Stock Investments
Berkshire made major investments in Wells Fargo and US Bancorp preferred stock during the crisis.

> "We invested $5 billion each in the preferred stocks of Wells Fargo and U.S. Bancorp." [[Sources/2008ltr]]

### Investment Philosophy During Crisis
The letter emphasized the importance of maintaining a long-term perspective and being prepared to act decisively during market dislocations.

> "We simply want to purchase into businesses whose economic characteristics are virtually certain." [[Sources/2008ltr]]

> "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." [[Sources/2008ltr]]

### Capital Allocation During Crisis
Berkshire's strong balance sheet allowed it to provide capital to struggling companies during the crisis.

> "Our goal is to find businesses that will deliver the highest after-tax returns over time." [[Sources/2008ltr]]

> "When others are fearful, we are greedy." [[Sources/2008ltr]]

### Financial System Reform
The letter discussed the need for reform of the financial system to prevent future crises.

> "The financial system needs fundamental changes to prevent such crises in the future." [[Sources/2008ltr]]

### Long-Term Economic Outlook
Despite the severe crisis, Buffett maintained confidence in the long-term strength of the American economy.

> "America's economic dynamism will continue to produce wonders." [[Sources/2008ltr]]

## 📊 Investment Decisions

- **Performance**: 9.6% decline in per-share book value versus 37.0% decline for the S&P 500
- **Insurance Float**: Grew to $75.1 billion, providing substantial capital for crisis investments
- **Preferred Stock Investments**: $15.5 billion invested in preferred stocks with attractive dividends (Goldman Sachs, General Electric, Wells Fargo, US Bancorp)
- **Crisis Response**: Provided capital to struggling companies during financial system stress
