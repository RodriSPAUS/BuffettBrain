# 2000 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 6.5% gain in per-share book value in 2000, significantly outperforming the S&P 500's -9.1% loss. The letter focused on the importance of patience in investing, the value of businesses with sustainable competitive advantages, and how market downturns create opportunities for disciplined investors. With the dot-com bubble bursting, Buffett's conservative approach proved prescient.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Patience and Long-Term Thinking
Buffett emphasized that patience is a key virtue in investing. The bursting of the technology bubble validated Berkshire's patient, disciplined approach to investment selection.

### Market Corrections Create Opportunities
The market downturn created opportunities to invest in quality businesses at more attractive prices. Market volatility benefits rational investors who can take advantage of others' panic.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantages
Berkshire continued to focus on businesses with durable competitive advantages ("economic moats") that could maintain their market position over long periods regardless of economic cycles.

### Insurance Operations Excellence
Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float at very low cost, providing capital for investments during market dislocations.

### Avoiding Speculative Manias
The letter reinforced the importance of avoiding speculative bubbles and focusing on fundamental business value. The technology boom and subsequent bust illustrated the dangers of investing in businesses without solid foundations.

### Business Fundamentals Over Market Sentiment
True value comes from businesses that generate cash flow and have sustainable competitive advantages, not from market sentiment or momentum.

### Capital Allocation Discipline
Berkshire maintained discipline in capital allocation, making acquisitions during market dislocations when quality businesses were available at attractive prices.

### Partnership Philosophy
Berkshire's approach to business partnerships emphasized trust, integrity, and long-term thinking rather than short-term financial engineering.

### Intrinsic Value Focus
The letter reinforced the importance of focusing on intrinsic value rather than market price, especially during periods of extreme market volatility.
