# 1962 Partnership Letter Summary

`Raw/1962ltr.md` holds two documents: the **July 6, 1962 mid-year report** — which
reprints the January "And a Prediction" in full before reporting the first half —
and the **November 1, 1962 commitment letter** for 1963, which carries the
year-to-October performance and the [[Cases/DempsterMill]] asset-conversion
progress. Together they are the down-market proof of the prediction: the Dow fell
21.7% in the first half while the partnerships lost 7.5% — and the year's high
point was turning Dempster's inventory into cash at virtually 100 cents on the
dollar.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### The prediction, reprinted in its entirety

The first document opens by reprinting, in full, the "And a Prediction" section
from the January 24 letter, beginning with its own preface:

> "While I have no desire to inflict cruel and unusual punishment upon my readers, nevertheless, a reprinting of that section, in its entirety, may be worthwhile:" [[Sources/1962ltr]]

The stakes are matched to the expectation he asks partners to sign up to:

> "It is most important to me that you fully understand my reasoning in this
> regard and agree with me not only in your cerebral regions, but also down in
> the pit of your stomach." [[Sources/1962ltr]]

The numbers restated there: whether the Dow is up 20% or more or down 35–40% in
a violent year, the partnership's job is the same each year of the decade —

> "If it is up 20% or more, we would struggle to be up as much." [[Sources/1962ltr]]

### The first half of 1962 — the down-market proof

Between yearend 1961 and June 30, 1962 the Dow fell from 731.14 to 561.28; with
about $11.00 of dividends received, the overall result was a loss of 21.7%.
The partnerships lost 7.5% before payments to partners — a 14.2-point advantage
Buffett calls "one of the best periods in our history". The target is stated
precisely:

> "Our target is an approximately 1/2% decline for each 1% decline in the Dow
> and if achieved, means we have a considerably more conservative vehicle for
> investment in stocks than practically any alternative." [[Sources/1962ltr]]

The accompanying admonition is the lesson, not the figure:

> "Please keep in mind my continuing admonition that six-months' or even
> one-year's results are not to be taken too seriously." [[Sources/1962ltr]]

### Investment companies during the first half

The Dow yardstick is defended again on fresh evidence from a falling market: in
the first half Massachusetts Investors Trust lost 23% and Investors Stock Fund
25.4%, while the three largest "growth" funds — Fidelity Capital, Putnam Growth,
and Wellington Equity — were hit hardest of all, averaging −32.3%. A June 13,
1962 *Wall Street Journal* article, "Funds vs. Market",
found every one of the 17 large common-stock funds studied had a record poorer
than the Dow from its peak of 734. The irony is not lost on him — investors had
rushed into the previously-beatific growth funds and so experienced the bad year
in large numbers:

> "This experience tends to confirm my hypothesis that investment performance
> must be judged over a period of time with such a period including both
> advancing and declining markets." [[Sources/1962ltr]]

### Asset values — the mechanics of the 6% withdrawal plan

With no allocation to the general partner below +6% on a market-value basis,
partners taking monthly payments saw equity fall twice: once from the results,
once from the withdrawals. He works the example so nobody misreads the plan —
$100,000 of equity at January 1, a −7% year, and $500-a-month payments leave
$87,000 at December 31, so next year's monthly payment is $435. The payments are
explicitly "in no sense guaranteed earnings": to the extent the partnership
earns more than 6% they come from earnings, and otherwise from capital. 

### The Dempster conversion — the high point of 1962

The November 1 letter carries the year's real story. The same discount method as
last yearend — 40% off inventories, 15% off receivables, auction value of fixed
assets — now yields a value of at least $50 per share against the $35 of a year
before, because Dempster has been converted, not merely reappraised:

> "The successful conversion of substantial portions of the assets of Dempster to
> cash, at virtually 100 cents on the dollar, has been the high point of 1962."
> [[Sources/1962ltr]] [[Cases/DempsterMill]]

The mechanics are given in one sentence: inventory of $4.2 million at last
yearend will be about $1.9 million this yearend, cutting the inventory discount
by about $920,000; and cash and investments of roughly $1 million against total
liabilities of $250,000 stand where $166,000 of cash against $2,315,000 of
liabilities stood at November 30, 1961. Consequences for the portfolio: a 5.5%
gain to October 31 against the Dow's −16.8% — a 22.3-point advantage, about 60%
from the non-Dempster portfolio and 40% from the increased Dempster value.

### The honest framing of an abnormal result

The letter's own verdict is the discipline, and it cuts both ways:

> "I want all partners and prospective partners to realize the results described
> above are distinctly abnormal and will recur infrequently, if at all."
> [[Sources/1962ltr]]

The result came from allocation, not prophecy — high general prices "partially
forced me into other categories or investment", and if the Dow had kept soaring
"we would have been low man on the totem pole". He closes the sermon with the
expectation reset for the future:

> "We fully expect to have years when our method of operation will not even match
> the results of the Dow, although obviously I don't expect this on any
> long-term basis or I would throw in the towel and buy the Dow."
> [[Sources/1962ltr]]

## 📊 Investment Decisions

- **Dempster Mill Manufacturing — conversion underway.** Value raised from $35
  to at least $50 per share as inventory was sold (about $4.2M down to ~$1.9M)
  and debt retired ($2.315M to ~$250K), cutting the discount per share by roughly
  $920,000. See [[Cases/DempsterMill]].
- **Results (H1):** partnerships −7.5% against the Dow's −21.7% — a 14.2-point
  advantage, "one of the best periods in our history"; to October 31, +5.5%
  versus −16.8%.
- **Structure:** amended 1963 agreement with no substantive changes; commitment
  letters requested by December 1; additions by January 10 (or earn 6% as
  advance payments); monthly payments restated as withdrawals, not guaranteed
  earnings; signatures to be notarized.
