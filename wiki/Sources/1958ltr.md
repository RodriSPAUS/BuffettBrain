# 1958 Partnership Letter Summary

Written after a wild bull year (the Dow rose 38.5% with dividends), this letter does
two things: it warns that "exuberant" markets end badly, and — for the first time —
it walks the partners through a *named* holding end to end. The Commonwealth Trust
example is the earliest fully documented case of the Buffett method: buy a sound
business at a large discount to [[Concepts/IntrinsicValue]], accumulate quietly,
become large enough to matter, and sell when something better appears.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### "Exuberant" markets, and the fate of the mercurial crowd

Buffett opens with a friend's line about the "mercurial temperament" of the American
investor and reads 1958's psychology as a warning [[Concepts/MrMarket]]:

> "However, I do believe that widespread public belief in the inevitability of profits from investment in stocks will lead to eventual trouble." [[Sources/1958ltr]]

Crucially, he separates price from value: a break would hit the *prices* of even
undervalued securities, but "not intrinsic values in my opinion". He will not
forecast the market:

> "I make no attempt to forecast the general market - my efforts are devoted to finding undervalued securities." [[Sources/1958ltr]]

### Commonwealth Trust Co. — the method, worked in full

The largest holding is revealed: Commonwealth Trust Co. of Union City, New Jersey —
a well-run bank earning about $10 a share, paying no dividend, and therefore selling
near $50 against an intrinsic value Buffett put at $125. [[Sources/1958ltr]]
[[Concepts/MarginOfSafety]] The structure of the bet was three-part: strong
defensive characteristics, value compounding at a good rate, and a probable eventual
merger that would unlock it (value that "might be one year or ten years" away).

Over about a year he accumulated roughly 12% of the bank at about $51, becoming the
second-largest holder with enough votes to warrant consultation on any merger. When
buying competition pushed the price to about $65, he stopped; late in the year, having
found a better situation, he sold the block at $80 — even though the quoted market
was about 20% lower — because "the relative undervaluation at $80 with an intrinsic
value $135 is quite different from a price $50 with an intrinsic value of $125".

### Why the stock market barely mattered here

Commonwealth had about 300 stockholders and traded a couple of times a month, which
lets Buffett make the point that undergirds the whole strategy: an inactive,
controlled position is "very little" affected by the general market — and that is why
"leakage" about holdings must be avoided, since tiny orders move an illiquid price.

### Manufacturing your own work-outs

With the market high and true work-outs scarce, Buffett describes creating them: take
a large enough position in an undervalued security that *you* can drive the corporate
action. The replacement for Commonwealth — about 25% of assets, a position where he
is the largest stockholder — is exactly that.

## 💬 Notable Quotes

> "I make no attempt to forecast the general market - my efforts are devoted to finding undervalued securities."

> "However, I do believe that widespread public belief in the inevitability of profits from investment in stocks will lead to eventual trouble."

> "The higher the level of the market, the fewer the undervalued securities"

## 📊 Investment Decisions

- **Commonwealth Trust Co.** — accumulated ~12% at ~$51 (intrinsic value ~$125),
  sold the block at $80 into a market quoted ~20% lower, to fund a better idea.
- **New largest holding** — ~25% of partnership assets, largest-stockholder position,
  described as a near-assured outperformer for the period held (this is the future
  [[Cases/SanbornMap]], named a year later).
- **Results:** the five full-year partnerships gained 36.7% to 46.2%, slightly ahead
  of the Dow's 38.5% — the "keep pace in a bull market" outcome Buffett had predicted.
