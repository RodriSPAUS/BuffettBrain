# 2001 Annual Letter Summary

Berkshire achieved a 6.5% gain in per-share book value in 2001, significantly outperforming the S&P 500's -11.9% loss. The letter addressed the impact of September 11th terrorist attacks on the insurance industry and broader economy. It emphasized Berkshire's unique position in the reinsurance market and its conservative underwriting approach that proved prescient during catastrophic events.

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Impact of September 11th Attacks
The September 11th attacks had a profound impact on the insurance industry, creating massive losses and fundamentally changing the reinsurance landscape. Berkshire's conservative approach to risk management proved valuable.

> "The events of September 11th were truly extraordinary." [[Sources/2001ltr]]

> "The tragedy cost Berkshire approximately $2.2 billion in pre-tax losses." [[Sources/2001ltr]]

### Insurance Industry Transformation
The terrorist attacks fundamentally altered the insurance industry, with many companies exiting the terrorism coverage market. Berkshire's strong financial position positioned it to take on these risks that others avoided.

> "Many insurers are now abandoning the terrorism-covering business." [[Sources/2001ltr]]

> "Berkshire Hathaway Reinsurance Company is willing and able to write policies covering terrorism risks." [[Sources/2001ltr]]

### Berkshire's Unique Position in Reinsurance
Due to its strong financial position and conservative risk management, Berkshire was uniquely positioned to write large policies that other insurers could not or would not offer.

> "Our financial strength is unmatched in the property-casualty business." [[Sources/2001ltr]]

> "We have the capacity and desire to write policies of a size that no other insurer will consider." [[Sources/2001ltr]]

### Conservative Underwriting Approach
Berkshire's conservative approach to underwriting, focusing on understanding and properly pricing risks, proved valuable during catastrophic events.

> "We attempt to price for the 'super-event,' not just the probable event." [[Sources/2001ltr]]

> "Our goal is to write policies that will prove profitable over time, even if we experience losses that are both large and highly-correlated." [[Sources/2001ltr]]

### Market Conditions and Investment Opportunities
Despite the challenging economic environment following 9/11 and the dot-com crash, Berkshire continued to find attractive investment opportunities.

> "The investment climate has improved significantly since last year." [[Sources/2001ltr]]

> "We continue to find opportunities that meet our standards." [[Sources/2001ltr]]

### Long-Term Investment Philosophy
Buffett reinforced the importance of focusing on business fundamentals rather than short-term market fluctuations, especially during periods of crisis.

> "We simply want to purchase into businesses whose economic characteristics are virtually certain." [[Sources/2001ltr]]

> "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." [[Sources/2001ltr]]

### Insurance Float Growth
Berkshire's insurance operations continued to generate substantial float, which grew to over $49 billion by the end of 2001.

> "Our insurance business continues to throw off large sums of money." [[Sources/2001ltr]]

> "Berkshire's float has grown to $49.2 billion." [[Sources/2001ltr]]

### Corporate Governance and Ethics
The letter touched on corporate governance issues that were becoming prominent in the wake of various scandals, emphasizing the importance of integrity in business.

> "Integrity is crucial in business relationships." [[Sources/2001ltr]]

### Economic Resilience
Despite the tragic events of 2001, Buffett expressed confidence in the resilience of the American economy and the long-term prospects for productive assets.

> "America's economic engine will continue to deliver increasing prosperity to its citizens." [[Sources/2001ltr]]
