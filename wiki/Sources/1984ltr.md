# 1984 Annual Letter Summary

*Letter to Shareholders of Berkshire Hathaway Inc. covering performance and philosophy for 1984*

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Accounting Substance vs. Form in Corporate Redemptions
- **GEICO Tender Offer**: "In mid-1983 GEICO made a tender offer to buy its own shares; at the same time, we agreed by written contract to sell GEICO an amount of its shares that would be proportionately related to the aggregate number of shares GEICO repurchased via the tender from all other shareholders" [[Sources/1984ltr#p1]]
- **General Foods Open-Market Redemption**: "In 1984, we had a virtually identical transaction with General Foods... we sold to the company, on each day that it repurchased shares, a quantity of shares that left our ownership percentage precisely unchanged" [[Sources/1984ltr#p1]]
- **Tax Treatment**: "The Tax Code logically regards such proportionate redemptions as substantially equivalent to dividends and, therefore, the $21 million we received was taxed at only the 6.9% inter-corporate dividend rate" [[Sources/1984ltr#p1]]
- **Accounting Disagreement**: "The New York office of Peat Marwick came into the picture... indicated that it disagreed with the conclusions of the firm's Omaha office and Chicago reviewing partner" [[Sources/1984ltr#p1]]
- **Economic Substance Principle**: "We disagree with the New York position from both the viewpoint of economic substance and proper accounting" [[Sources/1984ltr#p1]]

### Share Repurchase Philosophy and Greenmail Condemnation
- **Two-Tiered Benefit Framework**: "major repurchases at prices well below per-share intrinsic business value immediately increase, in a highly significant way, that value" (arithmetic benefit) and "management clearly demonstrates that it is given to actions that enhance the wealth of shareholders" (signaling benefit) [[Sources/1984ltr#p1]]
- **Greenmail Definition**: "greenmail repurchase - a practice we find odious and repugnant. In these transactions, two parties achieve their personal ends by exploitation of an innocent and unconsulted third party" [[Sources/1984ltr#p1]]
- **Three-Party Structure**: "(1) the 'shareholder' extortionist... (2) the corporate insiders who quickly seek peace at any price... (3) the shareholders whose money is used by (2) to make (1) go away" [[Sources/1984ltr#p1]]
- **Repurchase Examples**: "large share repurchases of GEICO, Washington Post, and General Foods, our three largest holdings" [[Sources/1984ltr#p1]]

### Managerial Integrity as Investment Criterion
- **Nebraska Furniture Mart Acquisition**: "NFM had never had an audit and we did not request one; we did not take an inventory nor verify the receivables; we did not check property titles. We gave Mrs. B a check for $55 million and she gave us her word. That made for an even exchange" [[Sources/1984ltr#p2]]
- **Mrs. B's Character**: "Mrs. B, Chairman of the Board, is now 91, and recently was quoted in the local newspaper as saying, 'I come home to eat and sleep, and that's about it. I can't wait until it gets daylight so I can get back to the business'" [[Sources/1984ltr#p2]]
- **Blumkin Family Management**: "All members of the family: (1) apply themselves with an enthusiasm and energy that would make Ben Franklin and Horatio Alger look like dropouts; (2) define with extraordinary realism their area of special competence" [[Sources/1984ltr#p2]]

### Insurance Industry Structural Forecasting
- **Combined Ratio Analysis**: "Our own combined ratio in 1984 was a humbling 134" compared to industry average of "117.7" [[Sources/1984ltr#p4]]
- **Financial Strength Moat**: "We are almost without question the strongest property/casualty insurance operation in the country, with a capital position far superior to that of well-known companies of much greater size" [[Sources/1984ltr#p4]]
- **Structured Settlements Growth**: "funds that we hold to offset assumed liabilities grew from $16.2 million to $30.6 million during the year" [[Sources/1984ltr#p4]]
- **GEICO Performance**: "GEICO achieved excellent unit growth in its primary insurance business during 1984, and the performance of its investment portfolio continued to be extraordinary" [[Sources/1984ltr#p4]]

### Errors in Loss Reserving Methodology
- **First Rough Draft Analogy**: "Phil Graham... described the daily newspaper as 'a first rough draft of history'. Unfortunately, the financial statements of a property/casualty insurer provide, at best, only a first rough draft of earnings and financial condition" [[Sources/1984ltr#p5]]
- **IBNR Challenge**: "estimates of ultimate dollar costs for losses that occurred during the year but of which the insurer is unaware (termed 'IBNR': incurred but not reported)" [[Sources/1984ltr#p5]]
- **Loss Reserve Revisions**: "Such revisions may be long delayed, but eventually any estimate of losses that causes the income for year X to be misstated must be corrected, whether it is in year X + 1, or X + 10" [[Sources/1984ltr#p5]]

## 📊 Investment Decisions

### General Foods Redemption
- **Open-Market Transaction**: "$21,843,601 in cash from General Foods" with ownership maintained at "exactly 8.75%" through contractual mechanism [[Sources/1984ltr#p1]]
- **Future Contract**: "This year we have again entered into a contract with General Foods whereby we will sell them shares concurrently with open market purchases that they make" [[Sources/1984ltr#p1]]

### Insurance Structured Settlements
- **Growth Metrics**: "funds that we hold to offset assumed liabilities grew from $16.2 million to $30.6 million" [[Sources/1984ltr#p4]]
- **Capital Reinforcement**: "added substantially to the capital of Columbia Insurance Company" [[Sources/1984ltr#p4]]

### Equity Portfolio Composition
- **Top Holdings**: "6,850,000 GEICO Corporation ($397.3M), 4,047,191 General Foods Corporation ($226.1M), 3,895,710 Exxon Corporation ($175.3M)" [[Sources/1984ltr#p3]]
- **Diversification Strategy**: "It's been over ten years since it has been as difficult as now to find equity investments that meet both our qualitative standards and our quantitative standards of value versus price" [[Sources/1984ltr#p3]]
