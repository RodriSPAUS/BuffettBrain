# 1984 Annual Letter Summary

*Report on Berkshire Hathaway's performance and philosophy as of February 22, 1985 (covering 1984 results)*

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Accounting Substance vs. Form
- Formalized principle: "economic substance and proper accounting" must align, rejecting "accounting approach only, having no bearing on taxes"
- GEICO/General Foods redemptions: "proportionate redemptions... substantially equivalent to dividends" despite auditor disagreement
- **Source**: [[1984ltr.md#p1]]

### Share Repurchase Philosophy
- Two-tiered framework: (1) obvious arithmetic benefit: "major repurchases at prices well below per-share intrinsic business value immediately increase... that value", (2) subtle signaling benefit: "demonstrated pro-shareholder leanings" increases market valuation
- Explicit condemnation of "greenmail": "odious and repugnant" practice exploiting "innocent shareholder standing by"
- **Source**: [[1984ltr.md#p1]]

### Managerial Integrity as Investment Criterion
- Nebraska Furniture Mart acquisition: "We gave Mrs. B a check for $55 million and she gave us her word. That made for an even exchange"
- Core principle: "evaluation of the integrity of Mrs. B and her family was demonstrated" through zero due diligence
- **Source**: [[1984ltr.md#p2]]

### Insurance Industry Structural Forecasting
- Quantitative forecast: "five years of 15%-per-year increases in premiums would be required to get the combined ratio back to 100"
- Financial strength as moat: "our premier financial strength would make a real difference in the competitive position of our insurance operation"
- **Source**: [[1984ltr.md#p4]]

## 📊 Investment Decisions

### General Foods Redemption
- First open-market redemption: "$21,843,601 in cash from General Foods" with ownership maintained at "exactly 8.75%"
- Contractual mechanism: "sell them shares concurrently with open market purchases that they make"
- **Source**: [[1984ltr.md#p1]]

### Insurance Structured Settlements
- New line of business: "funds that we hold to offset assumed liabilities grew from $16.2 million to $30.6 million"
- Capital reinforcement: "added substantially to the capital of Columbia Insurance Company"
- **Source**: [[1984ltr.md#p4]]
