*This summary synthesizes key insights from Warren Buffett's 1978 Annual Letter to Berkshire Hathaway shareholders, highlighting themes that shaped his investment philosophy and connecting them to broader concepts within the knowledge base.*

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Return on Equity Performance
Buffett reports that 1978 operating earnings were 19.4% of beginning shareholders' investment, nearly matching the 1972 record. He emphasizes that while capital gains are important for long-term growth, operating earnings measured by return on equity provide the most meaningful assessment of managerial performance.

[[Concepts/CapitalAllocation]]

### Textile Industry Economics
The letter provides a detailed analysis of why textile businesses produce inadequate returns: capital-intensive operations with undifferentiated goods lead to pricing based on direct operating costs rather than capital employed. Buffett notes this supply-excess condition is likely to persist in textiles.

[[Cases/BerkshireHathawayEnergy]]

### Insurance Underwriting Excellence
Phil Liesche's segment at National Indemnity Company achieved extraordinary results in 1978, realizing approximately $11 million in underwriting profit on $90 million of earned premiums. This performance is contrasted with industry conditions and highlights the importance of managerial talent.

[[Concepts/Moat]]

### Four Investment Criteria Reaffirmed
Buffett reiterates the four criteria for stock selection established in 1977: (1) businesses we can understand, (2) with favorable long-term prospects, (3) operated by honest and competent people, and (4) priced very attractively. He notes that while criteria (1)-(3) are often met, criterion (4) frequently prevents action.

[[Concepts/IntrinsicValue]]

### SAFECO as Investment Paradigm
The letter uses SAFECO Corporation as a prime example of buying outstanding businesses at bargain prices. Buffett notes that SAFECO was purchased at substantially under book value, demonstrating that superior companies can be acquired more cheaply through stock market purchases than through negotiated corporate acquisitions.

## 📊 Investment Decisions

- Acquired significant position in [SAFECO Corporation](Cases/GEICO), recognizing it as the best-run large property and casualty insurance company in the US, purchased at substantially under book value.
- Continued investment in [Government Employees Insurance Company](Cases/GEICO) through both convertible preferred and common stock holdings.
- Increased holdings in [The Washington Post Company](Cases/GEICO) Class B shares, noting its strong business fundamentals.
