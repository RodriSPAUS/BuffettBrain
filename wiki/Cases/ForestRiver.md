## Why this case matters

Forest River is the clearest modern illustration of how Berkshire actually
acquires a business: no auction, no due-diligence army, and a decision resting on
the character of the owner-manager. Buffett returns to it in 2024, nineteen years
later, to tell the story in full — which makes it the best single case for
[[Concepts/ManagementQuality]] and [[Principles/Fidelity]] observed over a long
horizon.

## The acquisition

> "Forest River, our second acquisition, closed on August 31. A couple of months
> earlier, on June 21, I received a two-page fax telling me – point by point –
> why Forest River met the acquisition criteria we set forth on page 25 of this
> report. I had not before heard of the company, a recreational vehicle
> manufacturer with $1.6 billion of sales, nor of Pete Liegl, its owner and
> manager. But the fax made sense, and I immediately asked for more figures."
> [[Sources/2005ltr]]

The company at the time:

> "Forest River has 60 plants, 5,400 employees and has consistently gained share
> in the RV business, while also expanding into other areas such as boats."
> [[Sources/2005ltr]]

And the operating principle applied from day one:

> "You can be sure that I won't be telling Pete how to manage his operation."
> [[Sources/2005ltr]]

Five years on, the arrangement was unchanged:

> "Pete Liegl, the company's CEO,runs a terrific operation." [[Sources/2010ltr]]

Between the acquisition and the 2024 retrospective, Forest River appears only
in passing — a name in a tuck-in acquisition list in 2008 and a contributor to
a "second tier" of non-insurance earnings, alongside Johns Manville, MiTek,
Shaw and TTI, in the condensed 2017 roll-up (the group earned $2.1 billion
combined that year, up from $1.7 billion). [[Sources/2008ltr]] [[Sources/2017ltr]]
Both mentions confirm the arrangement described in 2005 held for over a decade
without needing to be revisited in the letters — itself a form of evidence for
how little supervision Liegl required.

## The retrospective

Buffett devotes a named section of the 2024 letter to Liegl, who died in
November 2024 still working at 80:

> "Pete Liegl – One of a Kind Let me pause to tell you the remarkable story of
> Pete Liegl, a man unknown to most Berkshire shareholders but one who contributed
> many billions to their aggregate wealth." [[Sources/2024ltr]]

> "I first heard of Forest River – the Indiana company Pete founded and managed –
> on June 21, 2005." [[Sources/2024ltr]]

> "The writer said that Pete, the 100% owner of Forest River, specifically wanted
> to sell to Berkshire." [[Sources/2024ltr]]

That last point is the recurring Berkshire acquisition advantage: the seller
chose the buyer. See [[Principles/OwnershipMindset]].

The 2024 letter also finally discloses the compensation terms Liegl proposed
for himself at their first meeting — the term the earlier version of this page
was still missing:

> "Well, I looked at Berkshire's proxy statement and I wouldn't want to make
> more than my boss, so pay me $100,000 per year." [[Sources/2024ltr]]

Liegl added a request for a 10% bonus on earnings growth above the company's
then-current level — a handshake structure that held for the next 19 years,
during which "no competitor came close to his performance." [[Sources/2024ltr]]
Buffett uses the story to reject academic pedigree as a hiring filter,
pairing it with Bill Gates's decision to leave Harvard and Ben Rosner — a
Buffett/Munger retailing manager who, per his step-granddaughter, "never went
past 6th grade." [[Sources/2024ltr]] [[Concepts/ManagementQuality]]

## What the case establishes

- The deal process is a filter on people, not a valuation exercise. It began with
  a two-page fax about a company Buffett had never heard of.
- The owner-manager stays and runs the business unsupervised — the explicit
  commitment is not to tell him how to do it.
- A founder who wants a particular home for what he built is worth more to
  Berkshire than a competitive bid. The same dynamic appears in the Nebraska
  Furniture Mart purchase described in [[Sources/1984ltr]].
