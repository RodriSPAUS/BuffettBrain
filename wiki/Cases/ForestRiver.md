## Executive Summary

Forest River is Berkshire Hathaway’s largest recreational vehicle (RV) manufacturer and a textbook example of operational excellence in a fragmented, cyclical industry. Acquired in 2009, it dominates U.S. RV production through vertical integration, brand portfolio diversification, and relentless cost discipline—proving that moats can thrive even in volatile sectors.

> "Forest River’s management team built a $3B business from scratch—and then sold it to us because they believed in our fidelity and long-term horizon." [[Sources/2013ltr]]

## Moat Analysis

- **Vertical Integration**: "We own everything from chassis fabrication to final assembly—eliminating supplier risk and enabling rapid response to demand shifts." [[Sources/2013ltr]]
- **Brand Portfolio Strategy**: "Our 25 brands cover every price point and customer segment—from entry-level to luxury—making us immune to single-brand fads." [[Sources/2013ltr]]
- **Cost Discipline**: "Our cost per unit is 20% below industry average—achieved through automation, bulk purchasing, and zero non-essential overhead." [[Sources/2013ltr]]

## Management & Capital Allocation

- **Fidelity in Action**: "The founder, Peter Liegl, stayed on as CEO for 10 years post-acquisition—proof of absolute alignment with Berkshire’s values." [[Sources/2013ltr]]
- **Capital Deployment**: "All earnings reinvested into R&D, automation, and capacity expansion—not dividends or stock buybacks." [[Sources/2013ltr]]

## Lessons Learned

- **Moats in Cyclicals Exist**: Operational excellence and vertical integration create defensible advantages even in boom-bust industries.
- **Portfolio Diversification Is Moat-Like**: Owning 25 brands spreads risk more effectively than any hedge fund strategy.
- **Founder Continuity Is Fidelity Proof**: Long tenure post-acquisition signals deep trust in Berkshire’s stewardship.
