# FlightSafety International

Berkshire's pilot-training business — the world leader in the field — and a piece of
its aviation exposure that is training, not leasing or flying. Acquired in 1996 for
roughly $1.5 billion, it began when a longtime shareholder suggested the fit and
Buffett met the 79-year-old founder, Al Ueltschi.

## The one-manager acquisition

The deal is Buffett's standard pattern — a founder-operator he trusts, judged fast:

> "I had long been familiar with FlightSafety's business, and in about 60 seconds I knew that Al was exactly our kind of manager." [[Sources/1996ltr]] [[Concepts/ManagementQuality]]

It also fits the age-blind hiring Buffett defends across the letters: Ueltschi kept
running the business well past the age most companies retire people, the same
principle behind [[People/RoseBlumkin]] and the Kansas Bankers Surety purchase of
the same year. [[Sources/1996ltr]]

## The link to NetJets

FlightSafety trains the pilots of [[Cases/NetJets]], acquired two years later — the
two aviation subsidiaries reinforce each other, and it was FlightSafety's training
standard that gave Buffett the confidence to do all his flying on NetJets crews.
