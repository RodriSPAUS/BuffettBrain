# Coca-Cola

Berkshire's defining consumer-brand holding and the case Buffett uses to define a
[[Concepts/Moat]] built on brand rather than cost. The position, begun in 1988,
is the empirical anchor for "The Inevitables" — the handful of businesses whose
dominance he judges nearly certain over an investing lifetime.

## The purchase: fifty years late by his own account

Buffett bought the stake in 1988 and enlarged it in 1989, when Berkshire's holding
"increased from 14,172,500 shares at the end of 1988 to 23,350,000."
[[Sources/1989ltr]] He introduces it with characteristic self-deprecation:

> "This Coca-Cola investment provides yet another example of the incredible speed with which your Chairman responds to investment opportunities, no matter how obscure or well-disguised they may be." [[Sources/1989ltr]]

Having sold Cokes as a boy for a nickel and watched the product "blanket the world"
for 52 years without owning a share, he dates the decision to a single moment:

> "Only in the summer of 1988 did my brain finally establish contact with my eyes." [[Sources/1989ltr]]

He credits the buy not to a cheap price but to the business's transformation under
CEO Roberto Goizueta and Don Keough after 1981, and closes with a promise on
[[Concepts/CircleOfCompetence]] and reaction time: "My response time to the next
glaringly attractive idea will be slashed to well under 50 years." [[Sources/1989ltr]]

## Why it is a moat, not a business

Coca-Cola is Buffett's cleanest example of an [[Concepts/EconomicGoodwill]]-heavy
franchise whose advantage is preference, not cost. The 1993 letter states the moat
as self-evident:

> "The competitive strengths of a Coke or Gillette are obvious to even the casual observer of business." [[Sources/1993ltr]] [[Concepts/Moat]]

Paired with [[Cases/SeeCandies]], it defines pricing power — the ability to raise
price without losing the customer — as the observable test of a durable moat.

## "The Inevitables"

In 1996 Buffett gives the category its name, and Coca-Cola is the lead exhibit:

> "Companies such as Coca-Cola and Gillette might well be labeled 'The Inevitables.'" [[Sources/1996ltr]]

He attaches the warning that even an Inevitable can be mismanaged into stagnation —
noting Coke once strayed into growing shrimp — so the label rewards management
fidelity, it does not excuse its absence. [[Concepts/ManagementQuality]] The
holding illustrates the [[Concepts/IntrinsicValue]] point that a wonderful business
bought at a fair price compounds for decades: it is the archetype of a
[[Principles/OwnershipMindset]] position Buffett has no intention of selling.
