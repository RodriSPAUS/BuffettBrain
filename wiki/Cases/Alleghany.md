# Alleghany Corporation

## Why this case matters

Alleghany is Berkshire's cleanest illustration of "turn every page" producing
an acquisition: a $11.6 billion deal that Buffett says would never have
happened without a single unsolicited email, arriving one day before his own
letter told shareholders Berkshire saw little worth buying.

## The Deal, and the Email That Caused It

At the 2022 annual meeting, Buffett explains that he had followed Alleghany
for 60 years, keeping years of its annual reports on file out of long-standing
interest, well before any acquisition was contemplated. On February 25, 2022 —
one day before his own annual letter, dated February 26, told shareholders he
and Munger saw "little that excites us" in the market — Alleghany's new CEO
Joe Brandon sent Buffett a courtesy copy of his first annual report as CEO.
Buffett wrote back proposing a meeting in New York; that meeting led directly
to Berkshire's offer, with no bankers or formal sale process preceding it:

> "But otherwise, if that e-mail hadn't been sent, we would not have made the
> offer for Alleghany." [[Sources/Conference_call_2022]]

## What the case establishes

- Decades of unglamorous, undirected reading (Buffett's "turn every page")
  is itself the sourcing mechanism for large acquisitions, not a substitute
  for one.
- The deal was struck without investment bankers or a competitive process —
  consistent with Buffett's general preference for direct, single-buyer
  negotiations over auctions. [[Concepts/CapitalAllocation]]
- Timing in this business is irregular by nature: the same letter that
  confessed to seeing nothing worth buying was followed, within weeks, by
  Berkshire's largest acquisition in years.
