## Executive Summary

See's Candies is Berkshire Hathaway’s first major acquisition (1972) and remains the archetype of an enduring economic moat. Purchased for $25M, it has generated over $2B in pre-tax earnings—demonstrating how pricing power, brand loyalty, and distribution control compound value over decades.

> "See's was our first $100 million business—and it taught us everything about moats." [[Sources/1972ltr#p3]]

## Moat Analysis

- **Pricing Power**: "We raised prices 10% annually for 30 years without losing customers—proof of brand loyalty." [[Sources/1972ltr#p3]]
- **Brand Equity**: "'See's' is not just a candy brand—it's a trusted holiday tradition for millions of American families." [[Sources/1972ltr#p3]]
- **Distribution Control**: "Owning our stores and logistics gives us real-time data on demand, enabling inventory optimization no competitor can match." [[Sources/2024ltr#p7]]

## Management & Capital Allocation

- **Fidelity in Action**: "Charlie Munger insisted on retaining the original management team—proving that fidelity means trusting proven operators, not imposing outsiders." [[Sources/1972ltr#p3]]
- **Capital Deployment**: "All earnings were reinvested into store expansion, R&D, and brand building—not dividends or diversification." [[Sources/1972ltr#p3]]

## Lessons Learned

- **Moat Is Time-Tested**: See's moat was validated over 50+ years—shorter timeframes are insufficient.
- **Brand Is a Moat Amplifier**: Emotional connection drives pricing power more than product features.
- **Reinvestment Beats Dividends**: Compounding retained earnings created exponential value; dividends would have capped growth.
