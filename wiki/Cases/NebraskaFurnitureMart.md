Acquired in 1983 — 90% of the business, with 10% left with the family members in
management and 10% optioned to key young family managers.

## The business

One store of 200,000 square feet in Omaha, doing over $100 million of annual
sales in 1983 — more furniture, carpets and appliances than every Omaha
competitor combined, and more volume than any other home furnishings store in the
country [[Sources/1983ltr]].

## Why Buffett calls it the ideal business

The economics are a cost advantage deliberately given away at the counter, which
is what makes it durable rather than merely profitable:

> "They buy brilliantly, they operate at expense ratios competitors don't even
> dream about, and they then pass on to their customers much of the savings. It's
> the ideal business - one built upon exceptional value to the customer that in
> turn translates into exceptional economics for its owners." [[Sources/1983ltr]]

It is also where Buffett states the competitive test he says he always applies:
"One question I always ask myself in appraising a business is how I would like,
assuming I had ample capital and skilled personnel, to compete with it. I'd rather
wrestle grizzlies than compete with Mrs. B and her progeny." [[Sources/1983ltr]]

This is the wide-and-sustainable cost advantage of [[Sources/1982ltr]] in concrete
form. See [[Concepts/Moat]].

## Rose Blumkin

Left Russia at 23, talking her way past a border guard, with no formal schooling
and no English — learned the language in the evenings from her elder daughter.
Opened the store in 1937 with $500 saved from selling used clothing.

Two episodes Buffett reports as evidence of character rather than colour. When her
resources ran out early on, "she simply sold the furniture and appliances from her
home in order to pay creditors precisely as promised." And when Omaha retailers
pressured suppliers to cut her off and she was taken to court under Fair Trade
laws, she won every case — "At the end of one case, after demonstrating to the
court that she could profitably sell carpet at a huge discount from the prevailing
price, she sold the judge $1400 worth of carpet." [[Sources/1983ltr]]

At 90 she remained Chairman and worked the sales floor seven days a week, carpet
being her specialty.

On the family that followed her: "Geneticists should do handsprings over the
Blumkin family." [[Sources/1983ltr]] Louie Blumkin, her son, had been President for
many years and is described as the shrewdest buyer of furniture and appliances in
the country.
