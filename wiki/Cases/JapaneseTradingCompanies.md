# The Five Japanese Trading Companies

## Why this case matters

Berkshire's stakes in Japan's five major trading houses (referred to
collectively in this source, not named individually) are the clearest
example in these sources of a position explicitly bought with a
multi-decade — Buffett says fifty-to-hundred-year — holding horizon, and of
currency risk managed through matched local-currency debt rather than
through the equity position itself.

## Building the Position

At the 2025 annual meeting, Buffett describes finding the five companies
"selling at ridiculously low prices" while working through a large handbook
of Japanese companies, and building the stake toward a self-imposed 10%
ownership ceiling agreed with each company. By 2025 that ceiling was being
relaxed by mutual agreement, and the position stood at roughly $20 billion:

> "And we've got that market in the range of $20 billion invested, but I'd
> rather have $100 billion than $20 billion." [[Sources/Conference_call_2025]]

## A Fifty-Year Commitment, Stated Directly

Both Buffett and Greg Abel are explicit, in 2025, that the position is not
for sale on any foreseeable timeframe:

> "We will not be selling any stock." [[Sources/Conference_call_2025]]

> "It's super long term." [[Sources/Conference_call_2025]]

Abel describes regular in-person meetings with each company and an intention
to pursue joint ventures and larger opportunities together over time, framing
the relationship as expanding rather than static.

## Currency Risk, Hedged Through Debt Rather Than the Equity

Berkshire has issued yen-denominated debt to fund and roughly match the
Japanese investment — the first time, Buffett says, Berkshire has
deliberately matched an equity position's currency exposure with liabilities
in the same currency, distinct from a separate, one-time bet against the
U.S. dollar the firm made decades earlier and held for a couple of years.
[[Sources/Conference_call_2025]]

## What the case establishes

- A sufficiently attractive price, found through unglamorous research (a
  physical handbook of thousands of companies), can justify a position
  Buffett says he intends to hold for longer than his own remaining lifetime.
- Currency risk on a foreign equity stake can be managed at the funding
  level (matched local-currency debt) rather than by hedging or limiting the
  equity position itself.
- Size is treated explicitly as a constraint on returns: Buffett says
  directly that he wishes Berkshire could put $100 billion into the position
  rather than $20 billion, but "size is an enemy of performance at
  Berkshire." [[Sources/Conference_call_2025]]
