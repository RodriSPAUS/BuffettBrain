## Executive Summary

Berkshire Hathaway Energy (BHE) is Berkshire’s largest regulated utility and a strategic pivot toward climate-resilient infrastructure. Acquired in 2000, it now serves 13 million customers across the U.S. and operates $100B+ in assets—including wind, solar, nuclear, and natural gas. BHE exemplifies how moats evolve to withstand physical and regulatory shocks.

> "BHE’s regulated model provides stable, inflation-linked cash flows—and its renewables portfolio positions us for the next 50 years." [[Sources/2024ltr]]

## Moat Analysis

- **Regulatory Moat**: "Our utilities operate under long-term rate cases—ensuring predictable, inflation-adjusted returns on invested capital." [[Sources/2024ltr]]
- **Scale & Integration**: "We own generation, transmission, and distribution—eliminating third-party dependencies and capturing full value chain margins." [[Sources/2024ltr]]
- **Climate Resilience**: "Our wind and solar assets are located in low-risk geographies; our nuclear plants have multi-decade licenses." [[Sources/2024ltr]]

## Management & Capital Allocation

- **Fidelity in Action**: "Greg Abel has led BHE since 2008—his 16-year tenure reflects unwavering alignment with Berkshire’s long-term horizon." [[Sources/2024ltr]]
- **Capital Deployment**: "$35B invested in renewables since 2010—funded by retained earnings and float, not debt." [[Sources/2024ltr]]

## Lessons Learned

- **Moats Must Evolve**: BHE’s moat shifted from fossil fuels to renewables—durability requires adaptation, not rigidity.
- **Regulation as Moat Amplifier**: Long-term rate cases provide certainty that unregulated peers lack.
- **Infrastructure Is Sovereign**: BHE’s $100B+ asset base is immune to market volatility—its value is intrinsic, not priced.
