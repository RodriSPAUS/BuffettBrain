# 1994 Annual Letter Summary

*Report on Berkshire Hathaway's performance and philosophy as of February 25, 1995 (covering 1994 results)*

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Intrinsic Value Growth Constraint Reinforced
- Structural definition: "The problem is not that what has worked in the past will cease to work in the future. To the contrary, we believe that our formula... is certain to produce reasonable success"
- Quantitative insight: "If our intrinsic value is to grow at our target rate of 15%, our look-through earnings, over time, must also increase at about that pace"
- **Source**: [[1994ltr.md#p0]]

### Look-Through Earnings Framework Operationalized
- Structural insight: "Look-through earnings consist of: (1) the operating earnings reported... plus; (2) the retained operating earnings of major investees... less; (3) an allowance for the tax that would be paid by Berkshire"
- Behavioral insight: "We are now modestly ahead of schedule in meeting our goal, but to a significant degree that is because our super-cat insurance business has recently delivered earnings far above trend-line expectancy"
- **Source**: [[1994ltr.md#p1]]

### Super-Cat Insurance Competitive Advantage Codified
- Structural analysis: "Super-cat policies are small in number, large in size and non-standardized. Therefore, the underwriting of this business requires far more judgment than... auto policies"
- Economic insight: "Ajit Jain, our super-cat manager, whose underwriting skills are the finest. His value to us is simply enormous"
- **Source**: [[1994ltr.md#p2]]

### Acquisition Behavior Taxonomy Refined
- Structural insight: "At Berkshire, we have rejected many merger and purchase opportunities that would have boosted current and near-term earnings but that would have reduced per-share intrinsic value"
- Behavioral contrast: "Our approach, rather, has been to follow Wayne Gretzky's advice: 'Go to where the puck is going to be, not to where it is.'"
- **Source**: [[1994ltr.md#p0]]

## 📊 Investment Decisions

### Scott Fetzer Acquisition Strategy
- Formalized principle: "Berkshire purchased Scott Fetzer at the beginning of 1986. At the time, the company was a collection of 22 businesses, and today we have exactly the same line-up - no additions and no disposals"
- Structural insight: "We paid $315.2 million for Scott Fetzer, which at the time had $172.6 million of book value. The $142.6 million premium we handed over indicated our belief that the company's intrinsic value was close to double its book value"
- **Source**: [[1994ltr.md#p0]]

### Capital Allocation Framework
- Formalized principle: "Understanding intrinsic value is as important for managers as it is for investors. When managers are making capital allocation decisions... it's vital that they act in ways that increase per-share intrinsic value and avoid moves that decrease it"
- Behavioral insight: "When capital invested in an operation is significant, we also both charge managers a high rate for incremental capital they employ and credit them at an equally high rate for capital they release"
- **Source**: [[1994ltr.md#p0]]
