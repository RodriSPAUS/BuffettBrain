# CapitalAllocation

*The process of deploying capital to maximize long-term shareholder value — Buffett's most important managerial skill.*

## 📚 Definition

CapitalAllocation is the deliberate, disciplined deployment of financial resources across four primary options: (1) reinvest in existing businesses, (2) acquire new businesses, (3) repurchase shares, or (4) pay dividends. Buffett ranks these by *opportunity cost* and *long-term compounding power*, not short-term optics.

> "Berkshire shareholders can rest assured that we will forever deploy a substantial majority of their money in equities." — [[2024ltr.md#p6]]

## 🧭 Hierarchy of Options

| Rank | Option | Rationale | Evidence |
|------|--------|-----------|----------|
| **1** | Reinvest in existing businesses | Highest ROI when moats are intact; compounds value at business-level returns | GEICO transformation — [[2024ltr.md#p4]] |
| **2** | Acquire new businesses | Requires exceptional management fit and durable moat; rare but transformative | Forest River acquisition — [[2024ltr.md#p2-3]] |
| **3** | Share repurchases | Compelling only when price < intrinsic value; signals confidence | Berkshire's repurchase discipline — [[2020ltr.md]] |
| **4** | Dividends | Only paid once (1967); viewed as suboptimal use of capital vs. reinvestment | $101,755 total dividend history — [[2024ltr.md#p5]] |

## 💰 Float as Strategic Capital

- Insurance float ($171B in 2024) is treated as *permanent, low-cost capital* for investment — not idle cash
- Float quality depends on underwriting discipline: must be *costless or profitable* over time
