# 1979 Annual Letter Summary

*Report on Berkshire Hathaway's performance and philosophy as of March 25, 1980 (covering 1979 results)*

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Investor's Misery Index
- First formal articulation: "inflation rate plus the percentage of capital that must be paid by the owner... can be thought of as an 'investor's misery index'"
- Quantitative warning: when misery index > ROE, real purchasing power shrinks even if investor consumes nothing
- **Source**: [[1979ltr.md#p3]]

### Turnarounds vs. Good Businesses
- Foundational principle: "'turnarounds' seldom turn, and that the same energies and talent are much better employed in a good business purchased at a fair price than in a poor business purchased at a bargain price"
- Waumbec Mills case study: "bought well below working capital... but the purchase was a mistake"
- **Source**: [[1979ltr.md#p6]]

### Insurance Underwriting Discipline
- Reinforced policy: "if business makes sense, he writes it; if it doesn't, he rejects it" (Phil Liesche)
- Explicit contrast: "very few [managers] actually do so" despite claiming willingness to reduce volume
- **Source**: [[1979ltr.md#p7]]

### Bond Market Revolution
- Radical critique: "The very long-term bond contract has been the last major fixed price contract of extended duration still regularly initiated in an inflation-ridden world"
- Policy shift: stopped buying straight long-term bonds; shifted to convertible bonds with equity-like characteristics
- **Source**: [[1979ltr.md#p10]]

## 📊 Investment Decisions

### GEICO Corp.
- Massive expansion: 5,730,114 shares of GEICO common stock
- Strategic rationale: "truly outstanding businesses sometimes sell... at very large discounts"
- **Source**: [[1979ltr.md#p10]]

### Convertible Bonds
- New strategy: "purchase of convertible bonds" to obtain "far shorter average life than implied by maturity terms"
- Rationale: "conversion options... give that portion of the bond portfolio a far shorter average life"
- **Source**: [[1979ltr.md#p10]]
