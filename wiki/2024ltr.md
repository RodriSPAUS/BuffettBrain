*Report on Berkshire Hathaway's performance and philosophy as of February 22, 2025 (covering 2024 results)*

- **Climate-resilient moats**: “P/C insurance pricing strengthened during 2024, reflecting a major increase in damage from convective storms. Climate change may have been announcing its arrival." [[2024ltr.md#p4]] → moats must now withstand climate volatility, not just competition

## 🔍 Core Insights

### Mistakes as Core Learning Mechanism
> "A decent batting average in personnel decisions is all that can be hoped for. The cardinal sin is delaying the correction of mistakes or what Charlie Munger called 'thumb-sucking.'" [[2024ltr.md#p1]]

- **Psychological safety**: Acknowledging errors publicly (“16 times in my letters") builds trust and prevents groupthink
- **Action bias**: Delayed correction compounds loss—“problems cannot be wished away" [[2024ltr.md#p1]]
- **Legacy framing**: “Mistakes fade away; winners can forever blossom." [[2024ltr.md#p3]] → long-term compounding renders early errors irrelevant if corrected

### Pete Liegl as Archetype of Fidelity & Simplicity
> "Pete paused as his wife, daughter and I leaned forward. Then he surprised us: 'Well, I looked at Berkshire’s proxy statement and I wouldn’t want to make more than my boss, so pay me $100,000 per year.'" [[2024ltr.md#p2]]

- **Fidelity as non-negotiable**: Compensation capped by CEO’s salary signals absolute alignment with owners
- **Simplicity as strength**: No appraisal needed, no complex KPIs—trust in judgment and shared values replaces bureaucracy
- **Behavioral signal**: “Pay me $100,000" is a cultural litmus test—filters for ego, greed, and short-termism

### Climate-Resilient Moats & Float as Moat Amplifier
> "P/C insurance pricing strengthened during 2024, reflecting a major increase in damage from convective storms. Climate change may have been announcing its arrival." [[2024ltr.md#p4]]

- **Moat evolution**: Traditional economic moats (brand, scale, network effects) now require resilience against physical shocks (wildfires, hurricanes)
- **Float as amplifier**: $171B float isn’t passive—it’s strategic capital deployed to acquire climate-resilient assets and fund R&D (e.g., Occidental’s carbon capture)
- **Underwriting discipline**: “We must never write inadequately-priced policies in order to stay in the game." [[2024ltr.md#p9]] → moat protection requires pricing rigor, not market share

### Fidelity > Credentials in Management Selection
> "I never look at where a candidate has gone to school. Never!" [[2024ltr.md#p3]]

- **Nature over nurture**: “A very large portion of business talent is innate with nature swamping nurture." [[2024ltr.md#p3]] → pedigree correlates poorly with execution excellence
- **Evidence-based hiring**: Ben Rosner (6th grade), Bill Gates (Harvard dropout), Pete Liegl (unknown school)—all validated by results, not résumés
- **Anti-elitism**: Rejects credentialism as a proxy for competence; focuses on observable fidelity, judgment, and grit

### Bonus Tied to Performance & Capital Discipline
> "I would like an annual bonus of 10% of any earnings above what the company is now delivering." [[2024ltr.md#p2]]

- **Capital discipline**: Bonus only on *incremental* earnings—no reward for maintaining status quo or inflation-driven growth
- **Alignment mechanics**: “If Forest River makes any significant acquisitions we will make an appropriate adjustment" [[2024ltr.md#p2]] → bonus formula adapts to capital deployment decisions
- **Long-term lens**: “Our horizon for such commitments is almost always far longer than a single year." [[2024ltr.md#p4]] → ties incentives to multi-year value creation

### Reinvestment Hierarchy & Float as Strategic Capital
> "Berkshire shareholders endorsed continuous reinvestment... enabled the company to build its taxable income." [[2024ltr.md#p5]]

- **Hierarchy of capital use**: 1) Reinvest in existing businesses, 2) Acquire new ones, 3) Repurchase shares, 4) Pay dividends (only once, in 1967)
- **Float as sovereign capital**: “The float is likely to grow a bit over time and, with intelligent underwriting (and some luck), has a reasonable prospect of being costless." [[2024ltr.md#p9]] → float is Berkshire’s most scalable, lowest-cost funding source
- **Tax efficiency**: $26.8B tax payment reflects massive retained earnings → tax deferral is a hidden moat amplifier
