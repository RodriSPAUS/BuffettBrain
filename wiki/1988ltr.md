# 1988 Annual Letter Summary

*Report on Berkshire Hathaway's performance and philosophy as of February 25, 1989 (covering 1988 results)*

## 🔑 Key Themes

### Capital Base Growth Constraint Formalized
- Quantitative benchmark: "For the next decade, a 15% return demands profits of $10.3 billion"
- Structural insight: "Four years ago I told you that we needed profits of $3.9 billion... Today... a 15% return demands profits of $10.3 billion"
- **Source**: [[1988ltr.md#p0]]

### Accounting Transparency Framework
- Core principle: "What needs to be reported is data... that helps financially-literate readers answer three key questions"
- Three-question framework: (1) Approximately how much is this company worth? (2) What is the likelihood that it can meet its future obligations? (3) How good a job are its managers doing?
- **Source**: [[1988ltr.md#p1]]

### Managerial Longevity & Generational Continuity
- Formalized principle: "We do not remove superstars from our lineup merely because they have attained a specified age"
- Structural insight: "Superb managers are too scarce a resource to be discarded simply because a cake gets crowded with candles"
- **Source**: [[1988ltr.md#p2]]

### Insurance Industry Commodity Economics Confirmed
- Structural analysis: "The property-casualty insurance industry is not only subnormally profitable, it is subnormally popular"
- Behavioral insight: "Many relatively-unprofitable industries... habitually find themselves beat upon by irate customers even while other, hugely profitable industries are spared complaints"
- **Source**: [[1988ltr.md#p3]]

## 📊 Investment Decisions

### Borsheim’s Acquisition
- First family acquisition: "We are now 80% partners with another branch of the family"
- Structural fit: "The cornerstone for both enterprises is Mrs. B’s creed: 'Sell cheap and tell the truth'"
- **Source**: [[1988ltr.md#p4]]

### Permanent Holdings Philosophy Reinforced
- Formalized principle: "When we own portions of outstanding businesses with outstanding managements, our favorite holding period is forever"
- Behavioral contrast: "We are just the opposite of those who hurry to sell and book profits when companies perform well but who tenaciously hang on to businesses that disappoint"
- **Source**: [[1988ltr.md#p5]]
