## 📜 Source Summary

### Key Highlights

- **$81.4B GAAP net income**, driven by $24B operating earnings + $3.7B realized gains + **$53.7B unrealized gain** → highlights accounting distortion
- **Retained earnings framework**: Cites Edgar L. Smith’s *Common Stocks as Long Term Investments* and Keynes’ endorsement of compound growth from reinvested earnings
- **Insurance float grew to $129.4B**, up from $122.7B in 2018; underwriting profit remained at $400M pre-tax (16 of last 17 years profitable)
- **BHE wind self-sufficiency**: By 2021, BHE expected to generate ~25.2M MWh from wind, covering all ~24.6M MWh annual needs of Iowa customers
- **Board governance critique**: Exposes “independence” paradox — directors paid $250–300K/year are incentivized to avoid challenging CEOs on compensation or acquisitions

### Core Insights

#### GAAP Earnings Volatility & The $53.7B Unrealized Gain
> "That $53.7 billion gain requires comment. It resulted from a new GAAP rule, imposed in 2018, that requires a company holding equity securities to include in earnings the net change in the unrealized gains and losses of those securities." [[p.2]]

- **GAAP components**: $24B operating earnings + $3.7B realized gains + $53.7B unrealized gain = $81.4B net income
- **Volatility contrast**: 2018 ($4B) vs. 2019 ($81.4B) → $1,900% increase in GAAP earnings despite stable operating earnings
- **Investor guidance**: “Focus on operating earnings – which were little changed in 2019 – and to ignore both quarterly and annual gains or losses from investments”

#### Retained Earnings as Compound Growth Engine (Smith & Keynes)
> "Well-managed industrial companies do not, as a rule, distribute to the shareholders the whole of their earned profits... Thus there is an element of compound interest operating in favour of a sound industrial investment." [[p.3, quoting Keynes]]

- **Historical context**: Smith’s 1924 book overturned preconceptions about stocks as short-term gambles; established retained earnings as core driver of long-term wealth
- **Berkshire application**: $121B internal investment in PPE vs. $65B depreciation over past decade → massive capital recycling into productive assets
- **Look-through value**: Berkshire’s 10 largest holdings delivered $3.8B dividends but $8.3B in retained earnings in 2019 → intrinsic value creation occurs via retained earnings, not just dividends

#### Insurance Float Growth to $129.4B & Underwriting Profit Continuity
> "We may in time experience a decline in float. If so, the decline will be very gradual – at the outside no more than 3% in any year." [[p.6]]

| Year | Float |
|------|--------|
| 1970 | $39M |
| 2010 | $65.8B |
| 2018 | $122.7B |
| **2019** | **$129.4B** |

- **Float characteristics**: Cost-free or better-than-cost-free funding; accompanied by underwriting profit in most years
- **Underwriting continuity**: $400M pre-tax profit in 2019 vs. $2.0B in 2018; 16 of past 17 years profitable (exception: 2017’s $3.2B loss)
- **Competitive advantage**: Unrivaled financial strength enables superior float investment flexibility vs. peers facing low bond yields

#### BHE Wind Self-Sufficiency in Iowa
> "In 2021, we expect BHE’s operation to generate about 25.2 million megawatt-hours of electricity (MWh) in Iowa from wind turbines... That output will totally cover the annual needs of its Iowa customers, which run to about 24.6 million MWh." [[p.8]]

- **Rate leadership**: BHE residential rates rose <1%/year since 2000 vs. competitor’s 61% higher rates and recent 70% gap widening
- **Renewable scale**: First investor-owned utility globally projected to achieve wind self-sufficiency in a major state by 2021
- **Economic impact**: Enabled high-tech giants (3 of top 5 customers) to site plants in Iowa based on low-cost, renewable energy

#### Board Governance Critique & Director Independence Paradox
> "Director compensation has now soared to a level that inevitably makes pay a subconscious factor affecting the behavior of many non-wealthy members." [[p.12]]

- **Independence illusion**: Non-wealthy directors earning $250–300K/year for 6 days/year are financially dependent on CEO approval → “good” director = compliant one
- **Compensation complexity**: Consultants drive ever-more complex plans to justify fees, making proxy reading “mind-numbing”
- **CEO bias in acquisition review**: No CEO brings in an informed critic to oppose deals; board process favors deal-making over shareholder scrutiny

### 📌 Notable Quotes

> "Let not the left hand know what the right hand doeth." [[p.6, referencing Lubrizol fire & Berkshire insurance coverage]]

> "The important point to recognize is that if you are Bobby Fischer, you must play only chess for money." [[p.13]]

> "Don’t ask the barber whether you need a haircut." [[p.12]]
